## 2. AI Overviews / SGE Optimization

### Current Best Practice

AI Overviews (formerly SGE) now appear on a significant portion of search queries. Being cited as a source in these AI-generated answers is the new "position zero." Key strategies:

- **Answer-first format**: Place a direct, concise answer in the first 50-70 words of the page. Do not bury the answer beneath lengthy intros.
- **Citation-ready snippets**: Start every subheading (H2/H3) with a bold, concise summary. Google's Gemini model extracts "nuggets" from the first 40-50 words of each section.
- **Self-contained answer units**: AI prioritizes passages that fully answer queries in 134-167 word self-contained units.
- **Original data wins citations**: Pages with at least 3 unique data points (original market statistics, proprietary survey results, exclusive case studies) are 4x more likely to be cited in AI Overviews.
- **Schema markup for entity linking**: Use `SameAs` and `MainEntityOfPage` properties to connect content to Google's Knowledge Graph — this improves citation probability by up to 200%.
- **Topical authority**: A deep cluster of interconnected content on luxury real estate in Park City is far more likely to be cited than a single excellent article on an otherwise unrelated site.

### What Changed Recently

- AI Overviews have expanded dramatically in scope and frequency throughout early 2026.
- "Information Gain" scoring is now a key differentiator — pages introducing unique data, original case studies, or contrarian perspectives are preferentially cited.
- **Freshness threshold**: Content not updated in the last 6 months may be considered "stale" and excluded from AI Overviews. Continuous content updates and Digital PR activity signal active authority.
- Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) has emerged as a distinct discipline alongside traditional SEO.

### Actionable Recommendations for Laura Willis Real Estate

1. Restructure key content pages to lead with direct answers, followed by depth. Example: "What are Park City home prices in 2026?" should be answered concisely in the opening paragraph.
2. Create quarterly (or monthly) Park City luxury market reports with original data — median sale prices, days on market, inventory levels, price-per-square-foot trends by neighborhood.
3. Build deep topical clusters: a pillar page on "Park City Luxury Real Estate" linking to neighborhood guides, market reports, buying guides, and lifestyle content.
4. Update high-value content pages at least every 6 months with fresh data and insights.
5. Add `SameAs` schema linking Laura's profile to verified external profiles (LinkedIn, Realtor.com, Zillow, NAR).

## 3. E-E-A-T Signals

### Current Best Practice

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) remains the foundational quality framework for Google's ranking systems. **Trust is the most important component.** In 2026, with AI-generated content flooding the web, E-E-A-T signals are the primary differentiator.

**Experience:**
- Add "Our Experience" or "My Experience" sections to key content pages describing specific transactions, results achieved, and lessons learned.
- First-hand involvement is harder to fake and more valuable for differentiation than generic research.

**Expertise:**
- Provide specific examples with verifiable results rather than generic claims.
- Include relevant statistics, clear concept explanations, and demonstrations of professional understanding.
- Publish well-researched, detailed, insightful content.

**Authoritativeness:**
- Build authority through backlinks from trusted websites and mentions by credible platforms.
- Encourage the agent to contribute to third-party publications (local media, industry outlets), speak at events, and maintain an active professional presence.
- The more places an agent's name appears in connection with luxury real estate, the stronger the E-E-A-T signal.

**Trustworthiness:**
- Clear bylines on all content with links to detailed author bio pages.
- Transparent business information (license numbers, professional affiliations, contact details).
- Secure website (HTTPS), clear privacy policies, and honest representations.

### What Changed Recently

- E-E-A-T signals must now be **machine-readable**, not just human-readable. Structured author data, schema markup, and linked entity profiles matter more than ever.
- Sites demonstrating genuine experience saw 23% gains after the December 2025 Core Update.
- Google's March 2026 update further amplified the weight of verifiable expertise — 72% of top-ranking websites now feature detailed author biographies with verifiable credentials.
- Brand signals (branded searches, brand mentions across the web) now function as a proxy for authoritativeness.

### Actionable Recommendations for Laura Willis Real Estate

1. Create a comprehensive agent bio page with: years of experience, transaction history, awards, certifications, professional memberships (NAR, Utah Association of Realtors), and specific Park City expertise.
2. Add clear bylines to every blog post and content page, linking to the bio page.

4. Pursue guest contributions to Park Record, Salt Lake Tribune real estate sections, and national luxury real estate publications.

## 4. Schema Markup Best Practices

### Current Best Practice

Structured data (schema markup) is critical for both traditional search visibility and AI citation in 2026. **JSON-LD is the recommended format.**

**Essential Schema Types for a Luxury Real Estate Agent Website:**

| Schema Type | Where to Use | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `RealEstateAgent` | Agent bio page, About page | Identifies the business as a real estate agent, includes credentials, areas served, specializations |
| `LocalBusiness` | Site-wide (or homepage) | Establishes physical location, business hours, contact info — critical for Local 3-Pack |
| `RealEstateListing` | Individual property pages | Marks up price, beds, baths, square footage, address, availability |
| `FAQPage` | FAQ sections, neighborhood guides | Creates interactive Q&A boxes in SERPs, enlarges listing, improves CTR |
| `Review` / `AggregateRating` | Testimonials page, agent profile | Displays star ratings in search results |
| `Article` / `BlogPosting` | Blog posts, market reports | Identifies content for indexing, enables rich results |
| `VideoObject` | Property walkthrough videos | Enables video carousel placement in YouTube and Google search |
| `BreadcrumbList` | All pages | Improves site navigation display in search results |
| `Person` | Agent bio page | Links agent identity to Knowledge Graph via `SameAs` properties |

**Key Implementation Details:**
- Use `SameAs` properties to connect to verified external profiles (LinkedIn, Zillow, Realtor.com, social media).
- Use `MainEntityOfPage` to clearly define the primary entity of each page.
- Use `areaServed` to define service territories (Park City, Deer Valley, Summit County, etc.).
- Use `knowsAbout` to define expertise areas (luxury homes, ski properties, investment properties).
- Ensure all schema data exactly matches visible page content — discrepancies can result in penalties.

### What Changed Recently

- AI search engines (Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT) now rely heavily on structured data to answer queries like "Find me a luxury ski home in Park City under $5M." Without schema, properties and agents are excluded from AI-generated answers.
- `VideoObject` schema has become essential for property walkthroughs, improving rankings in video carousels.
- Entity-linking via schema (connecting your business to Google's Knowledge Graph) can improve AI Overview citation probability by up to 200%.
- Structured data in local search leads to 50% more conversions for "near me" searches.

### Actionable Recommendations for Laura Willis Real Estate

1. Implement `RealEstateAgent` schema on the bio/about page with full credentials, `areaServed` (Park City, Deer Valley, Summit County, Heber Valley, etc.), and `knowsAbout` properties.
2. Add `RealEstateListing` schema to every active property listing page.
3. Implement `FAQPage` schema on neighborhood guides and buyer/seller resource pages.
4. Add `Review`/`AggregateRating` schema to the testimonials page.
5. Implement `VideoObject` schema for all property tour videos.
6. Add `Person` schema for Laura with `SameAs` links to LinkedIn, Zillow profile, Realtor.com profile, and social accounts.
7. Validate all schema using Google's Rich Results Test after implementation.

### Current Best Practice

**Optimal Word Counts by Page Type:**

| Page Type | Recommended Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Neighborhood guide | 2,000-3,000 words | Deep, comprehensive coverage with local data |
| Market report | 1,500-2,500 words | Data-driven, updated monthly or quarterly |
| Blog post (informational) | 1,500-2,000 words | Focused, thorough coverage of a single topic |
| Property listing page | 400-800 words | Intent-driven; quality descriptions over length |
| Agent bio page | 800-1,500 words | Detailed but focused on credentials and experience |
| Service/area page | 1,200-2,000 words | Covers the topic completely without filler |

**Semantic Completeness:**
- Semantic completeness is the dominant content quality factor in 2026. Content scoring 8.5/10+ on semantic completeness is 4.2x more likely to be cited in AI Overviews.
- Cover at least 50% of semantically related subtopics/keywords for a given query. Once that threshold is met, text length becomes less critical.
- Structure content so AI can extract self-contained answer units of 134-167 words per section.

**Keyword Density:**
- Maintain 1-2% keyword density. Over-optimization is penalized.
- Focus on natural language and semantic variations rather than exact-match repetition.

**Freshness Signals:**
- Update key content pages at least every 6 months.
- Display "Last Updated" dates prominently.
- Add new data points, market statistics, and insights with each update.

### What Changed Recently

- 2026 rewards **specificity over length**. A 400-word product page can outrank a 2,000-word article if the intent match is better.
- Semantic structure (proper heading hierarchy, clear topic organization) has become as important as content length.
- AI-generated content without unique value, original data, or genuine expertise now ranks poorly following the March 2026 Core Update.
- Content clarity and technical predictability (consistent formatting, logical structure) are increasingly important signals.

## 7. Core Web Vitals & Page Speed

### Current Best Practice

Core Web Vitals are confirmed ranking factors, and their weight increased with the March 2026 Core Update. The three metrics and their thresholds:

| Metric | Good | Needs Improvement | Poor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LCP** (Largest Contentful Paint) | < 2.0s | 2.0s - 4.0s | > 4.0s |
| **INP** (Interaction to Next Paint) | < 150ms | 150ms - 500ms | > 500ms |
| **CLS** (Cumulative Layout Shift) | < 0.1 | 0.1 - 0.25 | > 0.25 |

These thresholds must be met for at least 75% of page visits (field data, not just lab data).

**Ranking Impact:**
- Pages ranking at position 1 are 10% more likely to pass CWV thresholds than pages at position 9.
- Sites with INP above 200ms saw average position drops of 0.8 places.
- Sites with INP above 500ms saw drops of 2-4 positions on competitive queries.
- When two pages are otherwise equal in content quality and relevance, the one with better CWV will rank higher.

### What Changed Recently

- **LCP threshold tightened**: Google adjusted the "good" LCP threshold from 2.5 seconds down to 2.0 seconds with the March 2026 update. Sites previously passing at 2.0-2.5s are now flagged as "needs improvement" and seeing ranking pressure.
- **INP replaced FID** as the responsiveness metric (completed in March 2024) and is now firmly established. INP below 150ms is required for ranking stability.
- Site speed was elevated as a more direct ranking factor in the March 2026 Core Update.

### Actionable Recommendations for Laura Willis Real Estate

1. Test all key pages using Google PageSpeed Insights and Chrome User Experience Report — prioritize fixing any pages not meeting the updated thresholds.
2. **Optimize LCP** (target under 2.0s):
   - Compress and properly size all property images (use WebP/AVIF formats).
   - Implement lazy loading for below-the-fold images.
   - Use a CDN for static assets.
   - Minimize render-blocking CSS and JavaScript.
   - Preload the largest contentful element (hero image).
3. **Optimize INP** (target under 150ms):
   - Minimize long JavaScript tasks.
   - Defer non-critical third-party scripts (chat widgets, analytics, social embeds).
   - Use web workers for heavy computations.
4. **Optimize CLS** (target under 0.1):
   - Set explicit width/height on all images and video embeds.
   - Reserve space for dynamic content (ads, map embeds).
   - Avoid inserting content above existing content after page load.
5. Real estate sites are particularly vulnerable to poor LCP due to large hero property images — prioritize image optimization.

## 9. Mobile-First Indexing Requirements

### Current Best Practice

Mobile-first indexing is Google's default — the mobile version of every website is the primary input for Google's search index. There is no separate "desktop index."

**Core Requirements:**

1. **Responsive Design**: Google strongly recommends responsive design as the optimal configuration. Use a single URL and HTML source for all devices, adapting layout based on screen size. This simplifies SEO because there is no need for separate mobile/desktop versions.

2. **Content Parity**: Mobile and desktop versions must have identical content, including:
   - All text content
   - All metadata (title tags, meta descriptions)
   - All structured data / schema markup
   - All images (with alt text)
   - All internal links
   If mobile has less content than desktop, Google may not index the missing content at all.

3. **Technical Performance**:
   - Meet Core Web Vitals thresholds on mobile devices.
   - HTTPS required.
   - No intrusive interstitials (pop-ups that block content on mobile are explicitly penalized).

4. **Mobile Usability**:
   - Touch-friendly navigation (tap targets at least 48x48px).
   - Readable font sizes (minimum 16px base).
   - No horizontal scrolling.
   - Properly sized viewport.
   - Fast-loading images optimized for mobile connections.

### What Changed Recently

- Mobile-first indexing completed its full rollout in 2024 and is now the only indexing mode.
- Google's enforcement of intrusive interstitial penalties remains active in 2026 — pop-ups, full-screen overlays, and aggressive lead capture forms on mobile can demote rankings.
- Mobile performance has become even more critical as Core Web Vitals thresholds tightened in March 2026.

### Actionable Recommendations for Laura Willis Real Estate

1. Test the site on Google's Mobile-Friendly Test and PageSpeed Insights specifically for mobile performance.
2. Ensure all content visible on desktop is equally accessible on mobile — no hidden tabs, collapsed sections that hide critical content, or desktop-only elements.
3. Audit and fix any intrusive interstitials or aggressive pop-ups on mobile. Lead capture forms should be inline or use small, easily dismissible banners — not full-screen overlays.
4. Ensure all structured data is present in the mobile HTML, not just the desktop version.
5. Optimize touch targets: property search filters, navigation menus, and CTA buttons should all be large enough for easy mobile tapping.
6. Test property listing pages specifically — image galleries, virtual tours, and map embeds must function smoothly on mobile.
