# Calgary Real Estate Market Report: Spring 2026

*By Neil Rowlandson, Calgary Castles Team | CIR Realty | Updated March 2026*

## Where the Calgary Real Estate Market Stands Right Now

Calgary's real estate market enters spring 2026 in a position of measured strength. After several years of post-pandemic adjustment, the city's housing market has settled into a pattern of steady, sustainable growth that benefits both buyers and sellers. The Calgary real estate market is neither the frenzied bidding-war environment of 2022 nor the uncertain terrain of early 2024 -- it is a balanced market trending slightly in favour of sellers in the detached home segment and slightly toward buyers in the condo market.

**Key numbers for spring 2026:**
- **Average home price (all types):** $641,000
- **Median home price:** $572,500
- **Year-over-year price change:** +2-3%
- **Active listings:** 5,500+ across all property types
- **Average days on market:** 28-35 days (detached), 40-50 days (condos)
- **Sales-to-new-listings ratio:** 55-60% (balanced market territory)

These figures reflect a Calgary housing market that has matured past the volatility of recent years. For context, Calgary home prices have risen approximately 35% since pre-pandemic levels, but the rate of increase has moderated to a sustainable 2-3% annually -- a pace that builds equity without pricing out new entrants.

## What Is Driving Calgary Home Prices in 2026

Several factors are sustaining the Calgary real estate market's current trajectory:

**Population growth.** Alberta continues to attract interprovincial migrants from British Columbia and Ontario, drawn by lower taxes, more affordable housing relative to Toronto and Vancouver, and a strong job market. Calgary's population growth rate remains above the national average, creating steady demand for housing across all types.

**Interest rates.** The Bank of Canada's rate cuts through late 2024 and 2025 brought the overnight rate down from its peak, improving affordability for buyers with variable-rate mortgages and making fixed-rate products more accessible. While rates have stabilized, they remain below the peaks that constrained the market in 2023-2024. Current five-year fixed mortgage rates in the 4.5-5.2% range are serviceable for most qualified buyers.

**Limited supply in key segments.** Detached homes in established SE Calgary communities -- Auburn Bay, Cranston, Mahogany, McKenzie Towne -- continue to see tight inventory relative to demand. The limited availability of buildable land in mature communities means resale inventory is the primary source, and well-priced detached homes in these areas sell within weeks.

**Economic diversification.** Calgary's economy has successfully diversified beyond oil and gas, with technology, logistics, film production, and renewable energy creating employment growth. This broader economic base supports housing demand that is less vulnerable to commodity price swings than in previous cycles.

## Calgary Real Estate Forecast: What to Expect Through 2026

The Calgary real estate forecast for the remainder of 2026 points to continued moderate growth:

- **Detached homes:** Expect 2-4% price appreciation through year-end, driven by population growth and constrained supply in established communities. The average detached home price in Calgary is projected to reach $680,000-$700,000 by December 2026.
- **Townhouses:** The strongest growth segment, with 3-5% appreciation expected as buyers priced out of detached homes turn to attached options. Calgary townhouses for sale represent the market's best value proposition for space-per-dollar.
- **Condos:** Moderate 1-2% growth, with the condo segment remaining the most accessible entry point for first-time buyers. Inventory in the condo market is higher relative to demand, giving buyers more negotiating power.
- **SE Calgary specifically:** Communities like Auburn Bay, Cranston, Mahogany, and McKenzie Towne are expected to outperform the city average, driven by lake amenities, school quality, and the continued maturation of the Seton commercial district.

CREB (the Calgary Real Estate Board) data supports this outlook, with sales volumes through Q1 2026 running 5-8% above the same period in 2025. The spring market is expected to be active but orderly, without the panic-driven behaviour that characterized earlier post-pandemic years.

## What This Means for Buyers

If you are looking to buy a home in Calgary in spring 2026, here is the practical guidance:

1. **Get pre-approved before you start looking.** In a market where well-priced detached homes sell in 2-4 weeks, having financing confirmed puts you in a strong position to act quickly.
2. **Consider townhouses and semi-detached.** These segments offer the best value-to-space ratio in the current Calgary housing market and are appreciating faster than detached homes.
3. **Look at established SE Calgary communities.** Auburn Bay, Cranston, Mahogany, and McKenzie Towne offer mature infrastructure, excellent schools, and strong appreciation histories at prices that remain accessible.
4. **Do not wait for a crash.** Calgary's fundamentals -- population growth, economic diversification, interprovincial migration -- support sustained demand. Waiting for a significant correction is a gamble against strong tailwinds.

## What This Means for Sellers

Sellers in the spring 2026 Calgary real estate market are well-positioned:

1. **Detached home sellers have leverage.** Low inventory in desirable communities means your listing will get attention. Price it right and expect competitive interest.
2. **Presentation matters more than ever.** With buyers being more selective post-frenzy, professionally staged and photographed homes sell faster and for more.
3. **Spring is your window.** Historically, April through June delivers the highest volume of buyer activity in the Calgary market. Listing now positions you for maximum exposure.
4. **Work with a local expert.** Understanding neighbourhood-specific dynamics -- not just city-wide averages -- is critical for pricing strategy. A home in Cranston Ridge commands different pricing than a comparable home in Copperfield.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Calgary real estate market going to crash in 2026?
No credible indicators point to a crash in the Calgary housing market. Population growth remains strong, employment is diversified, interest rates have stabilized, and inventory levels are balanced (not bloated). The 2-3% annual appreciation rate is sustainable, not speculative. While localized corrections are always possible in specific segments (e.g., luxury condos), the broad Calgary market is on solid footing.

### How does Calgary compare to Toronto and Vancouver for home prices?
Calgary remains significantly more affordable. The average home price in Calgary ($641,000) is roughly 40-50% below Toronto's average and 45-55% below Vancouver's. This affordability gap continues to drive interprovincial migration to Alberta, which in turn supports Calgary's housing demand. For buyers relocating from Ontario or BC, Calgary offers substantially more home for the money.

### What is the best time of year to buy or sell in Calgary?
The spring market (April-June) consistently delivers the highest transaction volumes and the broadest selection of inventory. Fall (September-October) provides a secondary peak. Winter sales tend to involve more motivated parties on both sides, which can benefit buyers looking for less competition. For sellers, listing in April or early May historically produces the best results in the Calgary real estate market.

# Best Neighborhoods in Calgary to Buy a Home (2026 Guide)

*By Neil Rowlandson, Calgary Castles Team | CIR Realty | Updated March 2026*

## How to Choose the Right Calgary Neighborhood

Choosing where to buy in Calgary is as important as choosing what to buy. With over 200 distinct communities spread across the city's four quadrants, the best neighborhoods in Calgary depend entirely on your priorities: family friendliness, commute time, price range, lifestyle amenities, and long-term appreciation potential.

After 20+ years selling Calgary real estate -- and a decade in banking before that -- I have watched neighborhoods rise, plateau, and transform. This guide reflects that experience, not just data from a spreadsheet. These are the Calgary neighborhoods I recommend to clients in 2026, organized by what matters most to different buyer types.

## Best Calgary Neighborhoods for Families

### Auburn Bay (SE Calgary)

Auburn Bay tops our family list because of one feature no other Calgary community can match at this price point: a 43-acre private lake. Swimming, fishing, kayaking, and winter skating are steps from your door. Add two schools within the community (Auburn Bay School K-4, Dr. George Stanley School 5-9), the adjacent South Health Campus, and direct Deerfoot Trail access, and Auburn Bay delivers the complete family package.

**Price range:** $220,000 (condos) to $1,100,000+ (lakefront detached)
**Best for:** Families who want active outdoor living without leaving the city

### Cranston (SE Calgary)

Cranston is the mature alternative to Auburn Bay -- a fully established community with 20 years of development, Bow River pathways, Fish Creek Provincial Park access, and a proven track record of stable property values. Schools are built and running (not under construction), landscaping is mature, and the community culture is well-established through the Cranston Residents' Association.

**Price range:** $200,000 (condos) to $950,000+ (river/ridge view detached)
**Best for:** Families who want established infrastructure and natural settings

### Mahogany (SE Calgary)

Mahogany's 63-acre lake is the largest in any Calgary community, and the Beach Club provides year-round programming. Three K-9 schools within the community, the Westman Village mixed-use development, and ongoing new construction give families maximum choice. Mahogany homes for sale span the widest price range of any single Calgary neighborhood.

**Price range:** $190,000 (Westman Village condos) to $1,200,000+ (estate homes)
**Best for:** Families wanting maximum amenities and housing variety

## Best Calgary Neighborhoods for First-Time Buyers

### McKenzie Towne (SE Calgary)

McKenzie Towne is the best value play in established SE Calgary. With condos starting under $200,000 and detached homes available below Calgary's median price, first-time buyers get proven community infrastructure -- High Street shops, established schools, extensive pathways -- at prices that newer communities with less amenity development cannot match.

**Price range:** $175,000 (condos) to $800,000+ (premium detached)
**Best for:** First-time buyers who want community character and affordability

### Copperfield (SE Calgary)

Part of the broader McKenzie Towne community, Copperfield offers newer construction at entry-level prices. Townhouses in the $270,000-$380,000 range provide space and modern finishes that first-time buyers in inner-city neighborhoods can only dream of at equivalent prices.

**Price range:** $200,000 (condos) to $600,000 (detached)
**Best for:** First-time buyers who want newer homes at accessible prices

### Bridgeland (NE Calgary / Inner City)

For first-time buyers who prioritize walkability and urban lifestyle over square footage, Bridgeland is one of the best neighborhoods in Calgary. Steps from downtown, the Bow River, and the Calgary Zoo, Bridgeland's condo and townhouse stock provides inner-city living at prices that remain accessible relative to Kensington or Mission.

**Price range:** $220,000 (condos) to $700,000 (infills)
**Best for:** Urban-lifestyle buyers who want walkability to downtown

## Best Calgary Neighborhoods for Investment

### Beltline (Central Calgary)

Calgary's densest residential neighborhood offers strong rental demand driven by downtown proximity, the 17th Avenue entertainment strip, and transit access. Condo prices in the $200,000-$400,000 range generate rental yields that outperform most suburban locations. The Beltline's long-term appreciation is tied to Calgary's continued economic diversification and downtown revitalization.

**Price range:** $180,000 (older condos) to $600,000+ (luxury units)
**Best for:** Investors seeking rental yield and long-term capital growth

### Seton (SE Calgary)

Seton is SE Calgary's commercial engine, and the surrounding residential development is attracting strong rental demand from healthcare workers (South Health Campus), retail employees, and young professionals. New construction condos and townhouses offer modern units that attract quality tenants, and the district's ongoing build-out supports appreciation.

**Price range:** $210,000 (condos) to $550,000 (townhouses)
**Best for:** Investors wanting new construction in a high-growth area

## Best Calgary Neighborhoods for Luxury

### Altadore (SW Calgary)

Altadore has emerged as Calgary's premier inner-city luxury neighborhood for families, with modern infills on 50-foot lots, Marda Loop's boutique shopping and dining, and proximity to Sandy Beach and River Park. Detached infills in the $900,000-$1,500,000 range offer the inner-city lifestyle that Mount Royal buyers seek at a more accessible price.

**Best for:** Affluent families wanting inner-city living with space

### Aspen Woods (SW Calgary)

For suburban luxury, Aspen Woods delivers large lots, executive-style homes, proximity to the Springbank Hill commercial area, and access to some of Calgary's top private schools. The community attracts established professionals and growing families who want space, privacy, and premium finishes.

**Best for:** Luxury buyers wanting suburban estate living

## How to Evaluate Any Calgary Neighborhood

Regardless of which Calgary neighborhood catches your eye, evaluate it against these criteria:

1. **Commute time.** Drive the route during rush hour, not on a Sunday afternoon.
2. **School proximity and quality.** Check the Calgary Board of Education and Calgary Catholic School District websites for boundary maps and program availability.
3. **Appreciation history.** Ask your agent for 5-year and 10-year price trends specific to the community, not just city averages.
4. **Amenity access.** Consider what matters to your daily life -- grocery, healthcare, recreation, transit -- and map the actual distances.
5. **Future development.** Check the City of Calgary's planning maps for upcoming infrastructure, transit, or commercial projects that could impact property values.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the safest neighborhood in Calgary?
Calgary is broadly safe by North American standards. Communities in the SE quadrant -- Auburn Bay, Cranston, Mahogany, McKenzie Towne -- consistently report low crime rates due to their residential character and community association oversight. For inner-city safety, Bridgeland, Altadore, and Mount Pleasant are strong options. The Calgary Police Service publishes a community crime map that allows you to compare neighbourhoods directly.

### Are there neighborhoods in Calgary where prices are still affordable?
Yes. McKenzie Towne, Copperfield, Saddle Ridge, and Martindale offer detached homes below Calgary's $572,500 median. For condo buyers, the Beltline, Forest Lawn, and Westman Village (Mahogany) provide options under $250,000. First-time buyers working with a Calgary real estate agent can identify opportunities that match their budget across all quadrants.

### How do Calgary neighborhoods compare to Edmonton for value?
Calgary home prices are approximately 15-20% higher than Edmonton's, but Calgary's economy is more diversified, population growth is stronger, and long-term appreciation has historically outpaced Edmonton's. For buyers choosing between the two cities, Calgary's broader employment base and lifestyle amenities typically justify the premium. Within Calgary, the best neighborhoods offer value that competes favourably with comparable Edmonton communities.

## Why I Approach Real Estate Like a Banker

Before I became a Calgary real estate agent, I spent 10 years in banking. That experience fundamentally shaped how I evaluate Calgary investment property: I look at the numbers first, the emotion second. When clients ask me whether Calgary real estate is a good investment, I do not give them a sales pitch -- I give them the same analysis I would have run as a lender evaluating a loan application.

The short answer for 2026: yes, Calgary real estate remains a strong investment, but the opportunity depends entirely on what you buy, where you buy it, and how you structure the deal. This post breaks down the data.

## Calgary's Investment Fundamentals in 2026

Every real estate investment rests on a foundation of local economic and demographic fundamentals. Here is where Calgary stands:

**Population growth:** Alberta leads Canada in interprovincial migration, and Calgary is the primary destination. The city's population surpassed 1.4 million in 2025 and continues to grow at 2-3% annually. More people means more housing demand -- the most fundamental driver of property values and rental demand.

**Economic diversification:** Calgary's economy has meaningfully diversified beyond oil and gas. Technology companies, renewable energy firms, film production studios, and logistics operations have established significant presence. This diversification reduces the boom-bust risk that historically made Calgary investment property volatile.

**Affordability advantage:** Calgary's average home price of $641,000 is 40-50% below Toronto and 45-55% below Vancouver. This gap drives migration and also means Calgary real estate has more room to appreciate before hitting affordability ceilings that constrain growth in more expensive markets.

**Interest rate environment:** Bank of Canada rate stabilization in the 3.5-4.0% range (overnight rate) has improved financing conditions for investment properties. While rates are above the pandemic lows, they are manageable for well-structured deals, and the rate direction appears stable for 2026.

Based on my analysis of Calgary's market data and 20 years of transaction history, these areas offer the strongest return on investment:

1. **Beltline / Downtown condos:** Highest rental demand, lowest vacancy rates, strong cap rates (5-6%). Best for cash flow investors.

## Canadian Tax Considerations for Investment Property

Calgary investment property comes with specific Canadian tax implications that differ from other countries:

- **Rental income** is taxable at your marginal rate, but you can deduct mortgage interest, property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and depreciation (CCA)
- **Capital gains** on sale are taxed at 50% inclusion rate (i.e., 50% of your profit is added to your income)
- **CMHC insurance** is required for down payments below 20%; investment properties with 1-4 units require a minimum 20% down payment
- **The Smith Manoeuvre** (converting non-deductible mortgage interest into deductible investment loan interest) is a strategy used by some Alberta investors -- consult a tax professional for suitability
- **GST/HST** applies to new construction purchases but not resale properties

Always work with a tax accountant who understands Alberta's real estate investment rules before purchasing a Calgary rental property.

## Risks to Consider

No investment analysis is complete without addressing risk:

- **Interest rate risk:** While rates appear stable, unexpected increases would compress cash flow. Stress-test your numbers at rates 1-2% above current levels.
- **Vacancy risk:** Calgary's vacancy rate is currently low (3-4%), but it can spike during economic downturns. Budget for 1-2 months of vacancy annually.
- **Maintenance costs:** Budget 1-1.5% of property value annually for maintenance and unexpected repairs.
- **Liquidity risk:** Real estate is not liquid. If you need to sell quickly, you may not achieve optimal pricing.
- **Concentration risk:** Putting all your investment capital into a single Calgary property concentrates your risk. Diversification across asset classes remains sound financial practice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much do you need to invest in Calgary real estate?
For an investment property in Calgary, you need a minimum 20% down payment (CMHC rules for non-owner-occupied properties). For a $300,000 condo, that is $60,000 down plus approximately $5,000-$8,000 in closing costs (land transfer fees, legal fees, inspections). You should also have 3-6 months of carrying costs in reserve for vacancies. Total capital required for a Calgary investment property entry point: approximately $70,000-$80,000.

### Is it better to invest in Calgary or Edmonton?
Calgary offers stronger fundamentals for real estate investment: higher population growth, better economic diversification, stronger long-term appreciation, and higher rental demand in desirable communities. # First-Time Home Buyer in Calgary: Programs, Down Payments & What to Expect

*By Neil Rowlandson, Calgary Castles Team | CIR Realty | Updated March 2026*

## Why Calgary Is One of the Best Cities in Canada for First-Time Buyers

If you are a first-time home buyer in Canada, Calgary should be at the top of your list. Here is why: while Toronto and Vancouver first-time buyers face average prices north of $900,000 and $1.1 million respectively, Calgary's median home price of $572,500 -- and entry-level condos starting under $200,000 -- means homeownership is genuinely achievable here without family wealth or a six-figure household income.

Having spent 10 years in banking before becoming a Calgary real estate agent, I have seen firsthand how the numbers work for Calgary first time home buyers. This guide walks you through every step of the process, using Canadian programs and Alberta-specific rules -- not US-centric advice about PMI, escrow, or "closing in 30 states" that has no relevance to buying a home in Calgary.

## How Much Do You Need for a Down Payment in Calgary?

The down payment is the single biggest hurdle for most Calgary first time home buyers. Here is how it works in Canada:

**Minimum down payment rules (CMHC):**

| Purchase Price | Minimum Down Payment | Example |
|---------------|---------------------|---------|
| Under $500,000 | 5% | $300,000 home = $15,000 down |
| $500,000 - $999,999 | 5% on first $500K + 10% on remainder | $600,000 home = $35,000 down |
| $1,000,000+ | 20% minimum | $1,000,000 home = $200,000 down |

For most Calgary first time home buyers, the target is a property in the $300,000-$500,000 range, meaning a minimum down payment of $15,000-$25,000. This is the realistic entry point for condos and townhouses in communities like McKenzie Towne, Copperfield, and Westman Village in Mahogany.

**Important:** With less than 20% down, you will need CMHC mortgage default insurance (sometimes called "mortgage insurance"). This is not the same as US-style PMI. CMHC insurance protects the lender and is added to your mortgage balance. The premium ranges from 2.8% to 4.0% of the mortgage amount depending on your down payment size. On a $285,000 mortgage (95% of a $300,000 home), CMHC insurance adds approximately $8,550-$11,400 to your total mortgage.

## Canadian Programs for First-Time Home Buyers

Canada offers several programs specifically designed to help first-time buyers. These are federal programs available to Calgary first time home buyers:

### Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)
The Home Buyers' Plan allows you to withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSP tax-free to put toward your first home. If purchasing with a partner, each of you can withdraw $60,000, for a combined $120,000. You must repay the withdrawal over 15 years (starting the second year after withdrawal) or the unpaid amount is added to your taxable income.

**Who this works for:** Calgary first time home buyers who have been contributing to RRSPs and have accumulated savings. Even a $20,000-$30,000 RRSP withdrawal can make the difference between renting and owning.

### First Home Savings Account (FHSA)
The FHSA, introduced in 2023, allows you to contribute up to $8,000 per year (lifetime maximum $40,000) into a tax-advantaged account specifically for buying your first home. Contributions are tax-deductible (like an RRSP) and withdrawals for a home purchase are tax-free (like a TFSA). This is the most tax-efficient savings vehicle for a first home in Calgary.

**Who this works for:** Prospective first-time buyers who are 1-5 years away from purchasing. Even two years of maximum contributions ($16,000) creates meaningful down payment capital with tax benefits.

### First-Time Home Buyer Tax Credit (HBTC)
A non-refundable federal tax credit of $10,000, resulting in a tax reduction of up to $1,500. This is claimed on your tax return for the year you purchase.

### GST/HST New Housing Rebate
If you buy a new-construction home in Calgary, you may qualify for a partial rebate of the GST paid. The federal rebate provides up to $6,300 on homes priced under $350,000, with a reduced rebate for homes between $350,000 and $450,000. This is significant for first-time buyers purchasing new condos or townhouses in developing communities.

### Alberta-Specific: No Land Transfer Tax
Alberta is one of the few provinces in Canada that does not charge a land transfer tax. In Ontario, a first-time buyer purchasing a $500,000 home pays approximately $6,475 in land transfer tax (even with the first-time buyer rebate). In Calgary, that cost simply does not exist. This is a meaningful saving that makes Alberta one of the most affordable provinces for first-time home buyers.

## The Calgary Home Buying Process Step by Step

Here is what to expect when you buy your first home in Calgary:

### Step 1: Get Pre-Approved for a Mortgage
Before you look at a single property, talk to a mortgage broker or your bank. A pre-approval confirms how much you can borrow, locks in an interest rate for 90-120 days, and signals to sellers that you are a serious buyer. In Calgary's spring 2026 market, well-priced homes sell in 2-4 weeks -- you do not have time to sort financing after finding the right property.

**The mortgage stress test:** All Canadian buyers must qualify at the higher of their contract rate plus 2% or the Bank of Canada's qualifying rate (currently 5.25%). This means if your actual mortgage rate is 4.8%, you must prove you can afford payments at 6.8%. The stress test reduces your maximum purchase price but protects you from rate increases.

### Step 2: Work with a Calgary Real Estate Agent
In Alberta, buyer's agents are typically compensated through the listing brokerage's commission split, meaning you do not pay your agent directly. A knowledgeable Calgary real estate agent will save you time by filtering listings to your criteria, provide comparable sales data for informed offers, negotiate on your behalf, and guide you through conditions, inspections, and closing.

### Step 3: Search and View Properties
With pre-approval in hand and an agent working for you, search begins in earnest. For Calgary first time home buyers on a budget, focus on these high-value communities:
- **McKenzie Towne / Copperfield** -- townhouses from $270,000, detached from $450,000
- **Mahogany (Westman Village)** -- condos from $190,000
- **Auburn Bay** -- condos from $220,000, townhouses from $320,000
- **Saddle Ridge / Martindale** -- affordable detached homes from $400,000

### Step 4: Make an Offer
In Alberta, the purchase contract is the AREA (Alberta Real Estate Association) standard form. Your offer will typically include conditions: financing (5-7 business days), home inspection (7-10 days), and sometimes a condo document review (10 days for condos). Your agent will help you structure a competitive offer based on comparable sales and current market conditions.

### Step 5: Conditions, Inspection, and Financing
Once your offer is accepted, the condition period begins. You will:
- Finalize your mortgage with your lender
- Schedule and attend a home inspection ($400-$600 for a standard inspection in Calgary)
- Review any condo documents (if applicable)
- Remove conditions once you are satisfied, making the contract firm

### Step 6: Close and Take Possession
Closing in Alberta is handled by a real estate lawyer (not a title company as in some US states). Your lawyer will:
- Register the property title transfer at Alberta Land Titles
- Collect and distribute funds
- Ensure the property is free of liens
- Provide you with keys on possession day

**Closing costs for Calgary first time home buyers (approximate):**

| Cost | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Legal fees | $1,200 - $1,800 |
| Home inspection | $400 - $600 |
| Title insurance | $250 - $400 |
| Property tax adjustment | Varies (prorated) |
| Moving costs | $800 - $2,000 |
| Utility connections | $100 - $200 |
| **Total closing costs** | **$2,750 - $5,000** |

Note: Alberta has no land transfer tax, which saves Calgary first time home buyers thousands of dollars compared to buyers in Ontario or British Columbia.

## Common Mistakes First-Time Buyers Make in Calgary

After helping hundreds of Calgary first time home buyers, these are the mistakes I see most often:

1. **Skipping the pre-approval.** Without pre-approval, you risk losing a property to a buyer who has theirs in hand.
2. **Ignoring closing costs.** Budget $3,000-$5,000 beyond your down payment for closing costs, or you may come up short.
3. **Buying the maximum you qualify for.** The stress test qualifies you at a higher rate, but your actual comfort level may be lower. Leave room in your budget for lifestyle, savings, and unexpected expenses.
4. **Not hiring a home inspector.** For $400-$600, an inspection can reveal $10,000-$50,000 in hidden issues. Never skip this step, even on new construction.
5. **Fixating on the wrong things.** Paint colour and landscaping are cheap to change. Foundation issues, plumbing, and HVAC are not. Focus on the bones of the home.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much income do I need to buy a home in Calgary?
For a $400,000 home with 5% down ($20,000), you will need a household income of approximately $85,000-$95,000 to pass the mortgage stress test with typical debt levels. For a $300,000 condo with 5% down, approximately $65,000-$75,000 household income is required. These are estimates -- your actual qualifying amount depends on existing debts, credit score, and the specific lender's criteria.

### Can I use my FHSA and RRSP Home Buyers' Plan together?
Yes. You can withdraw from both your FHSA and your RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan for the same home purchase. Combined, a Calgary first time home buyer could access up to $40,000 (FHSA) + $60,000 (HBP) = $100,000 in tax-advantaged savings toward a down payment. For a couple, the combined maximum is $200,000. This makes Canadian first-time buyer programs among the most generous in the world.

### What credit score do I need to buy a home in Calgary?
Most Canadian lenders require a minimum credit score of 600 for a mortgage with CMHC insurance, and 680+ for the best rates. If your score is below 600, focus on building it before applying: pay bills on time, reduce credit card balances, and avoid opening new credit accounts. A mortgage broker can advise on lender options for your specific credit profile.

### Should I buy a condo or a house as my first home in Calgary?
For most Calgary first time home buyers, the answer depends on budget and lifestyle. Condos ($200,000-$330,000) require smaller down payments and lower carrying costs, making them easier to qualify for. Detached homes ($450,000+) build equity faster through appreciation but require larger down payments and higher monthly payments. Townhouses ($280,000-$430,000) offer a middle path. Start with what you can comfortably afford, build equity, and upgrade in 3-5 years when your income and savings have grown.
