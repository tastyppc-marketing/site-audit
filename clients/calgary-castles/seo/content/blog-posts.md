# Calgary Real Estate Market Report: Spring 2026

The Calgary real estate market enters spring 2026 in a position of steady strength. After years of volatility — from the oil downturn to pandemic-era surges — the market has settled into a pattern of measured growth that benefits both buyers and sellers. Here is what the data tells us heading into the busiest selling season of the year.

## Market Snapshot: Where We Stand

The headline numbers paint a clear picture of a healthy, balanced market:

- **Average home price:** $641,000
- **Median home price:** $572,500
- **Active listings:** 5,500+
- **Year-over-year price change:** +2%
- **Average days on market:** 30–45 days (varies by segment)

The gap between average and median prices ($641K vs. $572.5K) tells an important story. Calgary's luxury segment — homes above $1 million — continues to pull the average higher, while the median reflects what most families actually pay. For a typical buyer entering the market, $572,500 is a more realistic benchmark than the headline average.

## Price Trends by Property Type

Not all segments are performing equally. Here is how the major property types break down heading into spring:

### Detached Homes

Detached single-family homes remain the backbone of Calgary's market, accounting for roughly 55% of all transactions. Prices have firmed in the $550,000–$750,000 range for standard suburban homes, with inner-city properties in established communities commanding $800,000 to $1.2 million.

The strongest demand is in SE Calgary communities like Cranston, Auburn Bay, and Mahogany, where family-oriented homes with modern amenities trade quickly. SW communities including Signal Hill, Lakeview, and Altadore continue to hold premiums for their location advantages.

### Condos and Apartments

Calgary's condo market has recovered from its pandemic-era softness. With affordability pressures pushing first-time buyers toward lower price points, condos in the $250,000–$400,000 range are seeing renewed interest. Downtown condos have particularly benefited from return-to-office trends, with units near the CTrain commanding premium rents and purchase prices.

### Townhouses

Townhouses continue to be the "sweet spot" for Calgary buyers who want more space than a condo but cannot stretch to a detached home. The $350,000–$500,000 townhouse segment is competitive, with well-maintained properties in desirable areas often receiving multiple offers.

## What Is Driving Calgary's Market in 2026

Several factors are supporting Calgary's steady performance:

### Population Growth

Alberta continues to attract interprovincial migration. Families from Ontario and British Columbia, drawn by Alberta's lower cost of living, no provincial sales tax, and competitive home prices, represent a significant buyer cohort. Calgary's population growth rate remains among the highest of major Canadian cities.

### Interest Rate Environment

The Bank of Canada's rate decisions remain the most closely watched variable for Calgary real estate. After a period of elevated rates, the gradual stabilization has brought mortgage qualification back within reach for many buyers. My 10 years in banking before entering real estate give me a deep appreciation for how rate movements affect purchasing power — a 0.5% rate change on a $500,000 mortgage shifts monthly payments by approximately $150.

### Employment and Economic Diversification

Calgary's economy has diversified significantly beyond energy. The tech sector, logistics, agriculture technology, and professional services have all expanded their Calgary footprints. This diversification reduces the boom-bust cycle risk that historically impacted Calgary real estate and provides a broader employment base supporting home purchases.

### Inventory Levels

At 5,500+ active listings, Calgary's inventory provides genuine selection for buyers without creating the oversupply conditions that depress prices. This is a healthy range — enough choice to prevent bidding wars on every listing, but not so much inventory that sellers lose leverage.

# Is Calgary Real Estate a Good Investment? What the Data Says

Before I became a REALTOR, I spent 10 years in banking. That background taught me to evaluate investments with data, not emotion. So when clients ask whether Calgary real estate is a good investment in 2026, I answer the way a banker would — with numbers, context, and an honest assessment of risks.

The short answer: yes, but your returns depend entirely on your strategy, timeline, and property selection. Here is the detailed analysis.

## The Investment Case for Calgary

### Price-to-Income Ratio

Calgary's price-to-income ratio remains one of the healthiest among major Canadian cities. With a median household income above $100,000 and a median home price of $572,500, Calgary homes are roughly 5.5x median household income. Compare this to Toronto (10x+) or Vancouver (12x+), and Calgary's relative affordability becomes obvious.

This ratio matters for investors because it indicates sustainable price levels. Markets with extreme price-to-income ratios are vulnerable to corrections; Calgary's moderate ratio suggests room for appreciation without the downside risk of overheated markets.

### Population and Demand Fundamentals

Real estate values are ultimately driven by population growth and housing supply. Calgary's fundamentals are strong:

- **Population growth rate** among the highest of major Canadian cities, driven by interprovincial migration from Ontario and BC
- **International immigration** contributing to rental demand, particularly in the condo and apartment segments
- **Young demographic profile** with a high proportion of working-age residents forming new households
- **Alberta's tax advantage** (no provincial sales tax, competitive income tax rates) continues to attract workers and families from higher-tax provinces

These demographic trends support both property appreciation and rental demand — the two pillars of real estate investment returns.

### Rental Market Performance

Calgary's rental market has tightened significantly. Vacancy rates have decreased as population growth outpaces new rental construction. Key rental metrics for investors:

- **Average 2-bedroom apartment rent:** $1,600–$1,900/month depending on location and condition
- **Average basement suite rent:** $1,200–$1,500/month
- **Single-family rental homes:** $2,200–$3,000/month in desirable communities
- **Vacancy rates:** Below 3% in most segments — a landlord-friendly market

For a typical investment property purchased at $450,000 with 20% down and rented at $2,400/month, the gross rental yield approaches 6.4%. After mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance, many Calgary investment properties achieve positive cash flow — something that is nearly impossible in Toronto or Vancouver at current prices.

### The No-PST Advantage

Alberta's absence of a provincial sales tax provides a measurable investment advantage. Every dollar of renovation, maintenance, and property improvement costs less in Alberta than in provinces with HST/PST. Over a 10-year investment horizon, this tax advantage compounds to tens of thousands of dollars in savings on a typical rental property.

Not all Calgary neighborhoods offer equal investment returns. Based on current data, here are the strongest segments:

### Best for Rental Yield: SE Calgary Suburbs

Communities like Legacy, Walden, and New Brighton offer newer construction at prices below the city average. Established communities like Lakeview, Altadore, and Marda Loop have limited land supply and growing demand. Appreciation in these areas has historically outpaced suburban communities. Downtown and inner-city condos near CTrain stations offer the best pure cash flow numbers. Homes built in the 1980s–1990s in communities like Signal Hill, Woodbine, and Woodlands offer renovation-driven investment opportunities. Purchase a dated home at $550,000, invest $80,000–$100,000 in targeted renovations, and the post-renovation value often exceeds $750,000. This forced appreciation strategy works well in Calgary's established SW communities.

## Risks to Consider

Every honest investment analysis includes risks. Here are the ones Calgary real estate investors should understand:

### Interest Rate Sensitivity

Calgary's investment returns are sensitive to mortgage rates. Higher rates increase borrowing costs, reduce cash flow, and can suppress appreciation. My banking background tells me to stress-test every investment property at current rates plus 2% before committing. If the numbers still work at higher rates, the investment is sound.

### Energy Sector Correlation

While Calgary's economy has diversified significantly, energy sector health still influences the local real estate market. A prolonged oil price decline would reduce employment and suppress housing demand. The correlation is weaker than it was a decade ago, but it still exists.

### Regulatory Risk

Alberta's landlord-tenant regulations are currently balanced, but regulatory changes — rent controls, eviction restrictions, or tax policy shifts — could affect investment returns. Monitor provincial policy developments if you are building a rental portfolio.

### Liquidity

Real estate is inherently illiquid. Unlike stocks or bonds, selling a property takes weeks to months and involves significant transaction costs (commissions, legal fees, land transfer taxes). Only invest capital you will not need for at least 5–7 years.

## A Banker's Framework for Real Estate Investment

From my years in banking, here is how I evaluate investment properties for clients:

1. **Cash flow first.** The property must cover all costs (mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy allowance) with room left over. Negative cash flow investments rely entirely on appreciation — that is speculation, not investing.
2. **Location quality.** Invest in communities with strong school ratings, low crime, and growing amenities. These factors drive both rental demand and long-term appreciation.
3. **Conservative leverage.** Just because you qualify for a large mortgage does not mean you should take one. I recommend 20–25% down payment on investment properties to maintain comfortable debt service ratios and avoid CMHC insurance premiums.
4. **Exit strategy defined.** Before buying, know how and when you would sell. What triggers a sale? What is your minimum hold period? Having this clarity prevents emotional decision-making during market dips.
5. **Diversification.** Real estate should be one component of your investment portfolio, not all of it. The best returns come from balancing property investments with financial instruments that provide liquidity and different risk profiles.

# Calgary First-Time Home Buyer Guide 2026: Everything You Need to Know

Buying your first home is the largest financial decision most people make, and the process can feel overwhelming. I spent 10 years in banking before becoming a REALTOR, which means I have sat on both sides of the mortgage desk. That experience gives me a perspective most agents cannot offer — I understand the financing as deeply as the real estate.

This guide covers everything a first-time buyer needs to know about purchasing a home in Calgary in 2026. No fluff, just the information that actually matters.

## Step 1: Know Your Numbers

Before you look at a single listing, you need to understand your financial position. Here is what lenders evaluate:

### Down Payment Requirements (Canada)

| Home Price | Minimum Down Payment | Example |
|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| Up to $500,000 | 5% | $500K home = $25,000 down |
| $500,001–$1,499,999 | 5% on first $500K + 10% on remainder | $600K home = $25,000 + $10,000 = $35,000 |
| $1,500,000+ | 20% | Not applicable for most first-time buyers |

For Calgary's median home price of $572,500, the minimum down payment is approximately $32,250 (5% of $500,000 + 10% of $72,500).

**The 20% threshold:** Putting down less than 20% requires CMHC mortgage insurance, which adds 2.8–4.0% of the mortgage amount to your costs. On a $540,000 mortgage, that is $15,120–$21,600 added to your loan. If you can reach 20% down, the savings are substantial.

### What You Can Actually Afford

The maximum mortgage lenders will approve is based on your Gross Debt Service (GDS) ratio (housing costs under 39% of gross income) and Total Debt Service (TDS) ratio (all debt payments under 44% of gross income).

A rough guideline for Calgary in 2026:

| Household Income | Approximate Buying Power | Monthly Payment (est.) |
|-----------------|------------------------|----------------------|
| $80,000 | $375,000–$425,000 | $2,100–$2,400 |
| $100,000 | $475,000–$525,000 | $2,650–$2,950 |
| $120,000 | $575,000–$625,000 | $3,200–$3,500 |
| $150,000 | $725,000–$775,000 | $4,000–$4,300 |

These are estimates — actual qualification depends on debts, credit score, employment stability, and current rates. Use the [mortgage calculator](/buyers/mortgage-calculator/) for a more precise estimate, or [get pre-approved](/buyers/mortgage-pre-approval/) for a definitive number.

**Important:** What the bank approves and what you should spend are different numbers. I always advise clients to stay 10–15% below their maximum approval. You need room for property taxes, maintenance, utilities, and life.

## Step 2: Get Pre-Approved (Not Pre-Qualified)

There is a critical difference:

- **Pre-qualification** is an estimate based on self-reported information. It carries no commitment from the lender and means little in a competitive offer situation.
- **Pre-approval** involves a full credit check, income verification, and underwriting review. It produces a written commitment letter with a specific approved amount and locked interest rate (typically for 90–120 days).

A pre-approval letter tells sellers you are a serious, qualified buyer. In a market with 5,500+ active listings and motivated sellers, a pre-approved buyer with a clean offer will beat a pre-qualified buyer almost every time.

From my banking experience, I know the pre-approval process inside and out. The most common issues that delay or derail approval:

- **Recent job changes** — lenders want 2+ years of stable employment
- **Outstanding consumer debt** — car loans, lines of credit, and student loans all reduce buying power
- **Credit score issues** — a score below 680 limits your options; below 600 makes conventional mortgages difficult
- **Irregular income** — self-employed and commission-based buyers need 2 years of tax returns and may qualify for less

Address these issues before applying. A few months of preparation can save you tens of thousands over the life of your mortgage.

## Step 3: Government Programs for First-Time Buyers

Several programs exist specifically for first-time buyers in 2026. Make sure you take advantage of every one:

### First Home Savings Account (FHSA)

The FHSA allows first-time buyers to save up to $8,000 per year (lifetime max $40,000) in a tax-advantaged account. Contributions are tax-deductible (like an RRSP) and withdrawals for a home purchase are tax-free (like a TFSA). If you have not opened one, do it today.

### Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)

You can withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSPs tax-free for a home purchase (increased from $35,000 in recent years). The withdrawal must be repaid over 15 years. Combined with the FHSA, a couple can access up to $200,000 in tax-advantaged savings.

### First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit

A non-refundable federal tax credit of $10,000 (worth $1,500 in tax savings at the 15% rate). Claimed on your tax return for the year you purchase.

### GST/HST New Housing Rebate

If you buy a new-construction home, you may be eligible for a partial rebate of the GST paid. In Alberta (no PST/HST), this applies to the 5% federal GST.

### Land Transfer Tax

Alberta does not charge a provincial land transfer tax — a significant savings compared to Ontario or BC. You will pay a property registration fee (approximately $350 for the land title transfer), but this is minimal compared to the thousands charged in other provinces.

1. **Submit your offer** with your pre-approval letter, deposit amount, conditions (financing, inspection), and proposed closing date
2. **Negotiation** — the seller may counter. Your REALTOR handles this to protect your interests
3. **Conditions period** (typically 7–14 days) — arrange final financing approval and complete a home inspection
4. **Remove conditions** — once financing is confirmed and inspection is satisfactory
5. **Closing day** — your lawyer handles the transfer. You bring a bank draft for your down payment and closing costs, and you get the keys

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

After 20+ years in real estate, I see these mistakes repeatedly:

1. **Skipping pre-approval.** Leads to disappointment when dream homes are above your actual budget.
2. **Maxing out the budget.** Just because you qualify for $600K does not mean you should spend $600K. Leave room for life.
3. **Ignoring resale value.** Your first home probably will not be your last. Buy in a location and configuration that will appeal to future buyers.
4. **Waiving the inspection.** Never. A $500 inspection can save you from $50,000 in hidden problems.
5. **Emotional decision-making.** Falling in love with granite countertops while ignoring a cracked foundation is expensive. Focus on structure, location, and systems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much do I need for a down payment in Calgary?**
Minimum 5% for homes under $500,000, plus 10% on the portion above $500,000. For Calgary's median of $572,500, that is approximately $32,250. Putting down 20% ($114,500) avoids CMHC mortgage insurance, saving $15,000–$22,000.

**What programs are available for first-time buyers in Calgary?**
Key programs include the First Home Savings Account (FHSA), RRSP Home Buyers' Plan ($60,000 withdrawal), First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit ($1,500), and the GST New Housing Rebate on new construction. Alberta charges no provincial land transfer tax, providing additional savings.

**What is the cheapest area to buy a house in Calgary?**
The most affordable family-friendly areas include Legacy, Walden, and New Brighton in SE Calgary (homes from $375K), Cochrane (homes from $300K), and Chestermere (homes from $280K). Condos and townhouses in Bridlewood and Evergreen start under $300K.

**How much are closing costs for a first-time buyer in Calgary?**
Budget 2–4% of the purchase price for closing costs, which includes legal fees, home inspection, title insurance, property registration, and moving expenses. For a $572,500 home, expect $8,000–$15,000 in closing costs beyond your down payment.
