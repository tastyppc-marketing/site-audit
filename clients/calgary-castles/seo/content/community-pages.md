# Lakeview Homes for Sale — Your Insider Guide to One of Calgary's Most Coveted Neighborhoods

Lakeview is one of Calgary's best-kept secrets — a quiet, established neighborhood tucked between Glenmore Reservoir and North Glenmore Park in the city's southwest quadrant. With tree-lined streets, stunning reservoir views, and easy access to downtown, Lakeview has attracted homeowners for decades who value quality of life over flashy amenities.

I know this firsthand because Lakeview is where I grew up. Walking to school along the reservoir path, skating at the community rink in winter, and watching Calgary's skyline change over the years from the hilltop lookout — these experiences shaped my understanding of what makes a great Calgary neighborhood. That personal connection is something no market report can capture, and it's an advantage I bring to every client looking at Lakeview real estate.

## Neighborhood Overview

Lakeview sits in Calgary's SW quadrant, bordered by Glenmore Trail to the north, Crowchild Trail to the west, and the Glenmore Reservoir along its eastern edge. Established in the 1960s, the community has matured into one of Calgary's most desirable inner-city neighborhoods.

The housing stock is a mix of original mid-century bungalows, renovated split-levels, and an increasing number of infill homes. Original homes on standard lots typically range from $650,000 to $850,000, while fully renovated or new infill properties regularly exceed $1 million. The average home price in Lakeview sits above Calgary's citywide average of $641,000, reflecting the neighborhood's premium inner-city location.

Lakeview's population is approximately 4,000 residents, giving it a small-community feel unusual for a neighborhood just 10 minutes from downtown Calgary. The community is well-established with long-term homeowners, though turnover has increased as younger families discover the area's appeal.

## Property Types

**Single-family homes** dominate Lakeview's housing stock. You will find:

- **Original bungalows and split-levels** (1960s–1970s) on generous lots, many with developed basements and attached garages. These represent the best value entry point into the community.
- **Renovated character homes** where owners have updated interiors while preserving the streetscape. Kitchen and bathroom renovations are common, and many homes have added secondary suites in the basement.
- **Luxury infill homes** on subdivided lots, typically 2,000–3,000 sq ft with modern finishes, open floor plans, and energy-efficient construction. These command $1.2M–$1.8M depending on finishes and reservoir proximity.
- **Townhouses and duplexes** along the neighborhood's edges, offering a more affordable entry point in the $500K–$700K range.

Land values in Lakeview have appreciated steadily due to the fixed supply of lots and the neighborhood's proximity to both downtown and major recreation areas. For investors, the infill trend shows no signs of slowing.

## Lifestyle and Recreation

Lakeview's defining feature is its unmatched access to outdoor recreation. The Glenmore Reservoir pathway system connects directly to the neighborhood, offering:

- **40+ km of paved pathways** for cycling, running, and walking along the reservoir and into North Glenmore Park
- **Glenmore Sailing Club** and **Calgary Canoe Club** — both accessible from the neighborhood
- **North Glenmore Park** with picnic areas, playgrounds, off-leash dog areas, and the Heritage Park Historical Village nearby
- **Weaselhead Flats** natural environment park at the west end of the reservoir — one of Calgary's most important urban wildlife corridors

The Lakeview Community Association runs year-round programming including a skating rink, summer camps, community garden, and regular social events. The annual Lakeview community cleanup and block party bring neighbors together in a way that larger communities cannot match.

For daily needs, the Glenmore Landing shopping center is a 5-minute drive and features grocery stores, restaurants, medical offices, and retail. Marda Loop's vibrant strip of shops, cafés, and restaurants is just north on Crowchild Trail — one of Calgary's best walkable commercial districts.

## Schools and Education

Families in Lakeview have access to excellent schools within the community and nearby:

- **Jennie Elliott School** (K–6, CBE) — the community's public elementary, walkable from most homes
- **Bishop Grandin High School** — Catholic secondary within the SW quadrant
- **Central Memorial High School** — public secondary, a short drive away
- **Mount Royal University** — less than 10 minutes by car, offering adult education and degree programs
- **Several private school options** within a 15-minute drive, including Calgary French & International School and Rundle College

The walkability to Jennie Elliott School is a major draw for young families. Kids can walk or bike safely through the quiet residential streets — a rarity in Calgary's newer suburban communities.

## Dining and Local Favorites

Lakeview residents enjoy proximity to some of Calgary's best food destinations:

- **Marda Loop** (5 min) — dozens of restaurants and cafés including Blanco Cantina, My Favorite Ice Cream Shoppe, Lina's Italian Market
- **Britannia area** (5 min) — farm-to-table dining and craft breweries
- **Heritage Park** (adjacent) — the Selkirk Grille overlooks the reservoir with upscale dining in a historic setting
- **Glenmore Landing** (3 min) — everyday dining options including Japanese, Thai, and casual bistros

The community itself has a few local gems, and the Lakeview Bakery has been a neighborhood institution for decades.

## Why Buy in Lakeview

**Location is permanent.** Lakeview sits 10 minutes from downtown Calgary, 15 minutes from the airport via Deerfoot Trail, and directly adjacent to thousands of acres of parkland. You cannot replicate this positioning in any new development.

**Value appreciation.** Inner-city Calgary neighborhoods with fixed lot supply consistently outperform suburban communities in long-term appreciation. Lakeview's combination of reservoir access, school quality, and community character creates durable demand.

**Community character.** Lakeview residents choose this neighborhood intentionally. The result is an engaged, stable community with low crime rates, well-maintained properties, and genuine neighborly connections.

**Infill potential.** For buyers comfortable with renovation or rebuilding, Lakeview's R-C2 zoning on many lots allows for duplexes and secondary suites, creating both lifestyle and income opportunities.

As someone who grew up on these streets, I can tell you that Lakeview delivers something rare in Calgary real estate — the feeling of living in a small town while being minutes from everything a major city offers.

# Cochrane Homes for Sale — Calgary's Premier Mountain-View Bedroom Community

Cochrane is one of Alberta's fastest-growing towns, and for good reason. Sitting just 20 minutes northwest of Calgary along Highway 1A, Cochrane offers the rare combination of small-town lifestyle, mountain views, and big-city employment access. For Calgary homebuyers priced out of the inner city or simply looking for more space, Cochrane has become a top destination.

## Town Overview

Cochrane is a town of approximately 36,000 residents in Rocky View County, positioned at the junction of the Bow River and Bighill Creek. The town's western edge offers unobstructed views of the Rocky Mountain foothills, while its eastern boundary is a 20-minute commute to Calgary's NW communities via Highway 1A or Highway 22.

Originally a ranching community — and still home to the famous Cochrane Ranche Historic Site — the town has evolved into a modern residential hub while fiercely protecting its western heritage character. The main street retains its small-town charm with independent shops, restaurants, and the iconic Mackay's Ice Cream, which draws visitors from across southern Alberta.

Cochrane's population has more than doubled in the past 15 years, driven by families seeking affordable housing, excellent schools, and a slower pace of life. New developments continue to expand the town's footprint, with communities like Fireside, Heartland, and Sunset Ridge offering modern homes at prices well below equivalent Calgary properties.

## Property Types and Pricing

Cochrane offers excellent value compared to Calgary proper:

- **Single-family detached homes** are the dominant property type, ranging from $450,000 for starter homes in established areas to $800,000+ for estate properties on larger lots or with mountain views
- **Townhouses and duplexes** provide affordable entry points at $300,000–$450,000, popular with first-time buyers and young families
- **Estate and acreage properties** on the town's western edge offer 1–5 acre lots with custom homes ranging from $900,000 to $2 million+

The town's western orientation means many homes enjoy direct sightlines to the Rocky Mountain foothills. Sunset views from Cochrane's west-facing communities are among the best in the Calgary region.

Highway 1A improvements and the continued expansion of Stoney Trail have steadily reduced commute times over the past decade. The Town of Cochrane is also actively developing its own commercial and employment base, reducing the need for Calgary commuting.

**Community and safety.** Cochrane consistently ranks among Alberta's safest communities. The small-town culture means neighbors know each other, kids play freely, and community events are well-attended.

**Growth and investment.** Cochrane's population trajectory suggests continued property value appreciation. As Calgary expands westward and remote work becomes permanent, demand for Cochrane real estate will only increase.

**Is Signal Hill a good neighborhood in Calgary?**
Signal Hill is one of Calgary's top-rated SW neighborhoods for families. Excellent schools (including nearby private options like Webber Academy), established shopping at Westhills, proximity to WinSport and the mountains, and mature streetscapes make it consistently popular with homebuyers.

**What quadrant is Signal Hill in Calgary?**
Signal Hill is in Calgary's SW quadrant, west of Sarcee Trail and south of Richmond Road. It is bordered by West Springs to the west, Glamorgan to the east, and the Westhills area to the south. The community has direct access to Sarcee Trail, Stoney Trail, and the Trans-Canada Highway.

**Are homes in Signal Hill old?**
Most Signal Hill homes were built between 1986 and 2000. While they are not new construction, the homes feature solid construction, generous lot sizes, and many have been renovated and updated by successive owners. The established landscaping and mature trees are features that new communities cannot offer.
