# Why Your Home Listing Isn't Showing Up on Google (and How to Fix It)

*By Lisa W. Johnson | Performance Property Partners | March 2026*

You listed your home for sale. Your agent took great photos, wrote a compelling description, and put it on the MLS. So you Google your own address — and your listing doesn't show up. Instead, you see Zillow, Redfin, Realtor.com, and maybe a few other real estate portals you've never heard of.

This is one of the most common frustrations I hear from sellers — and honestly, from agents too. As the owner of Performance Property Partners, I've made it a priority to understand why this happens and what we can do about it.

Here's the truth: it's not a mystery, and it's not unsolvable.

## H2: Why Google Shows Zillow Instead of Your Agent's Website

When someone searches for a specific address like "1004 Cabin Hill Way, Garner, NC," Google decides which pages to show based on a few key factors:

**Domain authority.** Zillow, Redfin, and Realtor.com are massive websites with millions of pages and tens of thousands of backlinks. Google trusts them. Your agent's website — even a well-built one — is a much smaller site with far less link authority.

**Structured data.** The big portals use something called schema markup — a type of code that tells Google exactly what the page is about. When Zillow marks up a listing page with the property address, price, photos, bedrooms, and bathrooms in a format Google can read, it gives that page a significant advantage.

**Content depth.** Portal listings often include neighborhood data, price history, estimated values, comparable sales, school ratings, and walk scores. That's a lot of content around a single address — which helps Google understand the page's relevance.

**Indexing speed.** Zillow and Redfin get their pages indexed by Google within hours. A smaller real estate website might take days or weeks for new listings to appear in Google's index.

## H2: What This Means for Sellers

If buyers can't find your listing on your agent's website, they find it on Zillow instead. And on Zillow, your listing sits alongside "Premier Agent" advertisements — which are typically agents who *paid* to appear next to your home. Those agents didn't list your home, may not know your neighborhood, and have no relationship with you.

Your listing becomes lead generation for someone else.

This doesn't mean your agent is doing a bad job. Most real estate websites — including platforms like Sierra Interactive, KvCORE, and BoomTown — are built for lead capture, not for SEO competition with billion-dollar portals. The good news is that specific, targeted changes can close the gap significantly.

## H2: How to Fix It — What Smart Agents Are Doing

Here's what we do at Performance Property Partners to give our listings the best chance of ranking:

### H3: 1. Add Structured Data (Schema Markup) to Every Listing

This is the single most impactful change. By adding JSON-LD schema markup to each listing page — specifying the address, price, property type, listing agent, and brokerage — we give Google the same structured signals that Zillow provides.

We use the `RealEstateListing` schema type, which tells Google: "This is an active listing by a licensed agent." It's the difference between Google guessing what your page is about and knowing exactly what it is.

### H3: 2. Optimize the Title Tag and Meta Description

Most MLS-fed listing pages have generic titles like "123 Main St, Anytown NC — MLS #12345." We rewrite these to include the full address, city, state, and brokerage name — matching exactly what buyers type into Google.

### H3: 3. Add Unique Content Beyond MLS Data

We add neighborhood descriptions, school information, and local context to listing pages. This gives Google more content to index and helps differentiate our page from the 5 portal pages showing the same MLS data.

### H3: 4. Build Internal Links

Every listing page on our site links to relevant community pages, blog posts, and search results. This internal linking structure helps Google discover and value our listing pages faster.

### H3: 5. Submit to Google Search Console

We actively submit new listing pages to Google for indexing rather than waiting for Google's crawler to find them organically. This cuts indexing time from days to hours.

## H2: What Sellers Should Ask Their Agent

If you're listing your home and want it to show up on Google — not just on Zillow — here are questions to ask your agent:

1. **Does your website use structured data (schema markup) on listing pages?** If they don't know what this means, that's a red flag.
2. **Can you show me my listing on your website?** Make sure it has a dedicated URL with your full address.
3. **Do you optimize title tags and meta descriptions for each listing?** Or are they auto-generated from the MLS?
4. **How do you promote my listing beyond the MLS?** Social media, email marketing, Google Business Profile, and direct SEO efforts all matter.

## H2: The Bottom Line

Your home deserves to be found by buyers searching for your specific address — and your agent's website should be the first result, not a portal that sells ad space next to your listing.

At P3RealtyNC, we're investing in the technical SEO infrastructure to make this happen for our sellers. It's not about competing with Zillow's domain authority — it's about giving Google the right signals so our listings earn their place in search results.

# North Carolina Real Estate Market Report: Spring 2026

*By Lisa W. Johnson | Performance Property Partners | March 2026*

Spring is when the North Carolina real estate market wakes up. After a measured winter, buyer activity is increasing, inventory is slowly expanding, and the data is starting to tell this year's story.

As a broker serving multiple NC markets — from Johnston County to Pinehurst to Coastal Hampstead — I see different dynamics in each area. Here's what the numbers are showing as we head into the spring buying season.

## H2: Statewide NC Market Overview

North Carolina continues to benefit from strong population growth. The state added approximately 120,000 new residents in 2025, with the Triangle, Charlotte metro, and coastal areas absorbing the majority. This migration — driven by remote work flexibility, lower cost of living compared to the Northeast and West Coast, and quality of life — keeps demand elevated.

**Key statewide metrics (Q1 2026):**

- **Median Home Price:** $335,000 (up 3.8% year-over-year)
- **Active Inventory:** Up 12% from Q1 2025 — still below pre-pandemic norms
- **Average Days on Market:** 31 days
- **Mortgage Rates:** Hovering around 6.5% for a 30-year fixed

The market has shifted from the frenzied pace of 2021–2022, but it's far from a buyer's market in most areas. Well-priced homes in desirable locations still receive multiple offers within the first week.

## H2: Johnston County — Clayton, Smithfield & Surrounding Areas

Johnston County remains one of the best values in the Triangle region. Clayton continues to lead growth, but Smithfield, Benson, and Archer Lodge are picking up as buyers look for more space at lower price points.

**Johnston County Q1 2026:**

- **Median Home Price:** $345,000 (up 4.2% YoY)
- **New Construction Share:** ~40% of closed transactions
- **Hottest Segment:** No-HOA homes on 1+ acres — inventory under 60 days
- **Days on Market:** 22 days average
- **Buyer Profile:** Young families from Wake County, military families, and remote workers

The no-HOA market in Johnston County is particularly competitive. Our page on [no-HOA homes in Johnston County](/no-hoa-homes-for-sale-in-johnston-county/) consistently draws interest from buyers who want space without restrictions.

New construction is driving volume, with communities like Flowers Plantation, Riverwood, and newer developments along NC-42 accounting for a significant share of sales.

## H2: Moore County — Pinehurst & Southern Pines

The Pinehurst market has its own rhythm, largely driven by retirees, second-home buyers, and golf enthusiasts. Q1 is typically quieter here as snowbird buyers return north — but this year, activity started earlier than usual.

**Moore County Q1 2026:**

- **Median Home Price:** $375,000 (up 3.1% YoY)
- **55+ Community Demand:** Very high — Del Webb at Pinehurst selling lots within weeks
- **Luxury Segment ($600K+):** Growing — up 18% in transaction volume
- **Days on Market:** 35 days average
- **Buyer Profile:** Northeast/Midwest retirees, second-home buyers, remote professionals

Golf community homes remain the top draw. Our [Pinehurst blog post](/blog/why-people-love-pinehurst-nc/) continues to attract readers from across the country — many of whom become serious buyers.

The Southern Pines downtown area is also driving interest, with walkability, restaurants, and a growing arts scene attracting younger buyers who want small-town charm.

## H2: Pender County — Hampstead, Surf City & Topsail

Coastal NC real estate follows seasonal patterns, and spring is when buyers start touring in earnest. The Hampstead market in particular has strong momentum heading into the season.

**Pender County Q1 2026:**

- **Median Home Price:** $385,000 (up 5.1% YoY — fastest appreciation in our markets)
- **Waterfront Homes:** Extremely limited inventory under $500K
- **New Construction:** Active in Topsail Preserve, Belvedere Plantation, and north Hampstead corridors
- **Days on Market:** 28 days average
- **Buyer Profile:** Military families (Camp Lejeune), Wilmington commuters, vacation-home buyers

Surf City and Topsail Island properties are commanding premium prices, especially turnkey vacation rentals with documented rental income. Oceanfront homes have appreciated 8–12% year-over-year.

## H2: What This Means for Buyers

If you're looking to buy this spring, here's my honest advice:

1. **Get pre-approved before you start touring.** In competitive markets like Clayton and Hampstead, sellers won't consider offers without pre-approval letters.
2. **Don't wait for rates to drop significantly.** Rates may ease slightly, but prices are climbing. Waiting for a lower rate could mean paying a higher price.
3. **Consider Johnston County.** If you're priced out of Wake County, Johnston County offers similar quality of life at a 15–20% discount.
4. **Move fast on waterfront and no-HOA properties.** These segments have the lowest inventory and highest competition.

## H2: What This Means for Sellers

Spring is traditionally the best time to list in North Carolina. If you're considering selling:

1. **Price right from day one.** Overpriced homes sit, especially as buyers have more options than they did in 2022.
2. **Invest in presentation.** Professional photos, staging, and curb appeal improvements still matter — and they directly impact sale price and speed.
3. **Don't ignore digital marketing.** Your listing needs to be optimized for Google, not just the MLS. Ask your agent about schema markup and SEO.
4. **Expect strong results.** Well-priced homes in good condition are selling at or above list price in most of our markets.

# Best Neighborhoods in Johnston County NC (2026 Guide)

*By Lisa W. Johnson | Performance Property Partners | March 2026*

Johnston County is one of the fastest-growing counties in North Carolina, and the question I hear most from relocating buyers is simple: "Where should I live?"

It depends on what you're looking for. Johnston County isn't one-size-fits-all — there are master-planned communities with every amenity, rural homesteads on 10+ acres, and everything in between. After years of helping families find the right fit, here are the neighborhoods and areas I recommend most often.

## H2: Flowers Plantation — Clayton's Flagship Community

**Best for:** Families with school-age children, buyers who want a self-contained community

Flowers Plantation is the largest master-planned community in Johnston County, and for many families, it's the obvious choice. Built around a Fred Couples-designed golf course, Flowers Plantation includes its own elementary and middle schools, a YMCA, shopping center, restaurants, and miles of walking trails.

**What to expect:**
- **Home prices:** $320,000–$650,000+
- **Home types:** Single-family homes from 1,400 to 4,000+ sq ft
- **HOA:** Yes — covers community amenities and landscaping standards
- **Schools:** Flowers Plantation Elementary, West Clayton Elementary, and Clayton-area middle and high schools
- **Commute to Raleigh:** 25–30 minutes via US-70

Flowers Plantation continues to add new phases, so there's both resale inventory and new construction available.

## H2: Riverwood — Clayton

**Best for:** Move-up buyers, golf enthusiasts, families wanting newer homes

Riverwood is centered around the Riverwood Golf Club and the Neuse River. It offers larger lots and newer construction compared to some older Clayton neighborhoods.

**What to expect:**
- **Home prices:** $350,000–$700,000+
- **Home types:** Single-family, many with 4+ bedrooms
- **HOA:** Yes
- **Lot sizes:** Larger than average for Clayton — many homes on 0.3–0.5+ acre lots
- **Key draw:** Golf course access, river proximity, and mature tree cover

## H2: Archer Lodge & Wendell Falls Area

**Best for:** Buyers who want newer homes with easy access to both Raleigh and Clayton

The Archer Lodge area — including the Wendell Falls community just across the Wake County line — offers some of the best new construction value in the region. Wendell Falls itself is technically in Wake County but borders Johnston County and shares many of the same market dynamics.

**What to expect:**
- **Home prices:** $300,000–$550,000
- **Home types:** New construction from national builders (Lennar, Garman, Meritage)
- **Amenities:** Resort-style pools, fitness centers, trails, and event programming
- **Schools:** East Wake schools (Wendell Falls) or Johnston County schools (Archer Lodge)
- **Commute:** 20–25 minutes to Raleigh, 10 minutes to Clayton

## H2: Downtown Smithfield

**Best for:** Buyers who want walkability, character, and lower prices

Smithfield is the Johnston County seat and has seen a genuine downtown revival. New restaurants, a renovated Ava Gardner Museum, and the annual Ham & Yam Festival give it a cultural identity that Clayton doesn't quite have yet.

**What to expect:**
- **Home prices:** $200,000–$400,000
- **Home types:** Mix of historic homes, renovated bungalows, and some newer construction
- **HOA:** Mostly no-HOA in the downtown core
- **Key draw:** Walkable downtown, lower prices, and proximity to I-95 for commuters
- **Character:** Smithfield has soul — old tobacco warehouses converted to shops, tree-lined streets, and a river walk along the Neuse

## H2: Benson & Four Oaks — Rural Johnston County

**Best for:** Buyers wanting acreage, no HOA, and a quiet lifestyle

If you want 2–10+ acres, outbuildings, room for animals, and no HOA telling you what color to paint your mailbox — south Johnston County is your area. Benson and Four Oaks offer the kind of rural living that's increasingly hard to find near the Triangle.

**What to expect:**
- **Home prices:** $225,000–$500,000+ (highly dependent on acreage)
- **Home types:** Older farmhouses, custom-built homes on large lots, modular homes
- **HOA:** Rare — most properties are unrestricted
- **Key draw:** Space, privacy, and significantly lower prices per acre than Wake County
- **Commute:** 30–40 minutes to Raleigh, 15–20 minutes to Smithfield

Our [no-HOA homes in Johnston County page](/no-hoa-homes-for-sale-in-johnston-county/) is consistently one of the most visited pages on our site — for good reason.

## H2: Wilson's Mills & Micro — Between Clayton and Smithfield

**Best for:** Buyers who want to split the difference — some land, reasonable prices, closer to Clayton amenities

Wilson's Mills and Micro sit between Clayton and Smithfield along US-70. These smaller communities offer:

- **Home prices:** $250,000–$450,000
- **Lot sizes:** Often 0.5–2 acres
- **HOA:** Mix — some newer communities have them, many older properties don't
- **Schools:** Johnston County schools — Cleveland and Corinth Holders zones
- **Vibe:** Semi-rural with easy access to Clayton shopping and restaurants

## H2: How to Choose the Right Neighborhood

Here's my framework for helping buyers decide:

| Priority | Best Areas |
|----------|-----------|
| Best schools | Flowers Plantation, Corinth Holders zone |
| Most affordable | Smithfield, Benson, Four Oaks |
| No HOA + acreage | Benson, Four Oaks, south Smithfield |
| New construction | Flowers Plantation, Archer Lodge, Riverwood |
| Closest to Raleigh | Archer Lodge, Cleveland area, Flowers Plantation |
| Most character | Downtown Smithfield |
| Golf community | Flowers Plantation, Riverwood |

# Hampstead NC: The Complete Guide to Coastal Living

*By Lisa W. Johnson | Performance Property Partners | March 2026*

I write about a lot of places in North Carolina, but Hampstead holds a special spot. It's where many of our buyers end up when they discover that Coastal NC living doesn't have to mean Wrightsville Beach prices or Outer Banks remoteness.

Hampstead is the Goldilocks of the NC coast — close enough to Wilmington for work and hospitals, close enough to Surf City for weekend beach days, and affordable enough that you're not house-poor the day you close.

If our [Pinehurst blog post](/blog/why-people-love-pinehurst-nc/) proved that personal, honest community guides resonate with buyers, this is the coastal companion piece. Here's everything I know about living in Hampstead, NC.

## H2: Where Exactly Is Hampstead NC?

Hampstead is an unincorporated community in Pender County, situated along US-17 between Wilmington (20 minutes south) and Surf City / Topsail Island (10 minutes north). It's technically not a town — it doesn't have a municipal government — which means no municipal taxes and no city zoning for most properties.

The community spans roughly from the Pender/New Hanover county line north to the Holly Shelter Road area, and from the Intracoastal Waterway east to I-40 west.

**Key distances from Hampstead:**
- Surf City Beach: 10 minutes
- Downtown Wilmington: 20 minutes
- Wrightsville Beach: 25 minutes
- Camp Lejeune main gate: 35 minutes
- Raleigh: 2 hours
- Wilmington International Airport (ILM): 25 minutes

## H2: Who Lives in Hampstead?

Hampstead attracts a diverse mix:

**Military families:** Camp Lejeune and MCAS New River are a manageable commute, and Hampstead offers more space and better value than Jacksonville.

**Wilmington commuters:** Professionals who work in Wilmington but want more land, newer homes, and lower prices. The US-17 commute is 20-25 minutes.

**Retirees:** Especially from the Northeast and Midwest who want coastal living at a fraction of Florida or Charleston prices. Mild winters, no state income tax on Social Security, and low property taxes are big draws.

**Remote workers:** Post-pandemic buyers who can work from anywhere and chose "anywhere" to be near the beach.

**Families:** Strong schools, safe neighborhoods, and a growing community of young families.

## H2: What Does It Cost to Live in Hampstead?

Here's a realistic breakdown:

- **Median home price:** $385,000 (2026)
- **Property taxes:** Pender County rate — approximately $0.56 per $100 of assessed value. A $385,000 home pays roughly $2,156/year in county taxes alone (no municipal tax for unincorporated Hampstead).
- **Homeowner's insurance:** $1,200–$2,500/year depending on age, construction, and wind zone
- **Flood insurance:** $0–$3,000/year depending on flood zone. Many properties west of US-17 are NOT in a flood zone.
- **Groceries, utilities, gas:** Comparable to Wilmington — slightly lower than national average

Overall, Hampstead's cost of living is 15–25% lower than Wrightsville Beach and comparable to or lower than Wilmington proper.

## H2: The Real Neighborhoods of Hampstead

**Topsail Preserve:** Newer community with homes from the $350s–$500s. Close to Surf City, strong builder options, community amenities.

**Belvedere Plantation:** Established community with golf course, marina, and deep-water ICW access. Homes range from $300,000 to $800,000+. Popular with boaters and retirees.

**Olde Point:** Golf community with a 36-hole championship course. Mix of existing homes and new construction. $350,000–$700,000.

**Sloop Point/Stump Sound Area:** Older, more rural feel. Larger lots, waterfront on Stump Sound, and a mix of older homes and custom builds. Great for buyers who want water access without a master-planned community.

**US-17 Corridor (West Hampstead):** The commercial growth area. New neighborhoods are going in along US-17 with easy access to shopping, restaurants, and I-40.

**Scotts Hill:** South Hampstead, close to the New Hanover County line. A blend of established neighborhoods and newer developments. Easy Wilmington access.

## H2: Beaches Near Hampstead

You don't live in Hampstead for the beach in your backyard — you live here because the beach is 10 minutes away and your mortgage is half what it would be on the island.

- **Surf City / Topsail Island:** 10–15 min. Your primary beach. Less crowded than Wrightsville, family-friendly, great surf fishing.
- **North Topsail Beach:** 20 min. Even less crowded. Good for shell collecting and long walks.
- **Wrightsville Beach:** 25 min. More amenities, restaurants, nightlife. Great for day trips.
- **Carolina Beach:** 35 min. Boardwalk, amusement park, family vibe.
- **Figure Eight Island:** Private island accessible only to residents/guests. Adjacent to Hampstead.

## H2: Schools in Hampstead NC

Pender County Schools serves Hampstead:

- **Topsail Elementary** — Strong community school, well-regarded
- **Topsail Middle School** — Recently improved facilities
- **Topsail High School** — Solid academics, competitive sports (especially soccer and track)
- **Cape Fear Community College** — Satellite campus in Hampstead area
- **UNCW** — 25 minutes south in Wilmington

Private options: Coastal Christian High School, several Montessori programs in the greater Wilmington area.

## H2: The Things Nobody Tells You About Hampstead

After years of helping buyers settle here, here are the honest things I tell everyone:

**The US-17 commute gets old in summer.** Beach traffic from Memorial Day to Labor Day can slow the drive to Wilmington to 35–40 minutes. Plan accordingly, or use back roads.

**Flooding is real — but manageable.** Properties near the ICW and tidal creeks will flood during major storms. But many neighborhoods west of US-17 are outside flood zones entirely. Always check the FEMA flood maps for specific addresses.

**Healthcare is improving.** Novant Health and NHRMC have expanded facilities along US-17. For major medical needs, Wilmington's hospitals are 20 minutes away.

**Bugs are part of coastal life.** Mosquitoes, no-see-ums, and sand gnats are active from May through October. Screened porches aren't optional — they're essential.

**The community is real.** Hampstead doesn't have a downtown or a mayor, but it has a strong identity. Local Facebook groups, church communities, volunteer fire departments, and neighborhood events create genuine connections.

## H2: Is Hampstead NC Right for You?

Hampstead is ideal if you:

- Want beach access without beach prices
- Need to commute to Wilmington, Camp Lejeune, or work remotely
- Value space, newer homes, and lower taxes
- Like boating, fishing, and outdoor living
- Want strong schools for your kids
- Prefer a quieter, less tourist-heavy coastal community

Hampstead may NOT be ideal if you:

- Want walkable urban living (there is no "downtown" Hampstead)
- Need to be within 5 minutes of major nightlife or entertainment
- Strongly prefer not to drive for daily errands (though this is improving quickly)

## H2: Frequently Asked Questions About Hampstead NC

**Q: Is Hampstead NC a safe place to live?**
A: Yes. Hampstead has low crime rates and a strong community feel. Pender County is one of the safer counties in the coastal NC region.

**Q: Is Hampstead NC growing?**
A: Rapidly. Pender County is one of the fastest-growing counties in NC, and Hampstead is the primary growth center. New neighborhoods, commercial developments, and infrastructure improvements are underway along US-17.

**Q: What's the difference between Hampstead and Surf City?**
A: Hampstead is a mainland community in Pender County — more affordable, with larger lots and newer subdivisions. Surf City is on Topsail Island — direct beach access, higher prices, and a more vacation-oriented atmosphere. Many Hampstead residents use Surf City as their primary beach.

**Q: Do you need flood insurance in Hampstead NC?**
A: It depends on the specific property. Many homes west of US-17 are outside FEMA flood zones and do not require flood insurance. Properties near the ICW, creeks, and low-lying areas will require it. Lisa Johnson can help you check flood zone status for any property.
