## 2. CPC and CTR Benchmark Analysis

### CPC: $0.82 vs Industry $1.50-$3.00

The average CPC of $0.82 is **45-73% below** typical real estate industry CPCs. While this sounds positive, it warrants caution:

- **Why it's low:** Maximize Clicks bidding actively seeks the cheapest available clicks. Combined with DSA, Google is finding low-competition, long-tail queries where clicks are cheap.
- **The trade-off:** Cheap clicks in real estate often mean lower-intent traffic. Users searching broad terms like "homes for sale in NC" are cheaper to reach than someone searching "3 bedroom home for sale in Pinehurst under $400K" -- but the latter is far more likely to become a client.
- **DS Cities #2 CPC is $1.12** -- closer to what you'd expect for real estate, and interestingly this campaign also has a higher conversion rate. Higher CPC may actually correlate with better-quality traffic.

### CTR: 9.70% vs Industry 3-5%

The CTR of 9.70% is **2-3x the industry average.** This is almost certainly a red flag rather than a positive signal:

- **DSA headline matching:** Dynamic Search Ads automatically generate headlines from the website, creating highly relevant-looking ad copy for any query. This inflates CTR.
- **Maximize Clicks amplification:** The bidding strategy preferentially serves ads to users most likely to click, regardless of conversion potential. Google's ML model optimizes for click probability, not lead quality.
- **The quality paradox:** Users who click on everything are cheap to reach and click at high rates -- but they are also the least likely to convert into real leads. A 9.70% CTR with only a 1.48% conversion rate confirms this pattern.

**Bottom line:** High CTR + Low CPC + Low Conversion Rate is the classic Maximize Clicks trap. The account is efficiently buying clicks, but not efficiently buying leads.

## 3. Conversion Analysis

### Conversion Metrics

- **30 conversions in 14 days** = 2.14 conversions/day
- **Conversion rate:** 1.48% (30 conversions / 2,024 clicks)
- **Cost per conversion:** $55.52
- **Industry benchmark CPL for NC real estate:** $30-$75

### Conversion Quality Concerns

The $55.52 CPL is within the NC real estate benchmark range, but conversion quality needs verification:

1. **IDX forced registrations** are not real leads. When a website forces visitors to register before viewing listings, almost everyone provides fake information (throwaway email, fake phone number). These registrations inflate conversion counts but rarely produce contactable prospects.

2. **Phone calls** are higher quality but need duration filtering. A 5-second call where someone hangs up is not a lead. Google Ads should be configured to only count phone calls longer than 60 seconds as conversions.

3. **Zero closed deals** after 30 "conversions" is a signal. While 2 weeks is too early to judge deal closure (real estate has long sales cycles), Lisa should be manually reviewing each conversion to assess actual lead quality.

### Positive Early Signals

- The account is generating traffic and some form of conversion activity in just 2 weeks
- The DSA campaigns are discovering relevant search terms across target markets
- The CPL is not catastrophically high for a brand-new account
- Some converting search terms show strong intent: "pinehurst nc homes for sale by owner," "horse farm for sale southern pines nc," "homes for sale with land near me"

### Concerning Signals

- Only 5 out of 2,939 unique search terms produced conversions (0.17% of terms)
- The vast majority of spend goes to non-converting search terms
- 81 of 85 ad groups with spend have zero conversions
- Conversion rate of 1.48% is below the 2-5% real estate benchmark
- DSA is matching to very broad queries that may not serve Lisa's specific markets
