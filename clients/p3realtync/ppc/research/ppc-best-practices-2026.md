## 1. Google Ads Best Practices for Real Estate Agents (2026)

### Current Best Practice

**Start with Search Ads and Local Service Ads (LSAs).** These are the highest-ROI starting points for most agents because they capture high-intent leads -- people actively searching for a realtor or homes right now. LSAs appear above traditional Search Ads and operate on a pay-per-lead model ($60-$75 per qualified contact), meaning you only pay when someone actually calls or messages.

**Campaign structure matters.** A solo agent on $1,000/month should run a tightly focused Search campaign, not spread budget across multiple campaign types. The recommended allocation is roughly 60% toward seller keywords (higher value, higher CPC) and 40% toward buyer keywords (lower CPC, higher volume).

**Landing pages must match ad intent.** Sending ad traffic to a general homepage cuts conversion rates by 50% or more compared to a dedicated landing page. A high-converting real estate landing page in 2026 has: one headline that mirrors the ad exactly, a single lead capture form, visible trust signals (reviews, headshot, license number), and zero navigation links that let the visitor wander away.

### 2026 Performance Benchmarks

| Metric | Healthy Range | Red Flag |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| Click-Through Rate (CTR) | 4% - 7% | Below 4% consistently |
| Cost Per Click (CPC) | $2.50 - $5.50 (buyer keywords); $5 - $30+ (seller keywords) | N/A -- varies by keyword |
| Landing Page Conversion Rate | 3% - 5% | Below 2% |
| Cost Per Lead (CPL) | $50 - $170 (varies by market) | Over $200 in non-luxury markets |
| Average Conversion Rate | 3.28% (search) | Below 2% |

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- Google's AI-powered bidding and targeting have matured significantly; automation is now expected, not optional.
- AI Max for Search campaigns launched in 2025, combining broad match and keywordless technology -- this may eventually replace DSA.
- Local Service Ads expanded to more real estate categories and markets.
- First-party data and conversion signal quality became the primary differentiator between accounts that scale and accounts that plateau.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Build a dedicated Search campaign first** -- not DSA-only. Use manually selected keywords with exact and phrase match for high-intent terms like "homes for sale Wilmington NC," "Wilmington NC realtor," and "sell my house Wilmington."
2. **Create dedicated landing pages** per keyword theme (buyer vs. seller, by area). Do not send PPC traffic to the IDX homepage.
3. **Set up proper conversion tracking before spending another dollar** -- distinguish between form fills, phone calls, and IDX registrations. Assign different values to each.
4. **Investigate Local Service Ads** -- at $60-$75 per qualified lead on a pay-per-lead basis, this may deliver better ROI than standard Search for a new agent building credibility.
5. **Budget discipline:** $1,000/month is viable for a solo agent but leaves no room for waste. Every click must be intentional.

## 2. Dynamic Search Ads (DSA) Best Practices and Optimization

### Current Best Practice

**DSAs should supplement standard Search campaigns -- never replace them.** DSA uses Google's web crawling to match user queries to your website content and auto-generate headlines. This is useful for discovering long-tail keywords you missed, but it sacrifices control over messaging, targeting precision, and landing page selection.

**DSA is best used for:**
- Discovery: Identifying new keyword opportunities (Google reports 85% of DSA-driven queries are incremental beyond existing keyword targeting)
- Large inventory sites (e-commerce, real estate listings)
- Filling gaps in keyword coverage

**DSA is problematic when:**
- Your website has thin, outdated, or poorly structured content
- You have non-transactional pages (blog posts, career pages, login pages) that trigger irrelevant ads
- You are on a tight budget where every click must be carefully controlled
- You lack robust negative keyword lists to filter junk queries

### Pros

- Discovers hidden long-tail keywords -- advertisers typically find 10-20% of total volume from queries not covered by manual keyword lists
- Reduced ongoing management -- auto-adapts when website content changes
- Can improve Quality Scores through highly relevant dynamic headlines
- Useful for sites with large, frequently changing inventory (like IDX listings)

### Cons

- **Limited headline control** -- Google picks the headline from your page content, which may not be your best messaging
- **Website quality dependent** -- if page titles and content are not well-optimized, you get irrelevant or weak ads
- **IDX-specific risk:** DSA will match queries to individual listing pages, login/registration pages, and other non-converting pages unless you aggressively exclude them
- **Budget risk on tight budgets** -- broader matching can burn through spend on low-intent queries quickly

### DSA Optimization Checklist

1. Start DSA at 10-20% of total search spend during discovery phase
2. After 30-60 days, reallocate budget to targets that meet CPA thresholds
3. Review search terms report weekly -- add negative keywords for irrelevant queries
4. Exclude non-transactional pages (blog, about, careers, login, registration pages)
5. Layer RLSA (Remarketing Lists for Search Ads) on DSA to bid more aggressively on returning visitors
6. Use page feeds to control exactly which URLs DSA can target
7. Write strong description lines (the only ad copy element you control)
8. Add all relevant ad extensions (sitelinks, callouts, call extensions)

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- **AI Max for Search** launched in 2025, combining broad match and keywordless technology similar to DSA but with more intelligence. DSA may eventually be deprecated in favor of AI Max.
- DSA now benefits from improved site-crawling AI, but still requires clean website structure.
- Budget allocation best practice shifted: start with Maximize Conversions bidding (not Maximize Clicks) on DSA from the start, then move to Target CPA after 30-60 days.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Do NOT run DSA as the primary or only campaign type.** Build a standard Search campaign with manually selected keywords first. DSA should be a supplementary discovery tool at 10-20% of budget ($100-$200/month).
2. **The IDX website is a DSA risk.** Hundreds or thousands of listing pages will trigger ads for all sorts of property queries -- many of which are low-intent or out-of-area. Individual listing pages change constantly as MLS data updates. Registration walls will cause poor user experience when DSA sends someone to a page they cannot view without signing up.
3. **If running DSA, exclude:** All registration/login pages, blog posts, about pages, agent bio pages, and any page that does not have a clear conversion path.
4. **Monitor search terms report religiously** -- at least weekly for the first 60 days. DSA with an IDX site will attract queries for specific addresses, competing brokerages, rental properties, and commercial real estate.
5. **Consider pausing DSA entirely until a standard Search campaign is established and hitting CPA targets.** Then add DSA as a controlled experiment.

## 3. Bidding Strategy: Maximize Clicks vs Maximize Conversions vs Target CPA

### Current Best Practice

The recommended progression for a new Google Ads account in 2026 is:

**Phase 1: Maximize Conversions (Days 1-30)**
Start with Maximize Conversions from Day 1, even on a new account. Google's algorithm now leverages broad auction-time signals beyond just your account history, making it viable even with zero conversion data. This prioritizes getting conversions rather than just cheap clicks.

**Phase 2: Target CPA (After 30+ conversions in 30 days)**
Once you have at least 30 conversions within a 30-day window, switch to Target CPA. Set the CPA target at or slightly above your current average CPA to avoid choking volume. Campaigns with 40-50 conversions per month show noticeably more stable performance.

**Phase 3: Ongoing Optimization**
Continue optimizing around Target CPA, adjusting the target down gradually as performance stabilizes. Never drop it more than 15-20% at once or you risk the learning phase reset.

### Why Maximize Clicks is Problematic

| Factor | Maximize Clicks | Maximize Conversions |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Optimization target** | Most clicks for budget | Most conversions for budget |
| **Traffic quality** | Does not filter for intent | Prioritizes likely converters |
| **Cost control** | You can set max CPC cap | Budget-constrained, no CPC cap |
| **Learning requirement** | None -- works immediately | Benefits from conversion data but works without it |
| **Risk** | Attracts low-quality, cheap clicks | May have erratic performance early while learning |
| **Best use case** | Brand awareness, audience building | Lead generation, sales |

**Key insight:** Maximize Clicks optimizes for volume, not quality. It will find the cheapest clicks available, which in real estate often means informational queries, tire-kickers, and renters -- not buyers or sellers. You pay for traffic that never converts.

### The Conversion Data Threshold

Google officially recommends at least 30 conversions in the last 30 days at the campaign level for Target CPA. However:

- The 30-conversion threshold is a minimum. 40-50 per month produces more stable results.
- **Critical caveat for this client:** 30 IDX registrations at $55.52 CPL may meet the volume threshold, but the conversion quality matters enormously. If these are forced IDX registrations (many of which are fake or low-quality), Google is optimizing toward junk leads. The algorithm will find more people willing to fill in a form -- not more people willing to buy a house.

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- Google's smart bidding algorithms improved significantly -- starting with Maximize Conversions on a new account is now viable and often recommended over the traditional "start with Maximize Clicks" approach.
- Target CPA is now a sub-setting within Maximize Conversions (you enable it by adding a target CPA amount), not a separate strategy.
- Offline conversion import became more important -- feeding back actual lead quality data from CRM helps the algorithm distinguish between junk registrations and real prospects.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Switch from Maximize Clicks to Maximize Conversions immediately.** The account has 30 conversions in 2 weeks, which exceeds the data threshold. Maximize Clicks is driving cheap, low-quality traffic.
2. **Before switching, fix conversion tracking.** The current "conversion" is an IDX forced registration. This conflates real leads with junk. Set up tiered conversion tracking:
   - Primary conversion: Phone call (60+ seconds) -- high intent signal
   - Primary conversion: "Schedule showing" or "Contact agent" form submission
   - Secondary conversion (observation only): IDX registration -- do NOT let Google optimize toward this
3. **After fixing tracking, switch to Maximize Conversions** using the phone call and form submissions as the primary conversion actions.
4. **Target CPA:** Once you have 30+ real conversions (phone calls + quality form fills) in 30 days, add a Target CPA. Start at your current average CPA for those quality actions, then reduce gradually.
5. **Import offline conversion data** from your CRM: when a lead becomes a showing, a listing appointment, or a closed deal, feed that data back into Google Ads so the algorithm learns what a real customer looks like.

## 4. Geo-Targeting: Presence vs Presence or Interest

### Current Best Practice

**For local service businesses like real estate agents, use "Presence" only targeting.** This shows ads exclusively to people physically located in or regularly located in your target area. The "Presence or Interest" default is one of the most common ways local businesses unintentionally waste ad spend.

### The Problem with "Presence or Interest"

Google defaults all new campaigns to "Presence or Interest" and labels it "Recommended." This shows your ads to:
- People physically in your target area (good)
- People anywhere in the world who have "shown interest" in your area (often bad for local services)

**What "interest" means in practice:** Someone in California searching "Wilmington NC homes for sale" will see your ad. While this may capture some relocation buyers, it also captures:
- Casual researchers with no purchase intent
- People browsing real estate in multiple markets with no commitment
- Competitors researching your market
- Investment researchers who will never contact a local agent

**For a real estate agent, this is a nuanced decision:**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| **Listing agent (sellers)** | Presence only -- sellers are local |
| **Buyer agent (local buyers)** | Presence only -- captures in-market buyers |
| **Buyer agent (relocation market)** | Presence or Interest may add value -- captures out-of-state buyers moving to Wilmington |
| **Tight budget ($1,000/month)** | Presence only -- cannot afford to waste clicks on casual researchers |

### The Real Estate Exception

Google's own data shows that advertisers in Travel, Real Estate, and Education who switched from Presence to Presence or Interest saw +5% more conversions on Search campaigns. This makes sense because real estate inherently involves people searching for properties in locations they do not currently live.

**However**, this data point comes with critical context:
- It measures conversion volume, not conversion quality
- A 5% increase in IDX registrations from out-of-state browsers is not valuable if none of them close
- On a $1,000/month budget, even a 10-15% waste on out-of-area clicks is $100-$150/month lost

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- Google's location signals improved but the default is still "Presence or Interest" -- this has not changed.
- More advertisers are reporting improved results with "Presence" only as awareness of the issue grows.
- No significant algorithm changes to how location targeting works in 2025-2026.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Switch to "Presence" only targeting immediately.** On a $1,000/month budget, every dollar wasted on out-of-area casual browsers is a dollar not spent reaching a local buyer or seller.
2. **Target specific geographies:** Wilmington, Wrightsville Beach, Carolina Beach, Kure Beach, Leland, Hampstead, Surf City, Topsail, Southport, and surrounding New Hanover / Brunswick / Pender County areas. Use a combination of radius targeting (e.g., 25-mile radius around Wilmington) and specific city/ZIP targeting.
3. **If the client wants relocation leads later**, create a SEPARATE campaign with "Presence or Interest" targeting, its own budget, and its own conversion tracking. This isolates the experiment and prevents contamination of the primary local campaign's data.
4. **Run a geographic performance report** on the existing campaign to quantify how much spend is going to out-of-area clicks. This data will reveal the scope of the current waste.

## 5. Negative Keyword Best Practices for Real Estate PPC

### Current Best Practice

A real estate PPC campaign without aggressive negative keywords is guaranteed to waste 20-40% of its budget on irrelevant clicks. Negative keywords should be added at three levels: account-level (universal exclusions), campaign-level (specific to campaign intent), and ad group-level (granular filtering).

### Industry-Standard Negative Keyword List for Real Estate

**Rental / Lease Terms (exclude if targeting buyers/sellers only)**
- rent, rental, rentals, renting, renter
- lease, leasing, leaseholder
- apartment, apartments, apt
- sublet, subletting
- section 8, subsidized housing
- month to month
- studio apartment

**Job / Career / Education Terms**
- jobs, job, career, careers, hiring
- salary, salaries, pay, income, compensation
- become, becoming, "how to become"
- license, licensing, exam, test, course, courses
- training, certification, school
- real estate agent salary
- real estate career

**Commercial / Industrial (exclude if residential-only agent)**
- commercial, industrial, warehouse
- office space, office building
- retail space, strip mall
- zoning, rezoning
- land development

**DIY / Informational / Non-Transactional**
- free, cheap, discount
- DIY, "how to"
- definition, meaning, "what is"
- template, form, calculator
- wiki, wikipedia
- youtube, video, tutorial

**Competitor Portals (unless running conquest campaigns)**
- zillow, trulia, realtor.com, redfin
- homes.com, movoto

**Government / Legal**
- foreclosure (unless investor-focused)
- tax lien, tax sale, tax deed
- HUD, government, auction
- sheriff sale, probate

**Irrelevant Property Types**
- mobile home, manufactured home, trailer
- tiny house, van life
- vacation rental, Airbnb, VRBO
- timeshare
- houseboat

**Spam / Junk**
- bitcoin, crypto
- scam, fraud
- no credit check
- wholesale (unless investor-focused)

### 2025-2026 Updates

- Performance Max now supports up to 10,000 negative keywords per campaign (up from 100), a 100x increase announced March 2025.
- Shared negative keyword lists are now supported across Performance Max campaigns (rolled out August 2025).
- Account-level negatives are now the recommended approach for universal exclusions (adult content, competitor names, career terms).
- Quality matters more than quantity -- over-blocking costs opportunity. Review search terms weekly and add negatives surgically rather than pre-loading thousands of untested exclusions.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Implement the full negative keyword list above immediately.** A DSA campaign without negatives on an IDX site will match to rental searches, job searches, competitor portal searches, and dozens of other irrelevant intents.
2. **Add account-level negatives** for: career/job terms, adult content terms, competitor portal names (unless running conquest), and "free" modifiers.
3. **Add campaign-level negatives** specific to each campaign's intent (e.g., exclude "sell" terms from buyer campaigns and "buy" terms from seller campaigns).
4. **Review the search terms report weekly.** For a DSA campaign on an IDX site, expect to add 10-20 new negatives per week for the first 2-3 months. Common offenders: specific addresses the agent does not represent, neighboring city names outside the service area, rental-intent queries, competitor agent names.
5. **Set a calendar reminder** to review search terms every Monday. This is non-negotiable for the first 90 days.

## 6. IDX Forced Registration Conversion Tracking

### Current Best Practice

IDX forced registration generates high lead volume but low lead quality. It is a legitimate lead capture mechanism, but it must NOT be treated as the primary conversion action for Google Ads optimization purposes.

### The Forced Registration Trade-Off

| Metric | With Forced Registration | Without Forced Registration |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| Lead volume | 5-13x higher | Baseline |
| Lead quality | Lower -- includes fake info, tire-kickers | Higher -- only truly motivated visitors register |
| Bounce rate | Higher -- visitors leave rather than register | Lower |
| Conversion rate to closed deal | 0.4% - 1.2% | Higher per-lead, but lower total volume |
| Fake/junk leads | Significant (fake names, fake numbers, invalid emails) | Minimal |

**Key statistic:** Forced registration generates up to 1,300% more leads via PPC, but the conversion rate from internet lead to closed transaction is only 0.4% - 1.2% industry-wide. That means for every 200 forced IDX registrations, expect to close 1-2 deals.

### The Conversion Tracking Problem

**If IDX registration is your primary Google Ads conversion action, you are training Google's algorithm to optimize for junk leads.**

The algorithm does not know whether a registration is a real buyer or someone who typed "aaa@fake.com" to get past the wall. It treats every registration as an equally valuable conversion and optimizes to find more people who will fill in the form. This creates a feedback loop:
1. Google optimizes for cheap registrations
2. You get more registrations from less qualified traffic
3. CPL looks good on paper ($55/lead) but ROI is zero (0 closed deals)
4. Google learns from this "success" and delivers more of the same

### Best Practice: Tiered Conversion Tracking

Set up a conversion hierarchy in Google Ads:

**Primary Conversion Actions (optimize toward these):**
- Phone call from website (60+ seconds duration)
- "Schedule a Showing" form submission
- "Contact Agent" form submission
- Call extension clicks (from ads directly)

**Secondary Conversion Actions (track but do NOT optimize toward):**
- IDX registration
- Property save/favorite
- Search alert signup

**Offline Conversions (import from CRM):**
- Showing scheduled
- Listing appointment set
- Offer submitted
- Deal closed

### Optimal Registration Trigger Timing

If using forced registration, the best practice is NOT to force it immediately on landing:
- Allow 2-3 property views before triggering registration
- Show key photos and basic details before the gate
- Clearly communicate that registration unlocks full details
- This reduces bounce rate while still capturing motivated browsers

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- Google Ads conversion tracking now supports "conversion value rules" that allow you to assign different values to different conversion types, making tiered tracking easier.
- Offline conversion import via enhanced conversions became more accessible and is now considered essential for lead gen campaigns.
- The industry consensus shifted firmly: volume of IDX registrations is a vanity metric. Quality signals (calls, showings, appointments) are what matter.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Immediately reclassify IDX registration as a secondary (observation-only) conversion.** Stop letting Google optimize toward forced registrations.
2. **Set up phone call tracking as a primary conversion.** Use Google's forwarding number or a third-party call tracker (CallRail, WhatConverts). Set minimum duration to 60 seconds to filter out wrong numbers and hang-ups.
3. **Create a "Contact Agent" or "Schedule Showing" landing page** separate from the IDX. This gives you a clean, high-intent conversion action to optimize toward.
4. **Adjust registration trigger to 2nd-3rd property view** instead of immediate forced registration. This will reduce junk registrations while capturing genuinely interested browsers.
5. **Implement CRM-based offline conversion tracking.** When an IDX registration becomes a phone call, a showing, or a closed deal, feed that data back to Google Ads. Over time, this trains the algorithm to find the KIND of person who closes, not just the kind who fills in a form.
6. **Reality check on current metrics:** 30 conversions at $55.52 CPL with 0 closed deals strongly suggests these are mostly junk IDX registrations, not real leads. The $1,665 spent may have generated near-zero actual pipeline.

## 7. Speed to Lead Statistics for Real Estate

### Current Best Practice

**Contact every lead within 5 minutes.** This is the single highest-impact factor in converting internet leads to appointments and ultimately to closed deals. No amount of ad spend optimization compensates for slow follow-up.

### The Data

| Response Time | Impact |
|---------------|--------|
| Under 60 seconds | 55% more leads converted to appointments vs. 5-minute response |
| Under 5 minutes | 21x more likely to qualify than leads contacted after 30 minutes |
| Under 5 minutes | 9x more likely to convert than leads contacted after 30 minutes |
| 5-30 minutes | Dramatic drop-off in contact and qualification rates |
| 30+ minutes | Lead is likely already talking to another agent |
| Average agent response time | **917 minutes (over 15 hours)** -- per Inman 2025 survey |
| Top 1% of agents | 2.3-minute average response time |

**78% of homebuyers end up working with the first agent who responds.** (Source: NAR)

### Agent Performance Benchmarks

| Metric | Average Agent | Top 1% Agent |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Response time | 47 hours | 2.3 minutes |
| Lead-to-appointment rate | 8% | 28% |
| Lead-to-client rate | 1.2% | 5.8% |

### Lead Nurturing Timeline

Not every lead converts immediately. The timeline matters:
- **Search/PPC leads:** 1-3 months average nurturing period before transaction
- **Social media leads:** 6-18 months nurturing before conversion
- **Leads receiving 6+ contact attempts:** Convert at rates 70% higher than those receiving fewer touches
- **Industry average:** 1 deal per 66 internet leads generated (roughly 1.5% close rate)
- **Top performers:** 3-5% close rate from internet leads

### Conversion Rates by Lead Source (2026 Data)

## 9. Google Ads Performance Max for Real Estate

### Current Best Practice

**Do NOT start with Performance Max.** Build Search campaign foundations first. Performance Max should only be added after 60-90 days of Search campaign data with clean conversion signals.

### What Performance Max Is

Performance Max (PMax) is Google's AI-driven campaign type that runs across ALL Google properties simultaneously: Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Discover, and Maps. It uses machine learning to allocate budget across channels based on where it predicts conversions will come from.

### Why It Fails for New Real Estate Accounts

| Problem | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| **Needs conversion data to optimize** | New accounts have no history; the AI guesses poorly |
| **Optimizes for cheap conversions by default** | If IDX registrations are the conversion action, PMax will find the cheapest registrations -- often from Display and Discover placements where intent is lowest |
| **Black box reporting** | Limited visibility into which placements and keywords are driving results |
| **Display/YouTube traffic contamination** | PMax often shifts budget to Display and YouTube where CPCs are cheaper but intent is near-zero for real estate |
| **Lead quality issues** | Widely reported problem: PMax generates high volume of junk leads for lead gen businesses |

### When Performance Max Makes Sense for Real Estate

Performance Max becomes viable when ALL of these conditions are met:
1. You have a mature Search campaign running for 60-90+ days
2. Your conversion tracking is clean (phone calls, quality form submissions -- NOT IDX registrations)
3. You have 30+ real conversions per month to feed the algorithm
4. You are importing offline conversion data (showings, appointments, closed deals) from your CRM
5. You have budget headroom beyond your Search campaigns ($500+/month incremental)
6. You have creative assets ready (images, videos, headlines, descriptions for all placements)

### Performance Max Lead Quality Solutions (if/when implemented later)

1. **Use value-based bidding** (Target ROAS) with different conversion values: phone call = $100, form fill = $50, IDX registration = $1
2. **Import offline conversion data** so the algorithm learns what a REAL customer looks like
3. **Apply negative keywords** (up to 10,000 per campaign as of 2025)
4. **Add audience signals** -- upload customer lists, use in-market audiences for real estate
5. **Regularly clean data** -- remove duplicate or spam leads to prevent AI bias
6. **Exclude brand terms** from PMax to prevent it from cannibalizing cheap brand search clicks

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- PMax negative keywords expanded from 100 to 10,000 per campaign (March 2025), dramatically improving control.
- Shared negative keyword lists now apply across PMax campaigns (August 2025).
- Search term insights improved, providing more visibility into what queries triggered ads.
- Despite improvements, the core issue remains: PMax is only as good as the data you feed it. Garbage in, garbage out.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Do NOT launch Performance Max now.** The account is 2 weeks old, conversion tracking is questionable (likely counting IDX registrations as conversions), and there is no CRM integration for offline conversion data.
2. **Sequence:** Build Search campaign first (months 1-3) --> Fix conversion tracking --> Accumulate quality conversion data --> Consider PMax as a supplement (month 4+ at earliest).
3. **Before ever launching PMax:** Implement phone call tracking, CRM offline conversion import, and value-based bidding on Search campaigns first.
4. **If PMax is added later:** Start at 20-30% of total budget as a test, with its own conversion tracking and negative keyword list. Monitor for Display/YouTube spam traffic closely.
5. **Alternative to PMax:** Consider Local Service Ads (LSAs) as the second campaign type instead. LSAs are pay-per-lead, appear above all other ads, and are specifically designed for local service providers. This is likely a better second campaign for a new real estate agent than PMax.

## 10. Ad Scheduling and Dayparting Best Practices

### Current Best Practice

**Let data drive scheduling decisions, not assumptions.** Run ads 24/7 for the first 30-60 days to collect baseline data, then use that data to apply bid adjustments and schedule restrictions.

### Real Estate Search Behavior Patterns

Based on industry data:

| Time Period | Search Behavior | Recommendation |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Weekday mornings (6-9 AM)** | Commute browsing -- moderate intent | Standard bids |
| **Weekday business hours (9 AM-5 PM)** | Active searching -- high intent, especially M-W | Increase bids 10-20% |
| **Weekday evenings (6-10 PM)** | Peak browsing -- people searching after work | Increase bids 20-30% |
| **Late night (11 PM-5 AM)** | Very low intent -- casual browsing, insomnia searches | Decrease bids 30-50% or pause |
| **Saturday** | High activity -- weekend house hunting | Increase bids 15-25% |
| **Sunday** | Moderate-high -- open house planning, post-weekend reflection | Increase bids 10-20% |

### Important Caveats

1. **Weekend CPL may be higher.** Data shows weekend spend has a 51% higher cost per lead than weekdays in some markets. Higher volume does not always mean better efficiency.
2. **Mon-Wed 10 AM-3 PM** showed conversion rates 30% above account average in some studies -- this is when serious buyers/sellers take action during business hours.
3. **10-15% of budget** is typically wasted on zero-conversion time slots (typically late night hours).
4. **Do not over-restrict early.** Narrowing schedules before you have data kills performance. You might assume nobody searches at 7 AM on Tuesday, but your data might show otherwise.

### Implementation Strategy

**Phase 1: Data Collection (Weeks 1-4)**
- Run ads 24/7
- Track conversion data by hour and day of week
- Use Google Ads "When and where ads showed" report

**Phase 2: Initial Optimization (Weeks 5-8)**
- Identify zero-conversion time slots (typically 12 AM - 5 AM)
- Reduce bids by 30-50% during consistently low-performing periods
- Increase bids 15-30% during peak conversion periods
- Do NOT pause any time slots entirely yet

**Phase 3: Ongoing Refinement (Month 3+)**
- Review monthly -- user behavior changes with seasons
- Real estate is highly seasonal: spring/summer peaks, winter slowdowns
- Adjust bid modifiers quarterly based on accumulated data
- Consider pausing late-night hours (12 AM - 5 AM) if consistently zero conversions

### Speed-to-Lead Interaction

**Critical consideration:** Ad scheduling must align with your ability to respond.

If you run ads at 10 PM but do not check leads until 9 AM the next day, that is an 11-hour response time. The lead is gone. Options:
1. Only run ads during hours you can respond within 5 minutes
2. Set up automated first-response (auto-text/auto-email) for off-hours leads
3. Use a virtual receptionist or AI answering service for off-hours calls

### What Changed in 2025-2026

- Google's automated bidding (Target CPA, Maximize Conversions) handles much of the dayparting optimization automatically. When using smart bidding, manual ad schedules are less critical because the algorithm adjusts bids in real time.
- However, ad schedules still control when ads CAN show. Smart bidding only adjusts bids within the window you allow.
- AI-driven scheduling tools emerged that automatically recommend schedules based on historical performance data.
- Seasonal patterns in real estate became more pronounced post-pandemic normalization.

### Recommendations for This Client

1. **Do not restrict ad scheduling yet.** The account is only 2 weeks old -- there is not enough data to make informed scheduling decisions.
2. **Run ads 24/7 for the first 30-60 days** with conversion tracking properly configured.
3. **After 30 days, pull the "Day and Hour" report** from Google Ads to identify:
   - Hours with zero conversions (candidates for bid reduction)
   - Hours/days with highest conversion rates (candidates for bid increases)
   - Hours/days with highest CPL (candidates for bid reduction)
4. **Align ad hours with response capability.** If the client cannot respond to leads after 9 PM or before 7 AM, either:
   - Set up automated first-response for off-hours leads, OR
   - Reduce bids by 40-50% during off-hours (do not pause entirely -- some people submit forms at night expecting a morning callback)
5. **Watch for seasonal patterns.** Wilmington is a coastal market with strong spring/summer demand. Budget allocation should be heavier March-August and lighter November-February.
6. **Once on Target CPA or Maximize Conversions bidding**, the algorithm handles much of the bid optimization by time of day. Your schedule should primarily control when ads are eligible to show, not micro-manage bid adjustments.
