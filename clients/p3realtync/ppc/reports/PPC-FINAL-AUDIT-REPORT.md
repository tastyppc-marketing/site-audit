## 3. The Core Problem

Here is the full picture of what is happening with this account:

**$1,665 spent in 2 weeks. 30 registrations. 0 closed deals.**

The money is being spent, but it is not reaching the right people, in the right places, at the right times. Five specific problems are compounding to create this situation:

### Problem 1: The Budget Is Uncontrolled
The daily budgets across all four campaigns total approximately **$119/day**, which is **3.6x the $33/day** that a $1,000/month budget requires. At this pace, the account will spend **$3,570 this month**.

### Problem 2: Ads Are Showing Everywhere Except the Service Area
The geo-targeting is set to **"Presence & Interest"**, which means anyone in America who searches for anything related to NC real estate can see these ads. The result:

| Where the Money Goes | % of Spend | What It Means |
|---------------------|-----------|---------------|
| Out of state (42 states + DC) | **42.2%** | People in California, New York, Kansas clicking on ads for NC homes |
| Other NC (outside service area) | **48.7%** | People in Raleigh, Charlotte, Fayetteville -- not Lisa's market |
| **Actual SE NC service area** | **9.1%** | The only clicks that could realistically become clients |

Only **$51.31 out of $560.78** in location-attributed spend reached the actual Wilmington/coastal SE NC service area. Ads were served in **51 states and territories**, including Hawaii, Alaska, Montana, and Wyoming.

### Problem 3: The Campaign Structure Is Over-Built
There are **221 ad groups** spread across 4 campaigns on a $1,000/month budget. At that budget, each ad group averages **$1.08/day** -- not enough for Google to learn anything or optimize effectively. Of those 221:

- **56 ad groups** (25%) have zero impressions -- Google found no matching content
- **65 ad groups** (29%) are near-dead with 1-5 impressions and zero clicks
- **121 total** (55%) are functionally inactive
- Only **4 named ad groups** actually produced conversions

### Problem 4: Duplicate Campaigns Bid Against Themselves
"DS Cities" and "DS Cities #2" contain the **exact same 18 ad groups**. "DS Property Features" and "DS Property Features #2" share **89 overlapping ad groups**. Google found **286 search terms** that triggered ads from both duplicate campaigns, meaning Lisa is bidding against herself in every auction -- driving up her own costs.

### Problem 5: The 30 "Conversions" Are Not Real Leads
The IDX website forces visitors to register before viewing listings. Most of these registrations involve fake email addresses and throwaway information. The conversion-to-closed-deal rate for IDX forced registrations is **0.4% to 1.2%** industry-wide. At 30 registrations and a 1% close rate, the statistical expectation is **0.3 deals** -- essentially zero, which matches the actual result.

**The bottom line:** The combination of DSA + Maximize Clicks + Presence & Interest targeting is a system optimized for buying the cheapest possible clicks from anyone, anywhere. It is doing that job well. It is not a system designed to find people in southeastern North Carolina who want to buy a home through Lisa Johnson.

## 11. Lead Quality and Follow-Up

### The Conversion Quality Problem

The 30 "conversions" reported by Google Ads are primarily **IDX forced registrations** -- the website requires visitors to create an account before viewing listings. This is a common lead capture technique in real estate, but it creates a misleading picture:

| What Google Reports | What It Actually Means |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| 30 conversions | 30 people filled in a registration form |

| 1.48% conversion rate | 1.48% of clickers filled in the required form |

### IDX Registration Reality

| Metric | Industry Data |
|--------|--------------|
| Forced registration lead volume | 5-13x higher than optional registration |
| Fake/junk info rate | Significant -- fake emails, fake phone numbers |
| Registration-to-closed-deal rate | **0.4% - 1.2%** |
| Expected deals from 30 registrations | **0.1 - 0.4 deals** (essentially zero) |

### Speed to Lead: The Most Important Metric Nobody Tracks

Research consistently shows that **response time is the single biggest factor** in converting internet leads to appointments:

| Response Time | Impact |
|---------------|--------|
| Under 60 seconds | 55% more leads converted vs. 5-minute response |
| Under 5 minutes | 21x more likely to qualify than 30-minute response |
| **78% of buyers work with the first agent who responds** | NAR data |
| Average agent response time | **917 minutes (over 15 hours)** |
| Top 1% of agents | 2.3-minute average response time |

**If Lisa is not responding to leads within 5 minutes, the ad spend is wasted regardless of how well the campaigns are optimized.** A $55 lead that gets a call 6 hours later is worth less than a $100 lead that gets a call in 2 minutes.

### What Needs to Happen

1. **Set up automated first-response** -- an auto-text or auto-email within 60 seconds acknowledging every lead
2. **Personal follow-up within 5 minutes** during business hours
3. **Track response time as a KPI** -- this matters more than CTR or CPC
4. **Plan for 6+ contact attempts per lead** -- leads receiving 6+ touches convert 70% better
5. **Implement a CRM** to track the full journey: registration --> contact --> showing --> offer --> close
6. **Consider reducing forced registration friction** -- allow 2-3 property views before triggering the registration gate
