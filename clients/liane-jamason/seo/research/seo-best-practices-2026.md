## 1. Google Algorithm Updates & Ranking Factors

### Current Best Practice

Google's ranking algorithm in 2026 is powered by AI-driven semantic understanding and continuous quality evaluation. The core ranking factors, in approximate order of weight, are:

1. **Content quality and topical authority** — Intent alignment, depth, and comparative value across SERPs. Google evaluates how much genuinely new information a page contributes vs. content that already ranks.
2. **E-E-A-T signals** — 72% of top-ranking pages feature detailed author biographies with verifiable credentials. Experience is the signal most resistant to AI fabrication.
3. **Core Web Vitals** — Technical performance now shapes search results more than ever. Pages in position 1 show a 10% higher CWV pass rate than those in position 9.
4. **Backlink authority and relevance** — Quality over quantity; editorial links from authoritative, topically relevant sources.
5. **User engagement signals** — Dwell time, bounce rate patterns, click-through rates.
6. **Structured data** — Schema markup as a direct translator between content and AI systems.

### What Changed Recently

- **March 2026 core update** (rolled out March 27, completed in 12 days): Over 55% of monitored sites experienced ranking shifts. Some saw 20-35% traffic drops in week one. SEMrush Sensor hit 9.5/10 volatility.
  - **Information originality** is now explicitly evaluated — pages that rephrase existing top results without adding original data, first-hand experience, or proprietary insights lose ground.
  - **Gemini 4.0 Semantic Filter** deployed to detect content produced at scale without meaningful human editorial oversight. AI-assisted content with genuine human expertise still performs well.
  - **Thin/generic content** hardest hit. Sites with strong topical authority and original insights gained.
- **February 2026 Discover update**: Prioritizes locally relevant content, reduces sensational/clickbait content, highlights in-depth original content from sites with demonstrated expertise.
- **December 2025 core update**: Reinforced E-E-A-T, content depth, and technical SEO as critical foundations.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- Ensure every page contributes unique value (original market data, first-hand waterfront expertise, local knowledge competitors cannot replicate).
- Use AI as a drafting tool only — all content must be reviewed and enriched with real experience, specific examples, and editorial judgment.
- Maintain strong technical performance (CWV passing) alongside content quality.
- Websites using original data saw a 22% increase in visibility — publish proprietary St. Petersburg waterfront market analyses.

## 2. AI Overviews / SGE Optimization

### Current Best Practice

Google AI Overviews (formerly SGE) appear in 50%+ of all searches by Q1 2026, with highest prevalence in informational, how-to, and comparison queries. For real estate specifically, AI Overviews appear in up to 50% of local-intent queries — significantly higher than the ~7% average for local searches.

**How to get cited:**

1. **Answer the question directly at the top** — State the answer clearly in the first two sentences, then support with detail. Do not bury the answer.
2. **Create extractable content blocks** — Definition blocks ("What is [Topic]? [Topic] is..."), comparison tables, numbered lists, and bullet summaries. AI wants a "nucleus" of truth it can extract instantly.
3. **Match H2 headers to search queries** — H2s are semantic triggers. AI crawlers look for "query-to-heading" match to identify relevant passages.
4. **Mirror the format of existing AI Overviews** — If Google presents a numbered list for your target topic, structure content as numbered steps. If it shows definition + examples, mirror that format.
5. **Implement comprehensive schema markup** — Schema acts as a direct translator between content and AI systems. Highest-impact technical investment for GEO (Generative Engine Optimization).
6. **Build authority signals** — E-E-A-T is foundational for GEO, not optional. Content must be extractable, verifiable, and from a credible source.

**Content characteristics that perform well in GEO:**
- **Extractability** — Key information can be isolated and referenced without losing meaning
- **Verifiability** — Every statement is sourced or factual
- **Atomicity** — AIs extract snippets, not entire pages

### What Changed Recently

- AI Overviews now appear in majority of informational searches, up from limited rollout in 2024.
- Google AI Mode cites its own properties in 17% of all answers (per analysis of 1.3M citations), making third-party citation harder but more valuable.
- 60% of AI Overview citations point to third-party publishers; 40% cite individual local businesses.
- For real estate, Google Maps and Google Business Profiles provide substantial citation material to AI Overviews.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- Structure every service page with a tight answer at the top, a real price/value range, the 3 local factors that matter, and one clear CTA.
- Create dedicated content blocks that AI can quote directly: "What does a luxury waterfront home in St. Petersburg cost? Prices range from..."
- Build FAQ sections structured as question-answer pairs matching actual search queries.
- Ensure Google Business Profile is complete and consistent — AI Overviews pull from GBP data.
- Earn reviews that mention specific services + neighborhoods/landmarks (e.g., "helped us find our waterfront home on Tampa Bay").

## 3. E-E-A-T Signals

### Current Best Practice

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is the evaluation framework used by Google's quality raters. In 2026, it directly impacts both traditional rankings and AI citation likelihood.

**Trust is the foundation** — "Trust is the most important member of the E-E-A-T family because untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how Experienced, Expert, or Authoritative they may seem." (Google Search Quality Rater Guidelines)

#### Experience (structurally immune to AI generation)
- First-hand knowledge of the subject matter — actually doing the thing you write about
- For real estate: personal transaction experience, property walkthroughs, neighborhood knowledge, client stories
- The scarcest and most valuable E-E-A-T signal in 2026 because AI cannot fabricate genuine experience
- **Signals:** Original photography, video walkthroughs, case studies with specific details, "I helped a client..." narratives

#### Expertise
- Deep, accurate, comprehensive knowledge of the subject
- **Signals:** Correct use of technical terms with definitions, content that explains the "why" not just the "what," in-depth explanations beyond the obvious
- For real estate: understanding of market dynamics, zoning, flood zones, insurance requirements, waterfront property considerations

#### Authoritativeness
- Authority is granted externally — you are authoritative when other authorities say so
- **Signals:** Mentions, links, and quotes from credible industry sites; media coverage; industry awards; professional affiliations
- For real estate: citations from local news, real estate publications, board memberships, speaking engagements

#### Trustworthiness
- **Signals:** Detailed "About Us" page, clear author bios with verifiable credentials, accessible contact info, privacy policy, transparent business practices
- For real estate: license numbers displayed, BBB rating, professional association memberships, client testimonials with full names (with permission)

### What Changed Recently

- E-E-A-T now affects AI citation likelihood, not just traditional rankings — AI tools like Gemini, ChatGPT, and Perplexity evaluate source credibility using E-E-A-T signals.
- The March 2026 core update explicitly reinforced E-E-A-T, with content lacking clear expertise or credible sourcing losing visibility.
- Brand signals are increasingly important — Google connects content quality to the broader brand entity.
- Author SEO (structured data for author profiles) is now a measurable ranking factor.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- **Author bio page:** Create a comprehensive about/bio page with license number, years of experience, transaction history, specializations (waterfront/luxury), professional photo, video introduction, and links to professional profiles.
- **Author schema markup:** Implement Person schema with `sameAs` links to Zillow, Realtor.com, LinkedIn, and professional association profiles.
- **Experience content:** Publish content that only someone with real waterfront real estate experience could write — flood insurance insights, dock permit processes, seawall inspection tips, specific market trend analysis for St. Petersburg waterfront neighborhoods.
- **Trust signals on every page:** Display license number, brokerage affiliation, contact information, and professional credentials.
- **Earn external authority:** Seek quotes in local St. Petersburg media, contribute to real estate publications, participate in local business organizations.

## 4. Schema Markup Best Practices

### Current Best Practice

Schema markup is the highest-impact technical investment for both traditional SEO and AI citation in 2026. Google recommends JSON-LD format exclusively.

#### Essential Schema Types for Real Estate

| Schema Type | Where to Use | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **RealEstateAgent** | Site-wide / About page | Primary business entity; subtype of LocalBusiness. Includes name, credentials, license, areas served, specializations |
| **LocalBusiness** | Site-wide | Establishes physical location, hours, contact info, service area. Foundation for local pack visibility |
| **RealEstateListing** | Property listing pages | Structured property data: price, availability, features, agent details |
| **Residence / SingleFamilyResidence** | Individual property pages | Specific property details: address, rooms, floor area, amenities, price |
| **FAQPage** | Service pages, neighborhood guides, FAQ sections | Structures Q&A for rich results; high AI citation value |
| **Article / BlogPosting** | Blog posts, market reports | Content type identification with author, date, topic |
| **Person** | About/bio page | Author/agent credentials, sameAs links, expertise areas |
| **BreadcrumbList** | All pages | Navigation structure for search engines |
| **VideoObject** | Property tour pages | Video indexing for YouTube and Google video carousels |
| **Review / AggregateRating** | Testimonial pages | Social proof structured data |
| **Service** | Service pages | Specific services offered with descriptions and service areas |

#### Implementation Rules

1. **Use JSON-LD format** — Google's recommended format; separates structured data from HTML
2. **Use the most specific subtype available** — `RealEstateAgent` over generic `LocalBusiness`
3. **Data must match visible content** — Search engines compare schema against page content; discrepancies result in penalties or reduced trust
4. **Validate with Google Rich Results Test** after every implementation
5. **Include `areaServed`** — Critical for local businesses to define service geography
6. **Add `sameAs` links** — Connect to Zillow, Realtor.com, LinkedIn, social profiles

### What Changed Recently

- Schema markup now feeds AI Overviews directly — structured data is the primary machine-readable signal AI systems use.
- VideoObject schema has become essential for property walkthroughs, improving rankings in YouTube and Google video carousels.
- Voice search optimization relies on schema — over 60% of home searches reportedly involve voice queries, and schema enables smart assistants to describe listings.
- 3D tour and augmented reality structured data tags are now indexable.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- Implement `RealEstateAgent` schema site-wide with full credentials, license number, and `areaServed` covering St. Petersburg and surrounding waterfront communities.
- Add `FAQPage` schema to every service page and neighborhood guide.
- Use `Person` schema on the about page with `sameAs` links to all professional profiles.
- Add `VideoObject` schema for any property tour or market update videos.
- Validate all schema with Google's Rich Results Test and Schema.org validator.
- Include `Service` schema for each service offered (buyer representation, seller representation, waterfront property specialist, luxury home marketing).

## 5. Content Quality Standards

### Current Best Practice

#### Word Count Benchmarks (2026)

| Page Type | Recommended Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Neighborhood guide / pillar page | 2,000-3,000 words | Hub pages; include market stats, school ratings, amenities, lifestyle |
| Service page | 1,500-2,000 words | Answer the primary query + address related questions |
| Blog post / market update | 1,500-2,000 words | Position 1 pages average ~1,750 words |
| Property listing | 300-800 words | Unique description + neighborhood context |
| About / bio page | 500-1,000 words | Comprehensive but scannable |
| FAQ page | 1,000-2,000 words | 10-20 questions with thorough answers |

**Key principle:** Pages ranked well because they were complete, not because they were long. Short pages still win if they fully satisfy intent.

#### Semantic Completeness

Every topic has a **Central Entity** and defining **Attributes**. Covering these attributes systematically ensures semantic completeness:

- Identify the primary entity of each page (e.g., "waterfront homes in St. Petersburg")
- Map all defining attributes (price ranges, neighborhoods, property types, lifestyle features, buying process, regulations)
- Cover related entities (schools, marinas, restaurants, parks, flood zones, insurance)
- Use natural language variations and related terms throughout — not keyword stuffing, but comprehensive coverage

#### Content Freshness

- **Top 3 results are nearly 2x fresher** than positions 4-10: average content age of ~9.5 months vs. ~18 months
- 72% of top-3 pages were updated within the past year
- Freshness matters most for topics where accuracy changes over time (market conditions, regulations, pricing)
- Updated examples, current data, and revised guidance improve trust signals

#### Quality Signals

- Original data and first-hand experience (not rephrased from existing top results)
- Clear structure with descriptive H2/H3 headers
- Internal linking to related content on the site
- External citations to authoritative sources
- Author attribution with credentials
- Multimedia: images, video, maps, charts where appropriate

### What Changed Recently

- Google's March 2026 update explicitly penalizes pages that contribute no new information vs. existing top results.
- AI-generated content at scale without human editorial oversight is being filtered (Gemini 4.0 Semantic Filter).
- Semantic structure and entity coverage are weighted more heavily than raw word count.
- Content that demonstrates "comparative value" (why is this page better than what already ranks?) is rewarded.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- Every page must answer: "What does this page offer that existing top results don't?" Original St. Petersburg waterfront market data, personal transaction insights, and hyperlocal knowledge are the differentiators.
- Update market-related content quarterly at minimum — prices, inventory, trends, regulations.
- Build comprehensive neighborhood guides (2,000+ words) covering all entity attributes: market data, schools, dining, recreation, waterfront access, flood zones, HOA info.
- Include original photography and video — signals experience and cannot be replicated by AI content.
- Add "Last updated" dates to all content pages.

## 6. Local SEO for Real Estate

### Current Best Practice

#### Google Business Profile (GBP) — The #1 Local Ranking Signal

GBP accounts for **more than 30% of map pack visibility** and is often the primary decision point for high-intent mobile searches.

**Optimization checklist:**
- Complete every field: business name, address, phone, website, hours, categories, service areas, attributes
- Primary category: "Real estate agent" (most specific available)
- Secondary categories: "Real estate agency," "Real estate consultant" as appropriate
- Service areas: All St. Petersburg waterfront neighborhoods and surrounding communities
- Business description: Keyword-rich, 750 characters, highlighting specializations
- Photos: 20+ high-quality images (office, headshot, property examples, neighborhood shots). Update monthly.
- Posts: Publish weekly — market updates, new listings, sold properties, community events
- Q&A: Seed with common buyer/seller questions and provide detailed answers
- Products/Services: List all services with descriptions

#### Review Management

Reviews account for **17% of local ranking formula**:
- Target consistent new reviews (2-4 per month minimum)
- Respond to every review (positive and negative) within 24 hours
- Encourage reviews that mention specific services + neighborhoods ("helped us find a waterfront home in Shore Acres")
- Review velocity and recency matter as much as total count

#### NAP Consistency

- Name, Address, Phone must match **exactly** across all platforms — even "St." vs "Street" matters
- Build 30-50 high-quality citations as a foundation
- Priority platforms for real estate: Zillow, Realtor.com, Homes.com, Redfin, Trulia, Yelp, BBB, local Chamber of Commerce
- Audit for inconsistencies quarterly

#### Local Content Strategy

- Build landing pages for every neighborhood served
- Include local market data, school info, amenities, lifestyle information
- Embed Google Maps with correct location
- Internal linking between neighborhood pages and service pages

#### Three Critical Local Ranking Factors

1. Google Business Profile completeness and activity
2. Review count, recency, and average rating
3. On-page relevance signals (location + service keywords on website)

### What Changed Recently

- **Dynamic profiles are the new ranking factor** — Static, "set and forget" GBP profiles lose ground to profiles with regular posts, photo updates, Q&A activity, and review responses.
- GBP data feeds AI Overviews — inconsistent or missing information means exclusion from AI-generated local recommendations.
- AI tools (Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity) reference GBP, Zillow, Realtor.com, and Yelp profiles when generating local recommendations. Inconsistent data across these platforms means you are less likely to be cited.
- GBP optimization produces local map pack appearances within 45-90 days; competitive dominance takes 9-18 months.

## 7. Core Web Vitals & Page Speed

### Current Thresholds (2026)

| Metric | Good | Needs Improvement | Poor | What It Measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LCP** (Largest Contentful Paint) | < 2.5s | 2.5s - 4.0s | > 4.0s | Loading speed — when main content is visible |
| **INP** (Interaction to Next Paint) | < 200ms | 200ms - 500ms | > 500ms | Responsiveness — how fast the page responds to clicks/taps |
| **CLS** (Cumulative Layout Shift) | < 0.1 | 0.1 - 0.25 | > 0.25 | Visual stability — how much the layout shifts during loading |

**All three must pass at the 75th percentile** for a page to achieve an overall "good" Core Web Vitals assessment.

### Impact on Rankings

- Pages in position 1 show a **10% higher CWV pass rate** than those in position 9.
- The March 2026 core update strengthened the weight of performance in the ranking algorithm.
- Sites passing all three CWV thresholds show **24% lower bounce rates** and measurably higher engagement.
- Pages loading under 2 seconds: 9% bounce rate. Over 5 seconds: 38% bounce rate.
- For every second of delay beyond the 2.5s LCP threshold, bounce rates increase by 32%.

### INP — The Most Failed Metric

**43% of sites fail the 200ms INP threshold**, making it the most commonly failed Core Web Vital in 2026. Only 47% of all sites reach "good" on all three metrics.

INP has three phases:
1. **Input delay** — Waiting for background JavaScript tasks to complete
2. **Processing time** — Running event handlers
3. **Presentation delay** — Layout recalculation and painting

**Key fix:** Break JavaScript tasks longer than 50ms into smaller chunks. Use `scheduler.yield()` to allow user interactions between work chunks.

**Mobile devices show 60-80% worse INP scores** than desktop due to slower processors — critical for real estate where most searches are mobile.

### What Changed Recently

- INP replaced FID (First Input Delay) as a Core Web Vital in May 2024. INP measures all interactions, not just the first.
- March 2026 core update explicitly increased performance weight in ranking algorithm.
- Only 47% of sites pass all three CWV thresholds — passing is a competitive advantage.
- The remaining 53% of sites that fail lose 8-35% of conversions, traffic, and revenue.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- **LCP target: under 2.0 seconds** — Optimize hero images (WebP/AVIF format, correct sizing, preload), minimize render-blocking CSS/JS, ensure fast server response (under 200ms TTFB).
- **INP target: under 150ms** — Audit all JavaScript for long tasks. Real estate sites with map widgets, property search filters, and image galleries are common INP offenders. Defer non-critical scripts, break long tasks, use `requestIdleCallback` for analytics.
- **CLS target: under 0.05** — Set explicit width/height on all images and ad slots. Reserve space for dynamically loaded content (maps, listing carousels).
- Test on real mobile devices, not just desktop — mobile INP scores are 60-80% worse.
- Use PageSpeed Insights and Chrome UX Report for field data (real user metrics), not just lab tests.

## 8. Link Building Strategies

### Current Best Practice — What Works

Link building in 2026 is about earning the right links from the right sources. Quality, relevance, and editorial context matter more than volume.

#### Highest-ROI Strategies

1. **Digital PR** — The most underused strategy in real estate. Journalists need expert sources for housing market trends, home buying advice, and neighborhood guides. Every media placement earns a high-authority editorial link (DR 80-90+).

2. **Original research and data** — Publish proprietary market reports, price analyses, neighborhood comparisons. Original data studies are the most durable link source.

3. **Guest posting on relevant publications** — Real estate, finance, lifestyle, and local media sites. Must be genuine expert contributions, not link-drop articles.

4. **Broken link building** — Find broken links on relevant sites and offer your content as a replacement.

5. **Community involvement** — Sponsor local events, collaborate with local organizations, contribute to area-specific websites. Builds relevance and trust in the local market.

6. **Local media and business partnerships** — Earn links from local news outlets, Chamber of Commerce, neighborhood associations, local business directories.

#### Real Estate-Specific Link Sources

- Local news coverage of market trends (Tampa Bay Times, St. Pete Catalyst, etc.)
- Chamber of Commerce and economic development organizations
- Local event sponsorships and community organizations
- Real estate industry publications and blogs
- Neighborhood association websites
- Local school and community resource sites
- Partners: mortgage brokers, home inspectors, title companies, interior designers

### What Gets Penalized

- **Buying links** — Violates Google's guidelines; risk of manual penalty
- **Private Blog Networks (PBNs)** — Detected more quickly than ever with AI
- **Link farms and link schemes** — Reciprocal link exchanges at scale
- **Over-optimized anchor text** — Exact-match anchors are one of the fastest penalty triggers
- **AI-generated link-building content at scale** — Detectable and penalized

### What Changed Recently

- Post-March 2026 update, editorial citations from original data studies and digital PR campaigns are the most durable link type.
- Google's AI detection has improved significantly — manipulative link schemes are detected faster.
- Link building is now about authority profile diversity, not volume. Sites dominating competitive SERPs have diverse, relevant backlink profiles built consistently over time.
- A solo agent who publishes detailed neighborhood guides and earns local media mentions can outrank national brokerages for "[neighborhood] real estate agent" terms.

## 9. Mobile-First Indexing Requirements

### Current Standards

Google uses the **mobile version** of a site's content for indexing and ranking. This has been the default since 2023 and is non-negotiable in 2026.

#### Responsive Design (Google's Preferred Method)
- Same HTML and URL for all devices
- CSS media queries adapt layout to screen size
- Simplifies maintenance and ensures consistent content/metadata

#### Content Parity
- Mobile site must contain **identical content** to desktop — same text, images, videos, structured data, meta tags, headings
- If mobile has less content than desktop, Google indexes the lesser version
- Do not hide content behind "Read more" toggles that require interaction to expand on mobile (Google will not index content behind user-interaction triggers)

#### Performance Standards
- Google recommends **under 2.5 seconds load time on 4G networks**
- Server response time (TTFB): under 200 milliseconds
- Tap targets: minimum 48x48 CSS pixels with 8px spacing
- Font size: minimum 16px for body text
- Viewport: must include `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">`

#### Interstitial Rules
- Full-screen popups on first page load are penalized
- Acceptable: banners taking less than 20% of screen height, or interstitials for legal requirements (age verification, cookies)
- No full-screen email captures on mobile landing pages

#### Technical Requirements
- Same `robots` meta tags on mobile and desktop versions
- Do not lazy-load primary content on user interaction
- All structured data present on mobile version
- Same canonical URLs
- Images: accessible, proper alt text, not too large for mobile bandwidth

### What Changed Recently

- Mobile-first is now so deeply embedded that Google rarely discusses it as a separate initiative — it is the baseline assumption.
- INP (Interaction to Next Paint) has become the primary performance concern on mobile, with 60-80% worse scores than desktop.
- Mobile devices account for the majority of real estate search activity, making mobile performance a direct revenue factor.
- Google penalizes sites with content disparity between mobile and desktop more aggressively.

### Actionable Recommendations for Liane Jamason

- Use responsive design exclusively — no separate mobile site or dynamic serving.
- Ensure full content parity: all text, images, schema, and meta tags present on mobile.
- Test on actual mobile devices (not just Chrome DevTools) for real-world INP scores.
- Optimize images: WebP/AVIF format, responsive `srcset`, lazy loading for below-fold images only.
- No interstitials on landing pages — use subtle bottom banners for newsletter signups or contact CTAs.
- Property search and map widgets must be touch-optimized with adequate tap targets.
- Test on slow connections (3G/4G throttling) to ensure acceptable performance for users in areas with weak signal.
