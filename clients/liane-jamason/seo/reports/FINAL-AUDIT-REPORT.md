# SEO Audit Report: lianejamason.com

**Client:** Liane Jamason, Broker/Owner — Corcoran Dwellings (Jamason Group)
**Site:** https://www.lianejamason.com/
**Location:** St. Petersburg, FL / Tampa Bay
**Goal:** Leads (primary) > Traffic > Brand awareness (full funnel)
**Audit Date:** April 8, 2026
**Platform:** WordPress (Genesis Framework / AgentPress theme) with Rank Math SEO

---

## Executive Summary

**Overall Site Health Grade: D+**

lianejamason.com is a content-rich site with 287 blog posts, 365+ total pages, and genuine expertise that most competitors cannot replicate. Liane's press credentials (Forbes, Wall Street Journal, USA Today, 9+ Bay News 9 TV segments), prolific long-form blogging (2,000-4,000 word posts), and broker/owner authority are real assets. However, the site has severe technical SEO deficiencies, broken lead capture funnels, and zero non-branded organic visibility that collectively prevent these strengths from translating into search traffic or leads.

**Top 5 Most Critical Issues:**

1. **Zero non-branded organic visibility.** Of 25 target keywords researched, the site ranks for only 2 — both branded queries ("Liane Jamason realtor" and "Corcoran Dwellings St Petersburg"). There is no ranking for any commercial keyword: luxury homes, waterfront homes, neighborhood names, or agent-intent queries. Every non-branded search sends potential clients to Zillow, Redfin, or local competitors.

2. **Missing sitemap.xml and broken robots.txt.** The two foundational files that tell search engines how to crawl the site are absent or non-functional. The existing sitemap index includes 7 attachment sitemaps feeding hundreds of thin media pages into Google's index, plus one broken sitemap returning a 404. Search engines are crawling junk while missing valuable content.

3. **Broken primary lead capture funnel.** The Market Analysis / home valuation page — the single most important seller lead capture mechanism — has a broken or non-rendering form. The homepage CTA "What's My Home Worth?" links directly to this dead end. The seller conversion path is: Homepage > Market Analysis > nothing.

4. **60%+ of pages missing meta descriptions.** The homepage, blog index, reviews page, property search pages, active listings, sold listings, and most area pages have no meta descriptions. Google generates its own snippets for these pages, and they are not optimized.

5. **70-83% of images missing alt text.** Across all audited pages, the majority of content images — hero images, headshots, press logos, property photos, blog featured images — lack alt text. This hurts accessibility, image search visibility, and AI content understanding.

**The bottom line:** Liane has more content than most competitors, stronger press credentials than any of them, and genuine local expertise that shows in her best blog posts. But the site's technical foundation is so weak that none of this content is discoverable through search. Fixing the technical issues and building targeted landing pages will unlock substantial organic traffic within 3-6 months.

## Section 2: Technical SEO Issues

### Critical Issues (Fix Immediately)

**1. Missing / broken sitemap infrastructure**
- **What:** The sitemap index at `/sitemap_index.xml` contains 13 child sitemaps, including 7 attachment sitemaps (feeding hundreds of thin media pages into the index) and one broken sitemap (`idx_page-sitemap.xml` returns 404).
- **Why it matters:** Search engines are wasting crawl budget on thin attachment pages while potentially missing valuable content pages. The broken sitemap signals poor site maintenance.
- **Fix:** In Rank Math settings, disable attachment page indexing to remove the 7 attachment sitemaps. Remove or regenerate the broken `idx_page-sitemap.xml`. Verify all remaining sitemaps resolve correctly.

**2. Robots.txt is inadequate**
- **What:** Current robots.txt is basic (blocks wp-admin, has a 10-second crawl-delay). It does not block attachment pages, author archives, thank-you pages, outdated job postings, or off-topic content.
- **Why it matters:** Crawl-delay of 10 seconds means Bing crawls the site extremely slowly. No rules exist for AI crawlers (GPTBot, CCBot). Thin pages are being indexed without restriction.
- **Fix:** Add disallow rules for `/author/`, `/tag/` (if thin), `/thank-you/`, `/thank-you-for-contacting-us/`, job posting pages, and off-topic content. Reduce crawl-delay to 2-5 seconds or remove it. Add AI crawler directives.

**3. 60%+ of core pages missing meta descriptions**
- **What:** The homepage, blog index, reviews page, property search, active listings, sold listings, and most area/neighborhood pages have no meta descriptions. The Active Listings page has the WRONG description ("View My Sold Listings"). The Market Analysis page has a typo ("you home" instead of "your home").
- **Why it matters:** Meta descriptions are your 160-character sales pitch in search results. Without them, Google auto-generates snippets that may not represent the page well.
- **Fix:** Write unique, keyword-rich meta descriptions for every page. Prioritize homepage ("St. Petersburg luxury & waterfront real estate..."), blog index, reviews, and all neighborhood pages.

**4. Broken pages that fail to load**
- **What:** The "In the Press" page times out on every attempt. The "Sell" page times out on first load. The "Short Sales" page times out. The "Clearwater" page shows 0 results (IDX query broken).
- **Why it matters:** If pages cannot load for users, they cannot load for Googlebot either. The Press page is particularly damaging because it contains Liane's strongest E-E-A-T signals (Forbes, WSJ, Bay News 9 mentions).
- **Fix:** Investigate what is causing the timeouts (likely heavy IDX widgets or broken embeds). Consider lazy-loading IDX components. Fix the Clearwater IDX query parameters.

**5. Market Analysis page form is broken**
- **What:** The primary home valuation / CMA request form at `/market-analysis/` appears to be non-rendering. The page has only ~30 words of content and a non-visible form.
- **Why it matters:** This is the single most important seller lead capture page on the site. Every "What's My Home Worth?" CTA links here. The conversion funnel is broken at its most critical point.
- **Fix:** Verify the form embed code. If the iframe or widget is outdated, replace it. Add 200+ words of content explaining what a CMA is, what the seller will receive, and why it matters.

### High Priority Issues

**6. Multiple H1 tags and heading hierarchy violations**
- **What:** Contact page has 2 H1s. St. Petersburg page has 2 H1s. Market Update blog post has 9 H1s. Gulfport has 2 H1s. Tampa has 2 H1s. Search Luxury has 2 H1s. REO Department has 2 H1s. Blog index has no H1. Multiple pages have generic single-word H1s ("About", "Sell", "Reviews", "Contact").
- **Why it matters:** H1 tags signal the primary topic of a page to search engines. Multiple H1s dilute topical signals. Generic H1s waste the most important on-page SEO element.
- **Fix:** Ensure every page has exactly one H1 that includes the target keyword. Change subheaders to H2/H3. Fix: "About" becomes "About Liane Jamason | St. Petersburg Luxury Realtor". "Sell" becomes "Sell Your St. Petersburg Home with Liane Jamason".

**7. 70-83% of images missing alt text**
- **What:** Across all audited pages, the majority of content images lack alt text. This includes hero/banner images, Liane's professional headshot, press logos (Bay News 9, Tampa Bay Times, Huffington Post), property photos, and blog featured images.
- **Why it matters:** Alt text is essential for accessibility (screen readers), image search visibility, and helping AI systems understand page content. Google explicitly uses alt text for image ranking.
- **Fix:** Add descriptive alt text to every content image. Examples: `alt="Liane Jamason, luxury waterfront realtor in St. Petersburg FL"` for the headshot. `alt="Waterfront homes along Shore Acres canal in St. Petersburg"` for neighborhood photos.

**8. Homepage and core page title tags are generic**
- **What:** Homepage title is "Home - Liane Jamason - Corcoran Dwellings". About page is "About - Liane Jamason - Corcoran Dwellings". Sell page is "Sell - Liane Jamason - Corcoran Dwellings". The Buy page still references old branding: "Buy A Home With Jamason Group".
- **Why it matters:** Title tags are the single most important on-page ranking factor. Leading with "Home" or "Sell" wastes the most valuable SEO real estate. Competitors like Avalon Group front-load keywords: "St Petersburg Realtor - Avalon Group Realty".
- **Fix:** Rewrite all title tags to front-load the target keyword. Homepage: "St. Petersburg Luxury & Waterfront Real Estate | Liane Jamason - Corcoran Dwellings". Sell: "Sell Your Home in St. Petersburg FL | Liane Jamason - Corcoran Dwellings".

**9. Off-topic pages diluting topical authority**
- **What:** Two pages — `/travel-draft/` (travel hacking guide) and `/lianes-credit-card-points-and-miles-hacking-tips/` (credit card points) — are indexed on a real estate website.
- **Why it matters:** Google evaluates topical authority at the site level. Off-topic content signals to Google that the site is not a focused authority on real estate. The March 2026 core update explicitly penalized thin/off-topic content.
- **Fix:** Either noindex both pages via Rank Math or remove them entirely. If Liane wants to keep the content, move it to a personal blog on a different domain.

**10. Old branding references ("Jamason Group") scattered across the site**
- **What:** The Buy page H1 reads "Buy A Home With Jamason Group". Title tag includes "Jamason Group". References to "Jamason Realty Group" and "Dwell Real Estate" (previous brokerage names) appear on older pages.
- **Why it matters:** Inconsistent branding confuses both users and search engines. It undermines the current Corcoran Dwellings brand positioning.
- **Fix:** Search-and-replace all instances of "Jamason Group", "Jamason Realty Group", and "Dwell Real Estate" with current branding. Update the Buy page H1 and title tag.

### Medium Priority Issues

**11. Schema markup is incomplete and partially incorrect**
- **What:** Homepage uses Article schema (should be WebPage). About page uses Article (should be AboutPage). No FAQPage schema on pages with FAQ sections. No Review/AggregateRating schema on the Reviews page. No BreadcrumbList schema. Author "varick" (developer name) exposed on older content.
- **Why it matters:** Schema markup is the highest-impact technical investment for AI citation and rich results in 2026. Missing review schema means no star ratings in SERPs. Missing FAQ schema means no rich results for question-based content.
- **Fix:** Correct schema types for Homepage and About page. Add FAQPage schema to blog posts with FAQ sections. Add AggregateRating schema to the Reviews page. Implement BreadcrumbList site-wide. Change author "varick" to "Liane Jamason" on all content.

**12. IDX subdomain breaking link equity flow**
- **What:** Property search pages live on `idx.lianejamason.com` rather than the main domain. Links from the main site to IDX search results are effectively external links for SEO purposes.
- **Why it matters:** Link equity from neighborhood pages pointing to IDX search results flows to the subdomain, not the main site. This is a known limitation of iHomefinder's default setup.
- **Fix:** Evaluate whether iHomefinder supports subdirectory installation (e.g., `lianejamason.com/search/`). If not, at minimum ensure the IDX pages link back to the main site prominently.

**13. Excessive script and stylesheet bloat**
- **What:** Pages load 36-63 JavaScript files and 11-17 stylesheets. The About page loads 61 scripts and 17 stylesheets. Blog posts load 63 scripts, 17 stylesheets, and 5 iframes. AdRoll retargeting pixels add 3+ tracking images per page.
- **Why it matters:** Script bloat directly impacts Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP). Pages in position 1 show a 10% higher CWV pass rate than position 9. Sites passing all CWV thresholds show 24% lower bounce rates.
- **Fix:** Audit for unused plugins and deactivate. Defer non-critical scripts. Combine and minify CSS/JS. Consider whether AdRoll is generating enough ROI to justify the performance cost.

**14. Inconsistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) data**
- **What:** Three different phone numbers appear across the site: 727-755-3325 (primary), 727-240-3636 (IDX pages), and 727-888-HOME (about/contact page). Two address formats: "1405 Dr MLK Jr St N" vs "1405 9th St N" (likely same location).
- **Why it matters:** NAP consistency is a core local SEO ranking factor. Inconsistent data across the site and external directories confuses Google and reduces local pack visibility.
- **Fix:** Standardize on one phone number and one address format. Update across all pages, the Google Business Profile, and all external directories.

**15. Blog pagination depth (94 pages)**
- **What:** The blog archive extends 94 pages deep. Older posts are 94 clicks from the homepage.
- **Why it matters:** Google evaluates crawl depth — content more than 3-4 clicks from the homepage is crawled less frequently and may be deindexed. 169+ older blog posts are effectively buried.
- **Fix:** Increase posts per page. Create topic hub/pillar pages that aggregate related posts. Implement category-based navigation in the main content area. Consider a "Best of" or "Most Popular" section.

### Missing Conversion Elements

**1. No email newsletter signup — anywhere.**
The site has 287 blog posts across 94 pages of archives. Not a single page offers an email signup, newsletter subscription, or "Get market updates" option. This is the single largest conversion opportunity being left on the table. Every blog visitor who reads a market update and leaves without subscribing is a lost lead.

**2. No lead magnets / downloadable content.**
Competitors offer downloadable guides ("Essential Guide to Buying Like a Pro" — Avalon Group), market report PDFs, and neighborhood guides in exchange for email addresses. Liane offers none. Potential lead magnets that align with her expertise:
- "The Waterfront Buyer's Checklist: What to Know Before Buying on the Water in St. Pete"
- "2026 St. Petersburg Market Report"
- "Your Home's Value: What Determines Price in Tampa Bay's Luxury Market"

**3. Broken Market Analysis / home valuation form.**
The form at `/market-analysis/` does not render. The meta description has a typo ("you home" instead of "your home"). The page has ~30 words of content with no explanation of what a CMA is or what the seller will receive. This is the #1 seller lead page on any real estate site, and it is non-functional.

**4. Sell page has zero conversion mechanism.**
The Sell page (`/sell/`) contains ~150 words of generic intro followed by 90+ testimonials with no structure, no headings, no CTAs, and no form. A visitor with high seller intent lands here and has no way to take the next step except scrolling through thousands of words of testimonials and then navigating elsewhere.

**5. Blog posts end with no next step.**
Every blog post — including the excellent 3,500-word "9 Mistakes Luxury Sellers Make" and the data-rich market updates — ends with only a comment form. There is no "Ready to sell? Get a free home evaluation" box, no "Subscribe for monthly market updates" form, no "Schedule a consultation" button.

**6. Reviews page has no CTA after 90+ testimonials.**
A visitor who reads through dozens of glowing reviews reaches the bottom of the page with no "Ready to work with Liane?" prompt, no contact form, no scheduling link.

| Review reader | Reviews page > nothing | NO NEXT STEP |

### Recommendations

1. **Fix the Market Analysis page immediately.** Verify and repair the form embed. Add 300+ words explaining what a CMA is, what the homeowner will receive, and why Liane's expertise makes her CMAs more accurate. Add testimonials from past sellers.

2. **Add email newsletter signup to every blog page.** Place a signup form in the blog sidebar, at the end of every post, and as a subtle bottom banner on all pages. Offer "Monthly St. Petersburg Market Updates" as the value proposition.

3. **Rewrite the Sell page as a true seller resource.** Replace the testimonial wall with: Liane's marketing approach (professional photography, staging guidance, pricing strategy), the selling process timeline, what makes Corcoran Dwellings different, 3-5 featured testimonials (not 90), and a prominent "Get Your Free Home Evaluation" CTA.

4. **Add end-of-post CTAs to every blog article.** A consistent box: "Ready to buy or sell in St. Petersburg? Schedule a free consultation with Liane" with a form or scheduling link.

5. **Add a CTA at the bottom of the Reviews page.** After 90+ positive reviews, the reader is primed. Add: "Ready to experience this level of service? Contact Liane today."

6. **Create at least one downloadable lead magnet.** Start with "The 2026 St. Petersburg Waterfront Market Report" — Liane has the data from her blog posts. Gate it behind an email capture form.

7. **Add neighborhood-specific CTAs to all neighborhood pages.** "Interested in Shore Acres? Schedule a private tour of available homes" with a contact form.
