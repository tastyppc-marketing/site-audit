Park City real estate investment still makes sense in 2026, but only if you underwrite it like a business. The buyers who win here are not guessing. They are running disciplined assumptions on demand, supply, regulations, seasonality, and exit liquidity.

The short version: Park City has durable drivers that support long-term value, including constrained land supply, strong destination demand, high-income buyer inflow, and major resort investment. ## Appreciation Case: Why Park City Holds Value

### Supply Constraints Are Structural

Park City is not a market where supply can expand without friction. Topography, protected land, entitlement complexity, and infrastructure realities naturally limit how quickly new inventory can come online in premium locations. Limited high-quality supply is one of the strongest long-term support mechanisms for property values.

This is especially important in ski-adjacent and amenity-rich corridors where replacement inventory is expensive and slow to deliver.

### Demand Is Deep and Diversified

Demand is not coming from one buyer profile. Park City attracts full-time relocations, second-home buyers, lifestyle retirees, and investors seeking both personal use and income offset. That diversified demand base helps reduce dependency on any single economic cohort.

Local market reporting entering 2026 also showed resilient sales volume and meaningful pricing power in key segments, even after the post-pandemic normalization phase.

## Rental Income Potential: Strong, But Market-Specific

### Peak-Season Revenue Can Be Excellent

Well-positioned assets can generate strong short-term rental performance during ski season, holidays, and event-heavy windows. Properties with true proximity to lifts, trails, dining, and transportation typically maintain the strongest occupancy profile across seasons.

In Park City, rental strategy is highly product-dependent. A median condo in a non-prime location will not perform like a premium resort unit with superior amenities and access. Underwrite accordingly.

### Shoulder Seasons and Operating Costs Matter

The biggest investment mistake is modeling only peak-season nightly rates. You need a full-year model that includes shoulder-season occupancy, cleaning and turnover expense, management fees, reserve capital, insurance, and HOA structure. Net income, not gross revenue, is what determines asset performance.

For investors balancing use and income, define your personal-use calendar first so your rental assumptions are realistic from day one.

## Regulation in 2026: Know the Rules Before You Buy

### Park City and Summit County Licensing Requirements

If you plan to rent short term, licensing and zoning are not optional details. Park City requires a nightly rental license for rentals under 30 days where zoning allows it, and Summit County requires nightly rental licensing in unincorporated areas for short-term operations.

The details matter at the property level: zoning designation, HOA constraints, permit process, inspection requirements, and operational compliance timeline all affect your timeline to revenue.

### Enforcement Is Tightening, Not Loosening

Utah's 2025 updates to municipal and county short-term rental statutes clarified how jurisdictions can use listing information with additional supporting evidence and pursue compliance actions. In practical terms, investors should expect more structured enforcement and less tolerance for informal operation.

Treat compliance as part of acquisition diligence, not a post-close task. The right way to buy is to verify legal use before you finalize your offer strategy.

## Demand Catalysts: East Village and the Wasatch Back Growth Arc

### Deer Valley East Village Is a Major Signal

Deer Valley's Expanded Excellence rollout materially changed the long-term demand conversation. The resort has added substantial terrain and lift capacity since late 2024, including the East Village portal and major on-mountain infrastructure upgrades. That scale of investment supports sustained visibility and buyer interest across the Wasatch Back.

Properties with strong access to Deer Valley and surrounding resort corridors are positioned to benefit from this multi-year growth cycle.

### Broader Regional Momentum

Park City also benefits from regional strength: access to Salt Lake City International Airport, year-round recreation, and growing lifestyle migration into mountain markets. Investors are not just buying a winter destination anymore; they are buying four-season utility, which tends to support broader demand durability.

Jordanelle-area growth and summer demand around the Deer Valley Music Festival further reinforce four-season occupancy potential.

Neighborhood selection still matters most. For many investors, shortlists typically include [Deer Valley area opportunities](/communities/deer-valley/), select product in [Canyons Village](/communities/canyons-village/), and expansion-linked opportunities in the [Heber Valley corridor](/communities/heber-valley/).

## Risk Management: How to Invest with Precision

### Underwrite Three Exit Paths

Every deal should pencil under three scenarios: hold for appreciation, hold for income, and resale within a shorter cycle. If the property only works under one optimistic assumption set, it is not an investment-grade acquisition.

I recommend conservative rent assumptions, realistic capex reserves, and a downside case where days on market stretches longer than expected.

### Buy Quality, Not Just Narrative

In this market, construction quality can preserve or destroy returns. Poor envelope design, deferred maintenance, underperforming systems, or weak finish durability can drain NOI and compress resale appeal. Strong quality in the right location remains the most reliable hedge.

Investment success in Park City comes from discipline: legal clarity, realistic underwriting, and asset-level quality control.

It can be, especially for properties in high-demand zones with strong year-round appeal and clear short-term rental compliance. The best-performing assets usually combine location, amenity access, and durable construction quality. Returns vary widely, so conservative underwriting is essential.

### Can you rent out your home in Park City?

Yes, in many cases, but only where zoning and local rules allow it. Owners typically need to comply with city or county licensing requirements for rentals under 30 days. Always confirm zoning, HOA rules, and permit requirements before you buy.

### What are the short-term rental rules in Park City?

Rules depend on whether the property is inside Park City limits or in unincorporated Summit County, plus the specific zoning district. ### Is Park City a good place to invest in real estate?
