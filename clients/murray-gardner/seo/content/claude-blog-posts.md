# Park City Real Estate Market Report: Spring 2026

Spring is when the Park City real estate market shifts gears. Ski season winds down, inventory begins to move, and buyers who spent the winter watching listings suddenly get serious. If you are thinking about buying or selling in the Park City area this spring, here is what the data shows — and what it means for you.

## Where Prices Stand Heading Into Spring 2026

The Park City market has held its ground through the post-pandemic correction that reshaped markets across the Mountain West. Median home prices in Park City proper continue to track in the $1.8 million to $2.2 million range for single-family homes, with the luxury tier — properties above $3 million — remaining exceptionally active. Condos and townhomes, which offer the market's most accessible entry points, are generally ranging from $650,000 to $1.4 million depending on location and whether the unit carries ski-in/ski-out access.

Deer Valley remains its own category. Entry-level properties in [Deer Crest](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/deer-crest/) and [Empire Pass](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/empire-pass/) still command a premium that puts them well above Park City averages, with many homes trading between $4 million and $12 million. The ongoing Deer Valley East Village expansion — the largest ski resort expansion in North American history — continues to draw investor and second-home buyer attention to the entire Deer Valley corridor.

Price per square foot across the broader market ranges from approximately $700 on the lower end in areas like Kimball Junction and [Jeremy Ranch](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/jeremy-ranch/) to $1,500 and above in slope-side communities at Empire Pass.

## Inventory: Still Below Historical Norms

Inventory remains the defining feature of this market. New listings in Summit County continue to come in below the pre-2020 baseline, which keeps competition meaningful even in a market that has moderated from its 2021-2022 frenzy. Well-priced properties in desirable communities — [Old Town](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/old-town/), Park Meadows, [Glenwild](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/glenwild/) — are still moving within 30 to 60 days. Overpriced listings are sitting longer, sometimes 90 to 120 days, which represents a significant shift from the multiple-offer environment of recent years.

That is actually good news for buyers. The era of waiving every contingency and bidding 15% over asking is largely behind us. Buyers now have room to negotiate — but do not mistake a more balanced market for a buyer's market. The underlying demand drivers in Park City have not changed.

## What Is Driving Demand in 2026

Park City's demand fundamentals remain as strong as any resort market in the country:

**Remote work is permanent.** The Wasatch Back has become a legitimate primary residence for high-earning remote workers from California, Texas, and the Pacific Northwest. The commute to Salt Lake City International Airport runs about 35 to 45 minutes from most of Park City, making it viable even for professionals who travel frequently.

**Deer Valley expansion is a catalyst.** The East Village development is adding terrain, a new village center, and significant resort infrastructure. Properties that can be marketed with Deer Valley access — including those in [Heber Valley](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/heber-valley/) and the Jordanelle corridor — have benefited from spillover demand.

**Supply is structurally constrained.** Park City is geographically hemmed in by mountains, ski resort boundaries, and protected open space. New construction exists but is limited relative to demand. That structural scarcity supports long-term values.

**Luxury buyers remain cash-heavy.** Approximately 50% of transactions above $2 million in the Park City market are cash purchases. This insulates the upper tier from interest rate volatility in a way that most markets cannot claim.

## The Sellers' Calculus This Spring

If you are considering selling in Spring 2026, timing and pricing are everything. Spring brings a surge of new listings — typically March through May — which means more competition for seller attention. Properties that come to market well-prepared and accurately priced in early spring capture the motivated buyers who have been watching all winter.

The single most common seller mistake right now is anchoring to 2022 peak prices. The market has recalibrated. Comparative market analysis using recent 2025 and early 2026 comparable sales will give you the most accurate picture of where your property actually stands. I offer a [free market analysis](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/sellers/free-market-analysis/) for homeowners considering a sale — this is not a Zestimate, it is a detailed look at what your specific property would command in today's market.

## The Buyers' Window

For buyers, spring 2026 represents one of the better entry environments in several years. Rates have stabilized. Inventory is slowly improving. And sellers, who watched the frenzied peak years pass, are generally more realistic about pricing than they were 18 months ago.

The communities I would watch most closely this spring: [Promontory](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/promontory/) continues to add new construction, giving buyers the rare combination of a built-out community and brand-new product. The Jordanelle area offers meaningful value relative to Deer Valley proper, with several projects close to the new East Village access. And Jeremy Ranch continues to attract families who want Park City proximity with slightly more breathing room on price.

If you are ready to search current listings, [browse homes for sale in Park City](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/) with real-time MLS data across every neighborhood.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Park City Real Estate Market Spring 2026

**Is the Park City real estate market going up or down in 2026?**
The Park City market has stabilized after the post-pandemic correction and is showing modest appreciation in most segments. The luxury tier above $3 million remains active. Entry-level condos and homes in the $600,000 to $1.2 million range are moving steadily. The days of double-digit annual appreciation are behind us, but the underlying demand drivers — resort lifestyle, limited supply, remote work migration — continue to support values.

**Is Park City a buyers or sellers market right now?**
It is best described as a balanced-to-slightly-sellers market in desirable locations. Well-priced homes in Old Town, Park Meadows, and Deer Valley are still attracting multiple qualified buyers. However, overpriced listings are sitting, and buyers have more negotiating leverage than at any point since 2019. Proper pricing and preparation remain the critical variables for sellers.

**What is the average price per square foot in Park City?**
Price per square foot varies widely by location and property type. Slope-side properties at Empire Pass and Deer Crest range from $1,200 to $1,800 per square foot. Mid-mountain communities like Park Meadows and Old Town generally run $700 to $1,100. More suburban areas like Jeremy Ranch and Kimball Junction typically fall in the $500 to $750 range.

**What are the real estate trends in Park City for 2026?**
The biggest trend is the Deer Valley East Village expansion and its ripple effect on the entire Jordanelle and Heber Valley corridor. Second, the continued migration of California and out-of-state buyers seeking primary or secondary residences. Third, new construction in Promontory, Tuhaye, and the Jordanelle rim communities is adding inventory in the luxury tier. And fourth, short-term rental regulations in Park City proper are increasingly steering investment-focused buyers toward Heber City and Midway, which have more permissive STR rules.

# The Best Neighborhoods in Park City, Utah (2026 Guide)

One of the first questions every buyer asks me is some version of: "Which neighborhood is right for us?" It is a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends. Park City's communities are genuinely different from one another — different lifestyles, different price points, different relationships to the ski resorts, and different appeal depending on whether you want walkability, acreage, a ski locker in your garage, or a fairway out the back door.

This guide covers the neighborhoods I get asked about most, what makes each one distinct, and the price ranges you should expect heading into 2026.

## Old Town: The Historic Heart of Park City

If you want to walk out your door and be on Main Street in five minutes, [Old Town](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/old-town/) is the only option. This is Park City's original mining-era neighborhood, and it has evolved into the most walkable, character-rich community in the area. Victorian miners' cottages sit alongside modern reconstructions and ski-in/ski-out condos with direct access to Park City Mountain Resort.

Old Town appeals to buyers who want to be in the middle of everything — Sundance Film Festival, Main Street restaurants, the resort, the Rail Trail. Entry-level condos start around $650,000. Single-family homes range from about $1.2 million for a smaller cottage up to $4 million and beyond for larger ski-access properties. Short-term rental (STR) opportunities are significant here, though the city's STR licensing rules require careful navigation.

## Park Meadows: Established, Family-Friendly, and Central

Park Meadows is the neighborhood that locals call home. Located between Old Town and the school complex, it offers large lots, mature trees, and proximity to everything without the density of the historic district. You will find single-family homes predominantly, ranging from approximately $1.4 million to $3.5 million depending on size, views, and proximity to the Park Meadows Country Club golf course.

Families favor Park Meadows for its walkability to Park City High School, the Recreation Center, and the trail network. It is also one of the few neighborhoods where you can still find a half-acre lot within the city limits. This is the community I recommend most consistently to buyers who want a permanent Park City residence rather than a ski vacation property.

## Deer Valley Communities: Empire Pass, Deer Crest, and Upper Deer Valley

Deer Valley is not one neighborhood — it is a collection of communities that share Deer Valley Resort access and a consistently premium price point. I cover several of these in dedicated guides, but here is the overview:

[Empire Pass](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/empire-pass/) sits at the top of the mountain and includes Montage Deer Valley and Talisker Tower, along with private ski-in/ski-out residences ranging from $3 million to well above $10 million. If ski access is the priority and price is secondary, Empire Pass is the pinnacle.

[Deer Crest](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/deer-crest/) is a private gated enclave above Deer Valley with some of the most dramatic mountain views in the area. The St. Regis Deer Valley's iconic funicular serves this community. Homes here are estate-scale, typically in the $5 million to $15 million range.

Upper and Lower Deer Valley sit closer to the resort base and offer a range of condos and single-family homes, with entry points somewhat lower than Empire Pass — think $1.5 million to $5 million for most properties.

## Jeremy Ranch: Space, Value, and an Easy Commute

[Jeremy Ranch](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/jeremy-ranch/) is the community that surprises buyers who come to it expecting a lesser alternative to Park City proper. What they find instead is a golf course community with generous lot sizes, relatively newer construction, and prices that are meaningfully lower than Old Town or Park Meadows — typically $900,000 to $2.5 million for single-family homes.

Jeremy Ranch sits along I-80, which makes Salt Lake City accessible in about 25 minutes. For buyers who need to be in the city regularly but want a mountain lifestyle, this location is hard to beat. The Jeremy Ranch Golf & Country Club anchors the community, and the trail network along Jeremy Creek is a genuine amenity.

## Promontory: The Amenity-Rich Golf and Ranch Community

[Promontory](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/promontory/) operates on a different scale from every other Park City neighborhood. This 6,400-acre private club community north of Park City features 36 holes of golf (two Pete Dye-designed courses), an equestrian center, multiple pools, ski shuttles, and a ski beach at Park City Mountain Resort. The community is still actively developing, which means buyers can choose from resale homes or new construction.

Price ranges vary: condos and smaller homes start around $1.2 million, while larger custom homes on prime lots push $5 million and beyond. The annual Club membership fee adds to the carrying cost, but for buyers who want resort-scale amenities with a private community feel, Promontory is unmatched in the Park City market.

## Glenwild: Park City's Most Exclusive Golf Community

[Glenwild](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/glenwild/) is intentionally kept small. This gated community on the north side of Park City has just 150 homesites surrounding the Glenwild Golf Club, ranked among the top 100 courses in the country. Membership is restricted to residents, which is by design. Homes here range from approximately $2.5 million to $6 million for finished properties, with lots available for custom builds.

If the appeal of Promontory is its scale and range of amenities, the appeal of Glenwild is its exclusivity and restraint. The community is quiet, architecturally consistent, and close enough to I-80 to make Salt Lake City accessible.

## Heber Valley: The Adjacent Market That Many Buyers Overlook

I have written extensively about [Heber Valley](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/heber-valley/), and I will keep repeating it: this is the market where Park City buyers find the most value per square foot in the greater area. Heber City, Midway, and the surrounding communities offer large lots, mountain views, and significantly lower price points — single-family homes from the high $400,000s to $2 million plus — with a 20-minute drive to Park City and direct access to the Jordanelle Reservoir and the Deer Valley East Village expansion.

The Tuhaye and Victory Ranch golf communities in Heber Valley represent the luxury tier here, offering golf, private fishing, and resort amenities at prices well below the Park City side of the Wasatch.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Park City Neighborhoods

**What is the best neighborhood to live in Park City?**
It depends entirely on your lifestyle priorities. For walkability and resort access, Old Town is the answer. For family living and community, Park Meadows. For maximum ski access and luxury, Empire Pass or Deer Crest. For value and space, Jeremy Ranch or Heber Valley. There is no single "best" — there is best for you.

**Where do locals live in Park City?**
Long-term Park City residents tend to cluster in Park Meadows, Jeremy Ranch, Pinebrook, and Silver Springs. These neighborhoods offer more space per dollar, proximity to the schools, and a quieter residential feel compared to Old Town or the ski resort communities. Many locals also live in the Snyderville Basin — the area between Park City and Kimball Junction — where newer housing has been built over the last two decades.

**What are the most exclusive neighborhoods in Park City?**
The Colony at White Pine Canyon, Deer Crest, and Empire Pass represent the apex of Park City exclusivity. The Colony is a private ski community on 4,600 acres where properties rarely sell below $5 million. Deer Crest and Empire Pass are Deer Valley's top tier, with properties ranging from $4 million into the eight-figure range.

**What is the difference between Park City and Deer Valley?**
Park City Mountain Resort and Deer Valley Resort are two separate ski resorts with different character. Park City Mountain allows snowboarders; Deer Valley is skiers-only and is consistently ranked among the top ski resorts in North America for grooming and service. Deer Valley neighborhoods (Empire Pass, Deer Crest, Upper and Lower Deer Valley) sit closer to Deer Valley Resort, while neighborhoods like Old Town, Park Meadows, and Jeremy Ranch are oriented toward Park City Mountain or are ski-resort-neutral.

# Is Park City Real Estate a Good Investment? What the Data Says

People ask me this constantly, and I will give you the same answer I give clients in my office: Park City real estate is a good investment for the right buyer with the right property in the right location. That non-answer frustrates people until I explain what it actually means — so let me do that here with real numbers and real context.

I spent 20 years as an F/A-18 pilot and Top Gun instructor before building luxury homes in Park City and then pivoting to real estate. In all three careers, the discipline was the same: evaluate the data honestly, identify the risk factors, make the decision with clear eyes. That is how I approach investment questions for clients.

## The Case For Park City as an Investment Market

Let me start with the structural advantages, because they are real and durable.

**Supply is genuinely constrained.** Park City is surrounded by ski resort boundaries, protected open space, and topography that limits where you can build. The Snyderville Basin has absorbed much of the new construction over the past two decades, but even that is increasingly built out. When supply is structurally limited and demand is growing, long-term appreciation has a natural floor.

**Demand is national, not local.** This is not a market where one local industry employs most of the buyers. Park City draws from California, Texas, New York, and increasingly international markets. High-net-worth buyers relocating or seeking a second home are not particularly sensitive to regional economic cycles. That diversification of demand is a stabilizer.

**The Deer Valley expansion is a genuine catalyst.** The Deer Valley East Village development — which will roughly double the resort's skiable terrain — is the most significant infrastructure investment in the Park City market in a generation. Properties that benefit from this expansion, particularly in the Jordanelle corridor and [Heber Valley](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/heber-valley/), are positioned for outsized appreciation as the project comes online over the next several years.

**Long-term appreciation has been consistent.** Despite the volatility of the 2020-2022 run-up and subsequent correction, Park City home values over a 10 or 20-year horizon have performed in line with or better than most comparable resort markets. The caveat: this applies to the market broadly, not to every individual property.

## Short-Term Rental Investment: What the Numbers Actually Look Like

The most common investment thesis I hear is: buy a condo or home in Park City, rent it short-term on Airbnb or VRBO, and let the rental income offset ownership costs. Here is what that actually looks like in practice.

A well-located Old Town condo purchased at $800,000 to $1.0 million with ski access can generate gross rental income of $60,000 to $100,000 annually in a strong year — which includes peak ski season (January through March), Sundance Film Festival (January), and the summer months. After platform fees, property management (typically 20-30% of gross), cleaning, maintenance, and HOA dues, net income before debt service often runs $35,000 to $60,000.

On an $800,000 property with a $600,000 mortgage at current rates, that net income covers a meaningful portion of carrying costs but likely does not fully offset them in year one. The investment calculus works when you factor in appreciation, equity paydown, and your own personal use of the property.

Properties in [Empire Pass](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/empire-pass/) and [Deer Crest](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/deer-crest/) at the $3 million to $6 million level can achieve gross rental yields of $150,000 to $300,000 annually, but the operating costs scale accordingly.

## The Short-Term Rental Regulatory Landscape: Know Before You Buy

This is where I slow investors down if they have not done their homework. Park City proper has strict short-term rental licensing rules. STR licenses are geographically limited, and not every property or zone qualifies. If you are purchasing primarily as an investment property to rent short-term, you must verify the property's STR eligibility before closing.

The better news for investors: Heber City and Midway currently operate under more permissive STR regulations. Combined with lower purchase prices, this makes [Heber Valley](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/heber-valley/) increasingly attractive for investment-focused buyers who want income generation as the primary objective.

I have a detailed guide to Park City and Heber Valley STR rules on the blog if you want the full regulatory picture.

## The Case Against Treating Park City Real Estate as Pure Investment

I would be doing you a disservice if I only made the bull case. Here are the real risks.

**The market is illiquid at the top.** A $5 million home in Deer Crest or [The Colony](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/the-colony/) has a small buyer pool. If you need to sell in a down market or under time pressure, you are at the mercy of how many qualified buyers exist in that particular moment. This is not a market where you call your agent Monday and have a contract by Friday at the upper end.

**Carrying costs are high.** HOA dues in Promontory and Deer Valley communities run $500 to $2,000 per month. Property taxes in Summit County are moderate but meaningful on high-value properties. Add insurance, maintenance, and potential club membership fees, and the cost of ownership is substantial.

**The STR market is competitive.** The number of professionally managed short-term rental properties in Park City has grown significantly over the past five years. Peak weeks will still perform, but mid-season and shoulder-season occupancy requires active management and competitive pricing.

**External risk factors exist.** A significant economic downturn, major changes to ski resort operations, or shifts in remote work trends could all affect demand. These are low-probability risks, but they are real.

## What I Tell Clients Who Are Serious About Investment

If your primary motivation is financial return, I push people toward the Jordanelle and Heber Valley corridor — good fundamentals, more permissive STR environment, Deer Valley East Village upside, and lower entry cost. If you want a property that you will personally enjoy and that may appreciate over time, the [Promontory](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/promontory/) community and Old Town both offer a combination of lifestyle value and long-term appreciation potential.

Start by [searching current investment-friendly listings](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/) across every Park City community, and then let's talk through the specific numbers for any property you're seriously considering.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Park City Investment Property

**Is Park City a good investment for rental property?**
For the right property in the right location, yes. Old Town condos with ski access and STR licenses have demonstrated consistent rental performance over time. The Heber Valley and Jordanelle areas offer better entry prices and more permissive STR rules for pure income-focused investment. The key variables are STR license eligibility, carrying costs, and your personal use expectations.

**What are the short-term rental rules in Park City?**
Park City proper has a licensing system that limits STR permits geographically and by property type. Not all properties qualify, and licenses are required for any rental under 30 days. Heber City and Midway currently operate under county rules that are more permissive. Always verify STR eligibility before purchasing any property with investment intent.

**What is the ROI on Park City rental properties?**
Gross rental yields on well-located Park City condos typically run 8-12% of purchase price annually in strong rental seasons. After operating expenses (management, cleaning, HOA, maintenance), net yields before debt service are generally 4-7%. Total return, including appreciation, has historically been stronger than yield alone suggests — but past performance does not guarantee future results.

**Is Park City a good place to invest in real estate long-term?**
The structural case is strong: constrained supply, growing national demand, a world-class ski resort expanding its footprint, and continued remote work migration. Long-term, Park City real estate has held value well through market cycles. The risks — illiquidity at the top, high carrying costs, competitive STR market — are real but manageable with the right property and strategy.

# Park City Luxury Ski Homes: Communities, Prices, and What to Expect

Before I became a real estate agent, I built homes. Before that, I flew F/A-18 fighter jets and instructed at Top Gun. Those two careers — one requiring an eye for structural integrity, design quality, and construction standards; the other demanding precision decision-making under pressure — shaped how I think about luxury properties. When I walk through a ski home with a client, I am not just looking at the views and the finishes. I am looking at the radiant floor installation, the snow-load engineering, the ski room layout, the mechanical systems. I see what most agents do not.

That perspective is what this guide is built on. Here is what you need to know about Park City's luxury ski home communities in 2026.

## What "Ski-In/Ski-Out" Actually Means in Park City

The term gets used loosely in listing descriptions, so let me be precise. True ski-in/ski-out means you click into your bindings at your front door and ski to the lift — and reverse that at the end of the day. In Park City, only a handful of communities genuinely qualify.

Slope-side means you are close to the resort — perhaps a short walk or a quick groomer shuttle — but not directly connected. Many properties marketed as "ski access" fall into this category. It is not a bad option, but it is a different product, and the price difference between true ski-in/ski-out and slope-side can be substantial.

## Empire Pass: The Summit of Deer Valley Luxury

[Empire Pass](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/empire-pass/) is where Deer Valley's altitude meets its most rarefied residential real estate. Situated at the top of the mountain, Empire Pass includes Montage Deer Valley — one of the premier ski-in/ski-out resort properties in North America — as well as Talisker Tower and a collection of private single-family residences that sit directly on or adjacent to groomed Deer Valley terrain.

From a builder's perspective, Empire Pass properties represent some of the most demanding construction environments in Park City. The altitude, wind exposure, and snow loads at this elevation require engineering that separates competent construction from exceptional construction. When I evaluate homes here, the quality of the snow management systems, the insulation specifications, and the ski room design tell me a lot about how the builder thought about the property's long-term performance.

Price range: Condos from approximately $2.5 million to $6 million. Single-family ski-in/ski-out homes from $5 million to well above $15 million. Montage branded residences carry a premium for the hotel services and brand association.

## Deer Crest: Gated Privacy Above Deer Valley

[Deer Crest](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/deer-crest/) is a private gated community perched above the Deer Valley base area, accessible via a private road that most Park City residents do not know exists. The St. Regis Deer Valley — which operates the famous funicular that takes guests from the base to the resort mid-mountain — anchors the community and provides hotel services to residences and St. Regis fractional ownership units.

What distinguishes Deer Crest from Empire Pass is its orientation: you are above the resort looking out over the Park City valley, with some of the most dramatic sight lines in the area. The ski access via the St. Regis gondola is convenient and unique, though it is a different experience from having a groomed run at your back door.

Construction quality in Deer Crest is consistently high. The gate and association structure ensure that properties are maintained to a standard that protects values. For buyers who want privacy, estate-scale properties, and Deer Valley access in a setting that feels more removed from the resort's activity, Deer Crest is the answer.

Price range: $5 million to $15 million and above for single-family residences. Fractional interests available through St. Regis at lower entry points.

## The Colony at White Pine Canyon: Park City's Most Private Ski Community

[The Colony](https://www.gardnergrouprealtors.com/communities/the-colony/) is in a category by itself. This 4,600-acre private community on the back side of Park City Mountain Resort is the largest private ski-in/ski-out community in the United States. There are just over 100 homesites on this massive terrain, each on lots ranging from 5 to 100 acres.

The Colony's ski access is via Park City Mountain Resort, with private ski-in/ski-out runs connecting each home to the resort trail system. The wildlife — moose, deer, mountain lion, elk — that passes through these properties is not incidental; it is one of the primary selling points. This is what buyers mean when they say they want privacy.

Properties here rarely sell below $5 million, and the most significant estates push into the $20 million and above range. The combination of ski access, acreage, privacy, and proximity to Park City makes The Colony the most difficult to replicate product in the market. There is simply no other community like it in Utah.

## Canyons Village: Ski Access with a Village Atmosphere

The Canyons Village area at Park City Mountain Resort offers a different flavor of ski property. Here, ski-in/ski-out access is organized around the village center — multiple lifts, pedestrian-scale amenities, restaurants and retail at the base. Products range from hotel-branded residences (Grand Summit, Sundial Lodge, Silverado) to private condos and townhomes.

The price point is generally more accessible than Deer Valley communities, with serious ski-access condos starting in the $1.2 million range and going up from there. For buyers who want resort-integrated living at Park City Mountain rather than the quieter, more estate-oriented environment of Deer Valley, Canyons Village is the primary option.

## What to Look For When Buying a Ski Home: The Builder's Checklist

Having built homes in Park City, I evaluate ski properties differently than most agents. Here are the items I look at closely on every luxury ski home walkthrough:

**Ski room design.** A proper ski room has bench seating, boot dryers, equipment storage, a floor drain, and heating. An afterthought ski room has a peg on the wall and a wet floor. The ski room tells you whether the builder was designing for a mountain lifestyle or just checking a box.

**Snow management.** At altitude in Park City, you can receive 300+ inches of snow annually. Roof pitch, heated gutters, heated driveways and entry walks, and structural snow load capacity matter in ways that buyers from flat-weather climates do not immediately consider.

**Mechanical systems.** Radiant heat in the floors and driveway is the standard in well-built ski homes, not a luxury upgrade. If a home in this price range is running forced air only, ask why.

**Orientation and solar exposure.** South-facing properties shed snow naturally and stay warmer. North-facing ski homes at altitude can be dark and cold. This affects both livability and long-term maintenance costs.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Luxury Ski Homes in Park City

**What are the best ski-in/ski-out communities in Park City?**
The Colony at White Pine Canyon, Empire Pass, and Deer Crest are the three communities that offer genuine ski-in/ski-out access at the luxury tier. Canyons Village offers ski-in/ski-out access in a more village-oriented setting at somewhat lower price points. Each has a different character and a different relationship to the mountain — the right choice depends on which resort you prefer, how much privacy you want, and your price range.

**What is the most expensive home in Park City?**
The upper end of the Park City market trades in the $20 million to $30 million range for the most significant estate properties in The Colony and Deer Crest. Montage and St. Regis branded residences in the $10 million to $20 million range represent the trophy end of the resort-integrated market. The all-time top sale in the Park City area has exceeded $30 million for a private compound.

**Are Deer Valley condos a good investment?**
Deer Valley resort properties have demonstrated strong long-term appreciation and, for properties with STR licenses, meaningful rental income potential. The key factors are STR eligibility (not all Deer Valley condos qualify), carrying costs including HOA and club fees, and your personal use expectations. Empire Pass and Lower Deer Valley condos with hotel services backing have shown particularly stable performance.

**Can you ski-in ski-out in Park City year-round?**
Ski-in/ski-out access is a winter-season feature. In summer, these same properties access the mountain trail networks for hiking and mountain biking. Deer Valley and Park City Mountain Resort both operate summer lift-served mountain biking and hiking programs. The summer access adds year-round utility to ski properties but does not replicate the winter experience.
