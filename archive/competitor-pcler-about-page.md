## 8. Schema Markup

### PCLER About Page -- 2 Schema Blocks

**Schema 1: LocalBusiness**
```json
{
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "The Fisher Group - Park City Realtors",
  "telephone": "(435) 962-0192",
  "address": {
    "streetAddress": "1750 Park Avenue",
    "addressLocality": "Park City",
    "addressRegion": "UT",
    "postalCode": "84060"
  }
}
```

**Schema 2: Product with AggregateRating** (from Elfsight Google Reviews widget)
```json
{
  "@type": "Product",
  "name": "Park City Utah Realtors Ben Fisher Park City Real Estate",
  "aggregateRating": {
    "@type": "AggregateRating",
    "ratingValue": 5,
    "reviewCount": 47,
    "bestRating": 5
  },
  "review": {
    "@type": "Review",
    "reviewBody": "Ben Fisher delivers an elevated level of service...",
    "author": { "name": "Handy Home Services" },
    "reviewRating": { "ratingValue": 5 }
  }
}
```

**Note:** Using `Product` type for a real estate agent is technically a schema misuse -- Google's guidelines specify `RealEstateAgent` or `LocalBusiness`. However, Google currently renders the star rating rich snippet for Product schema, which is likely why they chose it. This is a gray-hat tactic that could be penalized if Google tightens enforcement.

### Tisha's About Page
- **ZERO schema markup** on the About page

## 10. What Makes PCLER's About Page Effective

### Strengths (What They Do Well)

1. **Story-driven bio that feels human.** Ben talks about mountain biking, skiing, his kids, camping. This is not corporate copy -- it reads like a real person wrote it.

2. **Specific achievement anchors.** "$25 million Deer Valley sale" and "Co-Founder of Christie's PC" are memorable, concrete, and verifiable. They give prospects something to repeat when telling friends.

3. **Live Google Reviews on-page.** This is the single highest-impact trust element. Prospects don't have to leave the page to see what others say. The Elfsight widget pulls reviews dynamically, so new reviews appear automatically.

4. **"As Featured In" media logos.** Even though the articles are tangentially related (pandemic ski towns, resort home sales), the Vogue and WSJ logos create instant credibility association.

5. **Active listings embedded on the About page.** This is clever -- it says "I'm not just telling you I sell luxury homes, here are six of them right now." It turns the About page into a portfolio page.

6. **Contact form right on the page.** Zero friction. Read the bio, see the reviews, fill out the form. No navigating away.

7. **Schema markup generating star ratings in Google results.** Their About page can show "5.0 stars / 47 reviews" in Google search results. That dramatically increases click-through rate.

8. **Multi-language support.** 11 languages signals to international buyers (especially Chinese, Korean, Japanese) that this team is equipped to serve them.

### Weaknesses (Where They Fall Short)

1. **No video.** A 60-90 second "Meet Ben & Stan" video would massively increase engagement and time on page. This is a clear gap.

2. **Stan Fisher's bio is almost invisible.** The page is heavily focused on Ben. Stan is mentioned in the opening line but gets no dedicated paragraph. For a "father-son team" brand, this feels unbalanced.

3. **No specific awards listed.** They claim "top agent" status but don't list actual award names (e.g., "Real Trends America's Best," "Utah Business Top 500," etc.).

4. **Zach is mentioned in reviews but has no bio.** Multiple reviewers mention "Ben and Zach" but there's no information about who Zach is.

5. **The Product schema for reviews is technically incorrect.** Should be `RealEstateAgent` or `LocalBusiness` with reviews. Google could penalize this.

6. **Meta title is keyword-stuffed.** "Park City Utah Realtors Ben Fisher Park City Real Estate" repeats "Park City" twice and reads awkwardly.

7. **No FAQ section.** For 2026 AI Overview optimization, FAQ content on the About page would help them appear in AI-generated answers about "best Park City realtors."

8. **No community involvement or charity mentions.** Nothing about local organizations, events, or giving back. This hurts local trust signals.
