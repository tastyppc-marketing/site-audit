# Park City Real Estate Market Update — Spring 2026

*By Tisha Digman, Summit Sotheby's International Realty*

Every year, the transition out of ski season marks a defining moment in the Park City real estate market. Listings come to life, buyers who spent the winter watching from the sidelines begin making moves, and the pace of activity accelerates into one of the most dynamic periods in the local real estate calendar. Spring 2026 is shaping up to be no different — and if you are thinking about buying or selling in Park City this year, here is what the market looks like right now.

## Where Inventory Stands in Early 2026

Park City has operated under constrained inventory conditions for years, and 2026 continues that pattern. Active listings across Summit County remain well below the historical averages of the mid-2010s. New construction has added some supply — particularly in the Jordanelle corridor and around the Deer Valley East Village expansion area — but not enough to significantly shift the buyer/seller dynamic in established neighborhoods like Old Town, Deer Valley, and Promontory.

What does this mean for buyers? Competition. Well-priced homes in desirable communities are still moving quickly, often receiving multiple offers within the first two weeks of listing. Buyers who are prepared — pre-approved, working with a local agent, and clear on their priorities — are the ones successfully closing in this environment.

What does it mean for sellers? Leverage. A well-prepared and well-priced home in a quality neighborhood still has the upper hand. The sellers who are seeing the best results are those who invest in professional photography, strategic pricing, and the kind of marketing reach that only a Sotheby's International Realty platform provides.

## Price Trends: Where Values Are Moving

The luxury segment — properties above $3 million — has shown continued strength across Park City and the broader Summit County area. Deer Valley, Empire Pass, and The Colony at White Pine Canyon continue to attract domestic and international buyers who view Park City real estate as both a lifestyle asset and a long-term investment.

The mid-market ($1M–$3M) has seen more movement in days on market compared to the frenzied conditions of 2022–2023, but pricing has held remarkably well given the moderated pace. Sellers pricing within 5% of comparable sales are still achieving strong outcomes.

Condominiums in Old Town and around the base of Park City Mountain Resort are an area to watch in spring 2026. As Deer Valley East Village development progresses and the combined ski resort narrative grows, proximity to both resorts is becoming a more prominent value driver.

## The Deer Valley East Village Effect

No discussion of the Park City real estate market in 2026 is complete without addressing the Deer Valley East Village expansion. This development — connecting Deer Valley's existing terrain with an entirely new resort village — is the largest ski resort expansion in Park City's history and a meaningful catalyst for real estate values throughout the eastern Park City corridor.

Early residential development in the East Village area has attracted significant buyer interest, with pre-construction opportunities in particular capturing buyers who want to get ahead of the resort's opening timeline. Properties closer to the new village and lift infrastructure are seeing a pricing premium relative to comparable properties without direct East Village access.

Buyers curious about East Village opportunities should work with a local agent who has current knowledge of what is available, what is under construction, and what the realistic access and amenity timelines look like.

## What to Watch This Spring

As the ski season wraps up and the spring market accelerates, keep an eye on:

- **New listings in April and May.** Spring traditionally brings the largest wave of new inventory to the Park City market. Sellers who have been waiting for peak buyer activity will list in this window.
- **Interest rate sensitivity.** The broader mortgage market continues to influence buyer behavior. Buyers financing at the luxury level (over $2M) are generally less rate-sensitive, but the mid-market remains responsive to rate changes.
- **The Deer Valley East Village timeline.** As construction milestones are reached and a clearer opening timeline emerges, expect additional buyer demand to materialize in eastern Park City communities.
- **Short-term rental policy changes.** Summit County and Park City continue to evaluate short-term rental regulations. Buyers purchasing with rental income intentions should verify current and pending regulations before closing.

## Is It a Good Time to Buy or Sell in Park City?

The honest answer depends on your situation. For sellers in prime communities (Deer Valley, Old Town, Promontory, Park Meadows), the market continues to support strong outcomes for well-prepared sellers. Inventory constraints remain your ally.

For buyers, the opportunity cost of waiting has been real in Park City — prices have not corrected meaningfully, and the East Village expansion is only adding to the long-term demand story. Buyers who are financially ready and working with a knowledgeable local agent are finding the right properties and closing successfully.

The most important thing in either case: work with someone who knows this market from the inside. Park City is not a generic real estate market, and the strategies that work here are specific to its unique dynamics, buyer pool, and community-by-community nuances.

# Best Neighborhoods in Park City, Utah: A Local Agent's Guide

*By Tisha Digman, Summit Sotheby's International Realty*

"What's the best neighborhood in Park City?" is one of the first questions I get from buyers — and it is also one of the most interesting to answer, because it depends entirely on what you are looking for. Park City is a small city in geographic terms, but it contains multitudes: ski-in ski-out estates, historic Victorian cottages, golf course communities, family-friendly suburban neighborhoods, and everything in between.

Here is my honest, locally grounded guide to the neighborhoods I work in every day — and who each one is best suited for.

## Old Town: For the Walker, the Skier, the Culture Lover

If you want to walk to dinner, ski home from the resort at lunch, and live in a home with genuine historical character, Old Town is your neighborhood. The Victorian-era miners' cottages along the Historic Main Street are among the most sought-after properties in the Western US — irreplaceable architecture in an irreplaceable location.

Old Town is best for buyers who want walkable, urban mountain living. It is the most socially active neighborhood in Park City — Sundance Film Festival, summer arts events, and year-round Main Street activity keep it alive in every season.

**Trade-off:** Limited parking, older home stock (with corresponding renovation needs), and Park City's densest neighborhood feel. Not for buyers seeking large lots, privacy, or quiet.

**Price range:** $400,000 (studio condos) to $8M+ (renovated historic estates). [Explore Old Town listings →](/old-town/)

## Deer Valley: For the Skier Who Wants It All

Deer Valley is the address for buyers who place ski access and resort luxury at the top of the priority list. Whether you are drawn to the ski-in ski-out estates of Empire Pass and Deer Crest, the resort-base condominiums of Lower Deer Valley, or the emerging opportunities in the new Deer Valley East Village, the Deer Valley area is Park City's most prestigious resort community.

**Who it's best for:** Buyers whose primary motivation is ski resort proximity and luxury resort amenities. Second-home buyers who want a property that works as a rental when they are not in town.

**Trade-off:** Premium pricing for premium access. True ski-in ski-out properties command some of the highest prices in the state of Utah.

**Price range:** $750,000 (condos at the base) to $30M+ (estate ski-in ski-out homes). [Explore Deer Valley listings →](/lower-deer-valley-resort/)

## Park Meadows: For Families Putting Down Roots

Park Meadows is where Park City families live. Established tree-lined streets, the Park City Golf Club running through the neighborhood, direct trail access to Round Valley, and proximity to Park City's highly regarded schools make Park Meadows the neighborhood I recommend most often to buyers with children who are making Park City their primary residence.

The neighborhood is central — 10–15 minutes from all three ski resorts, 10 minutes from Main Street, and equally convenient to Kimball Junction's retail and the hospital. It is the most practical, livable neighborhood in Park City for year-round residents.

**Who it's best for:** Full-time families, buyers prioritizing schools, buyers who want mountain living without a resort-community premium.

**Trade-off:** Less "resort feel" than Deer Valley or Empire Pass. If pure ski-in ski-out access or the prestige of a private gated club is important to you, look elsewhere.

**Price range:** $750,000 (townhomes) to $8M+ (custom luxury homes). [Explore Park Meadows listings →](/park-meadows/)

## Promontory: For the Golf and Club Lifestyle

Promontory is Park City's answer to the question: "What if a world-class private golf club and a ski resort community had a child?" Two Jack Nicklaus courses, ski access to Park City Mountain Resort, equestrian trails, and a full-service private club on 6,000+ acres make Promontory unlike any other community in Utah.

**Who it's best for:** Buyers who prioritize golf and club amenities over ski-in ski-out access, buyers who want estate-level privacy and space, and buyers who are willing to participate in a club membership structure as part of ownership.

**Trade-off:** Membership fees are a real ownership cost that must be factored into your budget. The community is 15–20 minutes from Main Street — less walkable than Old Town or Deer Valley.

**Price range:** $900,000 (attached homes) to $15M+ (custom estates). [Explore Promontory listings →](/promontory/)

## Kimball Junction: For Accessibility and Value

Kimball Junction is Park City's most accessible neighborhood — it sits at the highway junction where I-80 meets US-224, giving it the fastest freeway access to Salt Lake City (30–35 minutes) and easy reach of both Park City Mountain Resort and Canyons Village. It is also home to the region's best retail: Trader Joe's, Walmart, the hospital, and dozens of restaurants.

Kimball Junction offers some of Park City's more attainable entry price points in a market that can feel inaccessible at first glance.

**Who it's best for:** Commuters working in Salt Lake City, first-time Park City buyers, buyers who prioritize value and convenience over resort proximity.

**Price range:** $500,000 – $2M. [Explore Kimball Junction listings →](/kimball-junction/)

## Jeremy Ranch: For the Family That Wants More Space

Jeremy Ranch is the I-80 corridor neighborhood that offers the most space per dollar in the immediate Park City area. Larger lots, a community golf club, excellent schools, and easy freeway access to Salt Lake City make Jeremy Ranch a favorite with families who want mountain living without the full mountain premium.

**Who it's best for:** Families, buyers with Salt Lake City work commutes, buyers who want space and privacy within the Park City School District.

**Price range:** $700,000 – $4M+. [Explore Jeremy Ranch listings →](/jeremy-ranch/)

## The Bottom Line: There Is No "Best" Neighborhood

Every neighborhood in Park City has a distinct character, and the right one depends on your priorities — skiing, schools, privacy, walkability, golf, rental income, budget, or some combination of the above. My job is to help you identify which neighborhood's trade-offs align with what matters most to you.

If you have a question about a specific area I have not covered here, [reach out](/contact/) — I am happy to share my honest perspective.

# Is Park City Real Estate a Good Investment in 2026?

*By Tisha Digman, Summit Sotheby's International Realty*

This is one of the most common questions I hear from buyers — and it is a fair one. Park City real estate is not cheap. Entry-level condos start around $400,000–$500,000, the median single-family home price is well above $1.5 million, and luxury properties in Deer Valley and Empire Pass routinely trade at $5M–$20M+. So the question deserves a genuine answer, not a sales pitch.

Here is my honest assessment.

## What Makes Park City Real Estate a Strong Long-Term Bet

### 1. Finite Land Supply in a Constrained Geography

Park City sits in a narrow mountain valley surrounded by National Forest and state land. There is a hard ceiling on how much new development can happen within the core Park City area. This isn't a market where you can build new subdivisions on the periphery indefinitely — the mountains are literally in the way.

Constrained supply combined with sustained demand is the most durable driver of long-term real estate appreciation. It is the reason coastal California, Manhattan, and established ski resort towns consistently outperform the broader housing market over multi-decade periods.

### 2. Consistent Demand from a Global Buyer Pool

Park City draws buyers from across the United States and internationally. The combination of Salt Lake City International Airport's direct connectivity to major US hubs and growing international routes means that Park City is accessible to wealthy buyers from both coasts and from abroad. Demand for Park City real estate is not driven by the local Utah economy alone — it draws on a much larger and more affluent pool.

### 3. Short-Term Rental Income Potential

Many Park City properties — particularly condominiums in Old Town, Lower Deer Valley, and Kimball Junction — generate meaningful short-term rental income. Ski season is the peak period, with nightly rates for well-positioned properties reaching significant premiums during holidays, Sundance Film Festival, and peak powder weekends.

It is important to understand the current regulatory environment. Park City and Summit County have STR licensing requirements, occupancy taxes, and in some cases caps on the number of active licenses in a given area. Buyers purchasing with rental income in mind should verify the current rules for the specific property and zone they are considering. Tisha and Cam can connect you with the right local resources to evaluate rental potential before you close.

### 4. The Deer Valley East Village Expansion

The ongoing expansion of Deer Valley Resort into the East Village is a generational catalyst for Park City real estate values. This is not a minor lift addition — it is the creation of an entirely new resort village connected to Deer Valley's existing terrain, extending the skiable acreage dramatically and adding significant resort infrastructure.

Real estate values in the eastern Park City corridor — Jordanelle, Hideout Canyon, and the areas immediately adjacent to the East Village — have already responded to this development. Buyers who get in early in the development of a major resort expansion have historically been well rewarded.

## The Honest Risks

### Concentration Risk

A single-market real estate portfolio is always a concentration risk. If you are putting a significant portion of your net worth into a Park City property, make sure that fits your broader financial picture.

### Carrying Costs Can Be Significant

HOA fees in gated communities and resort-adjacent developments can be substantial — sometimes $1,000–$3,000 per month in communities like Promontory, Empire Pass, or Deer Crest. Property taxes in Utah are relatively low by national standards, but overall carrying costs for luxury properties deserve careful modeling.

### STR Regulatory Uncertainty

Short-term rental regulations in Park City and Summit County have evolved over recent years and may continue to change. Buyers counting on vacation rental income as a core component of their investment thesis should build in regulatory risk — the income potential is real, but it is not guaranteed to remain at current levels indefinitely.

### Not a Cash Flow Business at Entry-Level Luxury Prices

If you buy a $2M condo in Deer Valley with 20% down and finance the rest, the rental income during ski season alone is unlikely to service the mortgage. Park City real estate investment is best thought of as a long-term appreciation play with rental income as a meaningful offset to carrying costs — not as a yield-generating investment in the traditional sense.

## The Verdict

For buyers who can hold long-term, are purchasing at realistic prices relative to comps, and understand the carrying costs and regulatory environment: yes, Park City real estate has historically been and continues to be a strong long-term investment.

The combination of supply constraints, global demand, a world-class resort ecosystem, and the Deer Valley East Village growth story makes Park City one of the more compelling markets in the Western US for a luxury second-home or investment property. But like any real estate investment, the outcome is heavily influenced by the specific property you buy, the price you pay, and how well you understand the market you are entering.

That is exactly why working with a knowledgeable local agent matters.

# Your Guide to Ski-In Ski-Out Homes in Park City and Deer Valley

*By Tisha Digman, Summit Sotheby's International Realty*

The phrase "ski-in ski-out" gets used loosely in real estate marketing — and in a market like Park City, where nearly everything is marketed with some connection to the ski resorts, the distinction between true ski-in ski-out access and "near the slopes" access matters enormously. Buyers who understand what they are actually buying — and what they are paying a premium for — make better decisions.

Here is what I tell every buyer who comes to me looking for a ski-in ski-out property in Park City or Deer Valley.

## What "True" Ski-In Ski-Out Actually Means

True ski-in ski-out means you can ski from your home's door to a ski run and ski back to your home at the end of the day without removing your skis or using a shuttle. Period. In the Park City market, this is achievable — but only in specific communities with specific properties. Not every property marketed as "ski access" meets this standard.

When evaluating ski access, ask:
- Can you literally ski from the front door (or an exterior door) to a groomed trail?
- Can you ski home from the resort — or do you need to walk to a shuttle stop?
- Is the ski access dependent on snow conditions (i.e., low-snow years might leave you walking)?
- Is the access private to your building/home, or shared with a larger resort area?

Tisha and Cam personally verify ski access details for every property we show to ski access-motivated buyers. We have walked the routes. We know which properties are genuinely ski-in ski-out and which are ski-adjacent.

## Communities with True Ski-In Ski-Out Access

### Deer Valley Access: Empire Pass and Deer Crest

**Empire Pass** is the jewel of Deer Valley ski-in ski-out real estate. Located at the top of Empire Canyon at approximately 9,000 feet elevation, Empire Pass offers direct access to Deer Valley's upper mountain terrain. The Talisker Club and the Montage Deer Valley hotel anchor the community, and private residences range from sophisticated condominiums to multi-million dollar estate homes.

Empire Pass is the most exclusive ski-in ski-out address in the Park City area — and priced accordingly. Entry-level condominiums begin around $1.5M–$2M, with estate homes reaching well above $15M.

**Deer Crest** occupies a stunning ridgeline position above Deer Valley Resort with fewer than 100 total homesites. Homes here offer ski-in ski-out access to Deer Valley's mid-mountain terrain and dramatic views of Jordanelle Reservoir below. Deer Crest is among the most private and exclusive communities in the entire Park City market.

### Park City Mountain Resort Access: Canyons Village and The Colony

**Canyons Village** is the base area of Park City Mountain Resort's Canyons side — the resort's western entry point. Condominiums and townhomes in the Canyons Village base area offer true ski-in ski-out access to the resort's terrain, along with base area amenities including ski valet, dining, and retail.

**The Colony at White Pine Canyon** is one of the most dramatic ski-in ski-out communities in North America. Located in the White Pine Canyon drainage adjacent to Park City Mountain Resort's terrain, The Colony consists of several hundred acres of private land with custom-built estate homes on large lots. Residents ski directly to the resort through the community's gated terrain. The Colony represents ski-in ski-out living at a true estate scale — with homes ranging from approximately $3M to over $25M.

## What Ski-In Ski-Out Access Costs

The premium for true ski-in ski-out access in Park City and Deer Valley is typically 15–35% above comparable properties without direct ski access, depending on the specific community and quality of access. Here is a general frame by community:

| Community | Ski Resort | Price Range |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Empire Pass | Deer Valley | $1.5M – $20M+ |
| Deer Crest | Deer Valley | $3M – $15M+ |
| Canyons Village | Park City Mountain | $600,000 – $5M |
| The Colony | Park City Mountain | $3M – $25M+ |
| Upper Deer Valley | Deer Valley | $2M – $10M+ |

## HOA Fees and Club Memberships: What to Budget

Ski-in ski-out communities in Park City typically carry higher HOA fees than non-resort-access communities. In Empire Pass, Deer Crest, and The Colony, monthly HOA fees ranging from $800 to $3,000+ are common, depending on the property type and community amenity package. Some communities also require membership in a private club (such as the Talisker Club at Empire Pass), which carries its own initiation and annual fee structure.

Total annual carrying costs — HOA fees, property taxes, insurance, utilities, and any club memberships — for a ski-in ski-out property in the $3M–$5M range can easily exceed $50,000–$80,000 per year. Understanding these costs fully before closing is essential, and your agent should be able to help you model them accurately for any specific property.

## Is a Ski-In Ski-Out Property Right for You?

Not every Park City buyer needs true ski-in ski-out access to have a wonderful ski property. Many buyers are better served by a home in Park Meadows, Old Town, or Kimball Junction with convenient resort proximity but without the premium access price and associated carrying costs — and they are genuinely happy with that trade-off.

The buyers who are best served by a ski-in ski-out property are those for whom the ability to step into their bindings from the front door, ski all day, and ski home is a core, non-negotiable part of the experience they are investing in. If you can honestly say that, then the premium is worth it. If you are buying it because it sounds good or because it seems like a stronger resale investment, think it through carefully — there are strong properties throughout Park City that appreciate well without the ski access premium.

I am happy to walk through the specifics with any buyer thinking about this question. The honest conversation before you buy is the most valuable thing a local agent can offer.
