# Auburn Bay Homes for Sale -- Calgary's Premier Lakeside Community

## Overview

Auburn Bay is one of SE Calgary's most desirable communities, built around a stunning 43-acre freshwater lake that residents can enjoy year-round. Developed by Brookfield Residential starting in 2005, this master-planned neighbourhood has matured into a vibrant lakeside community where families, young professionals, and retirees find an exceptional quality of life. With approximately 13,000 residents and a mix of housing options at various price points, Auburn Bay homes for sale attract buyers looking for that rare combination of lake living and urban convenience in Calgary.

What sets Auburn Bay apart from other SE Calgary communities is the lifestyle built into its DNA. The Auburn Bay Residents' Association maintains the lake, beach club, and extensive pathway system, creating a resort-like atmosphere within city limits. Whether you are searching for your first home or upgrading to a lakefront property, Auburn Bay real estate offers genuine value in Calgary's competitive housing market.

Auburn Bay continues to attract strong buyer interest heading into spring 2026. With Calgary's average home price sitting at $641,000 and the median at $572,500, Auburn Bay homes for sale represent solid value for the amenities and lifestyle included. Detached homes, townhouses, and condos all trade actively in this community, making it accessible to a wide range of budgets.

## Location and Access

Auburn Bay sits in Calgary's deep southeast, bounded by Seton Boulevard to the north, 52 Street SE to the east, and Deerfoot Trail to the west. This location puts residents in an excellent position for commuting and daily errands.

**Key distances and routes:**
- **Downtown Calgary:** 25-30 minutes via Deerfoot Trail (approximately 22 km)
- **Deerfoot Trail access:** Direct via Auburn Bay Boulevard, providing a fast north-south corridor to downtown, the airport, and Cross Iron Mills
- **Stoney Trail:** Minutes east via 52 Street SE, connecting to the ring road for quick access to the south and west
- **South Health Campus:** Less than 5 minutes (2 km), one of Calgary's newest and most advanced hospitals
- **Seton Urban District:** Adjacent to the north, providing a walkable commercial hub with over 1 million sq ft of retail and services
- **Calgary Transit:** Bus routes connect Auburn Bay to the Somerset-Bridlewood CTrain station; the future Green Line extension will significantly improve transit access for SE Calgary communities

The proximity to Deerfoot Trail is a significant advantage for Auburn Bay homeowners, cutting commute times compared to communities further east or south. For families with members working in the Foothills Industrial Park or heading to the Calgary International Airport, the location is highly practical.

## Lifestyle and Amenities

The centrepiece of Auburn Bay is its 43-acre lake, the largest in any Calgary community. The Auburn Bay Residents' Association operates the Auburn House, a community centre on the lake's edge that includes:

- **Sandy beach** with swimming area and lifeguard stations in summer
- **Fishing** (stocked lake, catch-and-release)
- **Non-motorized boating** (kayaks, canoes, paddleboards, and small sailboats)
- **Winter skating** on the frozen lake surface
- **BBQ areas and fire pits** for community gatherings
- **Playground** and splash park for young children

Beyond the lake, Auburn Bay features an extensive pathway network connecting to Fish Creek Provincial Park to the west and the broader Calgary pathway system. Cyclists and runners can access over 15 km of paved paths without leaving the neighbourhood's immediate area.

Parks and green spaces are woven throughout Auburn Bay, with several smaller playgrounds and open fields complementing the central lake area. The community hosts seasonal events including Canada Day celebrations, outdoor movie nights, and winter festivals that build genuine neighbourhood connections.

For families with younger children, multiple daycares and early learning centres operate in Auburn Bay and adjacent Seton, including national chains and independent Montessori-style programs.

Auburn Bay offers a combination of lifestyle, value, and location that few SE Calgary communities can match:

1. **Lake living within city limits.** The 43-acre lake is a genuine differentiator -- swimming, fishing, boating, and skating steps from your door, without the commute to Chestermere or Sylvan Lake.
2. **Strong price appreciation.** Lake communities in Calgary consistently hold and grow their value, supported by the limited supply of lakefront lots and the ongoing build-out of Seton's amenities.
3. **Adjacent to South Health Campus and Seton.** Hospital access, shopping, dining, and recreation are minutes away, reducing the need to drive across the city.
4. **Excellent schools.** Multiple K-9 schools within walking distance make this one of the strongest family communities in SE Calgary.
5. **Connected commuting.** Direct Deerfoot Trail access and proximity to Stoney Trail mean Auburn Bay is not as isolated as its deep-SE location might suggest.
6. **Active community culture.** The Residents' Association runs year-round programming, and the lake creates a natural gathering point that builds real neighbourhood bonds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can anyone use the Auburn Bay lake?
No. The Auburn Bay lake is a private amenity exclusively for residents and their guests. Access is managed by the Auburn Bay Residents' Association, and homeowners pay an annual fee that covers lake maintenance, beach operations, and community programming. This exclusivity helps protect property values and ensures the lake is never overcrowded.

### What are property taxes like in Auburn Bay?
Property taxes in Auburn Bay vary by assessed value. For a typical detached home assessed at $550,000-$650,000, expect annual property taxes of approximately $3,800-$4,600 based on Calgary's 2026 mill rate. This includes municipal and provincial education taxes. Calgary's property tax rates remain competitive compared to other major Canadian cities.

### How far is Auburn Bay from downtown Calgary?
Auburn Bay is approximately 22 km south of downtown Calgary. Via Deerfoot Trail, the drive typically takes 25-30 minutes outside of rush hour. During peak commute times, expect 35-45 minutes. The future Green Line CTrain extension is expected to improve transit options for SE Calgary residents significantly.

### Is Auburn Bay still growing or is it built out?
Auburn Bay is nearing completion, with approximately 90% of residential lots developed. Some new construction remains available, particularly townhouses and the final phases of detached homes. The neighbouring Seton commercial district continues to add retail, dining, and medical services, which benefits Auburn Bay residents. The community's near-built-out status means mature landscaping, established schools, and a stable neighbourhood feel.

# Cranston Homes for Sale -- SE Calgary's Established Riverfront Community

## Overview

Cranston is one of SE Calgary's most established and sought-after residential communities, stretching along the western bank of the Bow River in the city's deep southeast. Developed primarily between 2002 and 2018 by Brookfield Residential, Cranston has grown into a mature neighbourhood of approximately 16,000 residents who enjoy a combination of river access, ridge-top views, and close-knit community life that newer developments are still working to build.

Cranston homes for sale appeal to a wide range of buyers, from first-time purchasers looking at condos in Cranston Ridge to established families seeking detached homes backing onto the Bow River pathway. The community's mature landscaping, completed schools, and proximity to Fish Creek Provincial Park make it a particularly strong choice for families who want an established neighbourhood rather than a construction zone.

In Calgary's spring 2026 market, Cranston real estate benefits from its proven track record. The neighbourhood has demonstrated steady appreciation over two decades, and its combination of location, amenities, and community culture continues to draw buyers willing to pay a slight premium over newer, less established SE Calgary communities.

## Location and Access

Cranston occupies a prominent position in SE Calgary, perched on the bluffs above the Bow River and bounded by Deerfoot Trail to the west, the Bow River to the east, and Stoney Trail to the south.

**Key distances and routes:**
- **Downtown Calgary:** 20-25 minutes via Deerfoot Trail (approximately 19 km)
- **Deerfoot Trail access:** Direct access via Cranston Boulevard and Seton Boulevard, one of the fastest north-south routes in the city
- **Stoney Trail:** Immediate access at Cranston's southern boundary, connecting to the ring road for east-west travel
- **Fish Creek Provincial Park:** Adjacent to the northwest, with direct pathway connections from the community
- **South Health Campus:** 5 minutes (3 km), providing full hospital services
- **Seton Urban District:** 5-7 minutes, with major retail, dining, and the Seton YMCA
- **Calgary International Airport:** 25-30 minutes via Deerfoot Trail

Cranston's Deerfoot Trail access is one of its strongest selling points. Unlike communities further east that rely on secondary roads, Cranston residents can be on Deerfoot in minutes, making the daily commute to downtown or the north end of the city noticeably faster.

6. **Diverse housing stock.** From affordable condos to premium riverfront homes, Cranston offers options across the budget spectrum.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does Cranston compare to Auburn Bay?
Both are excellent SE Calgary communities, but they serve slightly different needs. Auburn Bay centres around its 43-acre private lake, making it ideal for buyers who prioritize water recreation. Cranston offers Bow River access and Fish Creek Park connectivity, with a more established, mature feel. Cranston homes tend to price slightly higher for equivalent detached homes due to the neighbourhood's maturity and river/ridge views. Both communities share access to Seton's amenities and the South Health Campus.

### What is the Cranston Residents' Association fee?
The Cranston Residents' Association charges an annual membership fee that covers access to Century Hall, community events, and maintenance of shared amenities. As of 2026, fees are typically in the range of $200-$300 per household annually, though exact amounts vary. This is separate from municipal property taxes and is significantly lower than the Auburn Bay Residents' Association fee, which covers lake operations.

### Is Cranston a good community for retirees?
Yes. Cranston's mature landscape, flat pathway system, proximity to South Health Campus (5 minutes), and variety of housing types including low-maintenance condos and townhouses make it appealing for downsizers and retirees. The community's walkability to daily services in Cranston Market and the active Residents' Association programming provide social opportunities beyond what many retirement-focused communities offer.

### Are there still new homes available in Cranston?
Cranston is largely built out, with only limited new construction remaining in Cranston Ridge (the newest sub-phase). Most Cranston homes for sale are resale properties, which means buyers benefit from established landscaping, fenced yards, and finished basements. The limited new supply helps support resale values in the community.

Mahogany homes for sale span the full housing spectrum, from affordable condos in the Westman Village mixed-use development to premium lakefront estate homes. The community's sheer scale means there is always active inventory, giving buyers more choice than in smaller, fully built-out SE Calgary neighbourhoods. Combined with the private Mahogany Beach Club, year-round lake access, and the Mahogany Village Market commercial centre, this community delivers a self-contained lifestyle that reduces dependence on the rest of the city.

Calgary's spring 2026 market shows continued interest in Mahogany real estate, driven by the community's amenity package and the ongoing expansion of nearby Seton. ## Overview

McKenzie Towne holds a special place in Calgary's residential landscape as the city's first new-urbanism community, designed from the ground up to prioritize walkability, neighbourhood character, and old-fashioned town centre charm. Launched by Carma Developers (now Brookfield Residential) in 1995, McKenzie Towne was revolutionary for Calgary at the time, borrowing design principles from traditional small towns: front porches facing the street, rear-lane garages, a central High Street with shops and services, and public squares that encourage neighbours to actually meet each other.

Thirty years later, McKenzie Towne homes for sale continue to attract buyers who value community character and established infrastructure over the newest construction. With approximately 18,000 residents spread across five distinct villages -- Inverness, Prestwick, Elgin, Copperfield, and McKenzie Lake -- the community offers one of SE Calgary's most mature and complete neighbourhood experiences.

McKenzie Towne real estate is competitively priced relative to Calgary's spring 2026 benchmarks. With the city's average home price at $641,000 and the median at $572,500, McKenzie Towne's established detached homes often trade below or near the median, representing strong value for the level of community infrastructure and amenity access included. For buyers comparing SE Calgary homes, McKenzie Towne delivers the most "bang for the buck" of any established community in the quadrant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is McKenzie Towne the same as McKenzie Lake?
McKenzie Lake is one of the five villages within the broader McKenzie Towne community. McKenzie Lake features a private lake amenity (smaller than Auburn Bay or Mahogany's lakes) accessible to McKenzie Lake village residents. The other villages -- Inverness, Prestwick, Elgin, and Copperfield -- do not have lake access but benefit from McKenzie Towne's overall amenity package including High Street, pathways, and parks.

### Are the older homes in McKenzie Towne a concern?
The oldest homes in McKenzie Towne (Inverness and Prestwick, built 1995-2005) are now 20-30 years old. Like any established neighbourhood, buyers should budget for potential updates to roofing, HVAC, and cosmetic finishes. However, the original build quality in these villages was generally strong, and many homeowners have invested in renovations. The character and walkability of the original villages often justify the renovation investment, as these areas command strong resale demand precisely because of their unique design.

### How walkable is McKenzie Towne compared to other SE Calgary communities?
McKenzie Towne is the most walkable community in SE Calgary, bar none. The original new-urbanism villages (Inverness and Prestwick) were designed specifically for pedestrian use, with connected sidewalks, narrow streets that slow traffic, front porches that encourage street-level interaction, and the High Street commercial centre within walking distance of most homes. Walk Score ratings for McKenzie Towne's core villages typically range from 50-65, significantly above Calgary's suburban average of 30-40.

### What is the parking situation in the original McKenzie Towne villages?
The original villages (Inverness, Prestwick) feature rear-lane garage access, which means narrower street fronts and no front-drive garages. On-street parking is available but can be tight, particularly during community events. For buyers who own multiple vehicles, it is worth confirming garage size and lane access during property viewings. The newer villages (Elgin, Copperfield) feature conventional front-drive garages with wider streets.
